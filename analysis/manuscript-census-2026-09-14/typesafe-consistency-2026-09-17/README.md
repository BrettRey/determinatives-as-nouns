The 55 pairs at or above 0.5 or labelled contradiction were read in full by the screening session ([pairs.json](pairs.json) has all 4,241).

**Result: no contradiction.** Two sentences account for most flags, because each is a scoped or negated statement that the model paired against every positive claim about the same lexeme:

- `du-017` (§det-uniform): "In Det or internal Mod function before a target, ordinary *some* and *few* exclude external determination, partitive complements, and relative postmodifiers within their own phrase." Flagged against *the very few who objected*, *few who come ever leave*, *the few people*, *the few mistakes*. All compatible: those are independent uses, or the external determiner belongs to the larger NP, which is what "within their own phrase" says.
- `sat-013` (§saturation): "The dependent use of *some* doesn't by itself require a Det function inside every independent occurrence." Flagged against a dozen licensed *some* claims. Compatible: it denies an inference, not a use.

The remaining flags are pairs of competing analyses the paper compares (CGEL's fusion beside ordinary Head), semantic beside syntactic statements (*mine* "without an understood nominal head" beside "ordinary Head in both uses"), and table cells beside prose. The top-scored pair (0.84), *a* "belongs to the complex determinative" beside "*a* isn't an ordinary Det selecting a plural *few* target", is the same claim from two sides.

**What it licenses.** The paper doesn't contradict itself at the level of its participation claims. The two hub sentences are the ones a referee reading fast will take as categorical, so a clarifying clause in each ("within the determinative's own phrase, not the containing NP"; a positive rewording of the double negative) is worth considering; that is a readability point, not a defect. The screen's contradiction label is not reliable on scoped statements, which is the same limit the construction labelling showed at the taxonomic boundary.
