**The rival columns and their sources.**

- *CGEL*: Huddleston & Pullum (2002), pages as given. Determinative is a primary category beside noun.
- Hudson: Hudson (2000), "Grammar without functional categories", §§2, 3, 5, and Hudson (2004), "Are determiners heads?", §§1, 3.2. Pronouns are nouns, and his "determiners" are the pronouns that take a common-noun complement ("there is in fact no word class Determiner", 2000 §5). He sets aside *all*, the numerals, and the words restricted to plural or non-count nouns (*a few*, *a little*, *many*, *much*, *enough*; 2004 §1), so for those words his column says he makes no prediction.
- Split A/N, an English hypothesis: *few*, *many*, *much*, and *little*, which inflect for grade, are adjectives, and the rest of the inventory is nominal. It's the split that Van Eynde's (2003) criterion (inflection and agreement shared with prenominal adjectives) would give for English. His evidence is Italian and Dutch, and data about lexemes and lexical categories in one language tell us little about another (Brett, 24 Oct), so the column states the hypothesis's predictions for English and the rows test them on English data alone. His architecture, in which determiner status is a marking value separate from lexical category (§2), is compatible with this paper and cited for that.
- Coordinate: this paper, with determinative a subcategory of Noun beside common noun, proper noun, and pronoun.

"(inf.)" marks a prediction inferred from a source, not stated in it. The split column is a hypothesis, so its cells are predictions by construction and carry no mark.

**The "discriminates?" column.** *Excludes attested: R* means rival R's own analysis predicts that something attested is out (or the reverse). *Against R* means R can state the datum only by splitting what the others state once. *Favours R* means the datum supports R over this paper. *Predicted here only* means this paper predicts it and the rivals are silent. *Economy: R* means every analysis can state it and R needs more statements, with nothing else following. *None* means all four agree. *Unsettled* means the answer waits on analysis not yet done.

## What the seed shows (24 Oct)

Against the separate category, most rows are economy: *CGEL* states the same facts with more statements. One kind of economy isn't a statement count: with determinative separate, every determinative standing alone puts a change of category on the head path, on either head analysis, and Noun membership removes it (category-change). The rows where *CGEL*'s own analysis predicts that something attested is out are its conversions in the secondary use (reflexive-person, else-survives), and the schema predicts a gap that no rival predicts (no-description-no-use). So the open problem of 16 Oct, an observation the separate category fails to predict, currently rests on the secondary use. Two cautions follow. Reflexive-person concerns pronouns, which *CGEL* already puts in Noun, so it bears on determinatives only through the schema. Else-survives assumes ordinary Head, since under fusion *a certain someone else* has no route but conversion. The pre-head adjective candidate has been unsettled since 18 Oct.

Against the split (the gradable four as adjectives), the grade and degree row favours it; the partitive and restricted-dependent rows cut against it, and its criterion can't place the demonstratives. The bare-use row cuts against it only if bare *few* turns out far commoner than bare *numerous*, so bare-quant-adjectives is the gap worth a search: without rates, that argument rests on impressions of frequency.

Between nesting and coordination, no row discriminates by counting statements. The properties shared with quantity nouns need two statements on either structure, and the properties shared with pronouns favour nesting. Hudson's own set largely avoids the conflict in determiner-on-determinative, so that row counts against nesting only if the nesting extends to *CGEL*'s whole inventory. The desk count (24 Oct) found the pronoun-like properties confined to four small subgroups of determinatives and some characters in conflict with any one tree. That makes multiple inheritance a third structure to weigh, but it doesn't decide between the three, since no noun is coded. Coding a reference set of nouns and adjectives (noun-reference-set) is the step that would.
