## 1. What is coded

A cell records whether a **word form** has a **property** in a **frame**.

- **Rows are forms**, as in the 2021 matrix: *this* and *these*, *he* and *him*, and now *dog* and *dogs*, are separate rows. A lexeme row would make nouns look number-flexible beside number-fixed demonstratives, an artefact of how rows were drawn.
- **One lexeme commitment** is made in advance: a determinative standing alone keeps its category and lexeme (*CGEL* p. 420, "argues against any part-of-speech distinction between the two uses", a commitment the fusion paper also holds). So *few* in *Few came* and in *few people* is one form. The analysis may test this; the coding doesn't build in the opposite.
- **Morphology is coded at three levels** (Brett, 24 Oct: "I meant the bases, but both apply"): the **word** (the row's own form: *mine* is an independent genitive, *dogs* is a plural in *-s*); its **lexeme** (the paradigm: *me* belongs to a lexeme with *my* and *mine*); and the **bases** of a compound (*something* has the bases *some* and *thing*; *CGEL* p. 423 analyses the compound determinatives as "a determinative base with a nominal one", *body*, *one*, *thing*, *where*). Lexeme and base facts are the same for every form that shares the lexeme or base, so they take no m, and the model pools them by lexeme and by base rather than counting one fact once per form. The base columns record morphological relatedness, not distribution, so the tests run with and without them. Facts are drafted by rule in `analysis/codebook/morphology-facts.csv`; *takes plural -(e)s* is a possibility claim and goes to the coders.
- **Columns sit under super-columns.** A super-column is a property (determiner, gender, number, pre-head adjectives); under it sit the alternatives: the possibility of the property, the possibility of its absence where that is informative (section 4), and the frames where the answer can differ (section 5). The super-column is also the family the analysis pools over (section 9).

## 2. Values and statuses

| | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **y** | The form can do this. One admissible instance settles it. |
| **m** | It can, but only in a way that sounds degraded to speakers generally (*CGEL*'s ?). |
| **n** | It never does. A defeasible generalization, held to the evidence standard in section 3. |
| **x** | Not applicable: the property can't arise for the form (gender for a form that never refers; an object position for a form that can't head an object). A status, not a value. |
| unjudged, contested | Statuses of the cell's review, never values. |

- **Variety and register are a field, not a value.** A form available in some varieties or registers (bare *select few* in Indian English; headlines) is y for those, with the variety recorded. The analysis runs such cells as y and as n.
- **Construction licensing is a frame, not a value.** If a form can do something only in a special construction or reading, that construction or reading is a frame (section 5), and the form is y there.
- So m is reserved for degradation alone. This answers both reviewers' objection that the earlier m mixed three dimensions.

## 3. Evidence

- **A y needs one admissible instance**: a natural example in the frame, with error, coercion, quotation, pun, metalinguistic use, reanalysis, and use as a different lexeme ruled out (*a lot* "a plot", *number* the verb).
- **The set-denoting secondary use is not a different lexeme for coding.** *The Smiths*, *an Ophelia*, *a he or a she*, *ten mes*, *a special someone*, *somethings*, *a nobody*, *in threes*: *CGEL* treats these as conversion (p. 423 n. 43, p. 429 n. 46) or a change of category (p. 385), and the evidence ledger's row `secondary-use-schema` treats them as one secondary use with no change of category. Discarding them as different words would build *CGEL*'s analysis into the data, so coders grade them in the set-denoting frame (section 5), and whether they count as the same lexeme is a question for the analysis, run both ways. (A side agent's note, 24 Oct: one y-to-m coder rejected *He's a nobody* as "the common noun, a different word".)
- **An n is a generalization** and records what supports it: a *CGEL* statement or starred example (with page); Brett's judgment (dated); a bounded corpus search where the form had ample opportunity (corpus, query, count); and the adversarial search, in which coders from at least two model families each give the strongest y they considered and why it fails. "Two families found no y" is weak support, recorded as such.
- **Attestation isn't acceptability.** *Every of* is attested in unedited web text and speech; Brett judged all 67 lines unacceptable, so the cell is n with the attestation noted.
- **Kinds of evidence** (from the combined manuscript, §2): a *CGEL* description records an established possibility; a starred form records a judgment; an attestation records an occurrence in a register and context; a bounded search with no result records absence where there was opportunity.

## 4. Polarity

- **Every column states a possibility**, ◇P. Then n means ¬◇P (never) everywhere, and the burden runs the same way in every column.
- **Universal columns are reversed**: "must be stressed as object after a particle" becomes "may follow a particle unstressed"; "must be outranked by a coindexed element" becomes "may occur without a binder". The 2021 values convert mechanically.
- **Definable columns are derived, not coded**: "does not inflect" follows from the inflection columns. (Coded separately, it contradicted them for 17 forms.)
- **The absence column ◇¬P is added only where P can be necessary.** For an optional dependent ◇¬P is y for every form and carries nothing. The determiner is the main case: *dog* must take one in an argument NP, *she* and *Kim* take none in their ordinary uses, *few* takes one or not.
- **Joint states.** Where both ◇P and ◇¬P are coded (or a pair such as personal/non-personal is), they give three states: must, must not, either. There is no fourth. A form for which the property doesn't arise is x (dummy *it* for gender), not "neither". This corrects the earlier four-state reading, which both reviewers rejected: under a non-empty domain "neither" is impossible, and Blanché's "determined" (□P ∨ □¬P) is only the negation of "contingent". Fixed against free (gender fixed in *she* and *something*, following the referent in *the baby*) is a hypothesis about these states, not a further column.
- **Term negation.** "May be non-personal" matches "may not be personal" only where non-personal covers the whole domain of the property; otherwise it is a contrary with gaps (Horn 1989, on Aristotle's predicate denial and term negation). The codebook gives each such contrary its extension.

## 5. Frames

- **A frame restricts the domain of a possibility claim**: which constructions or readings the "may" ranges over. (The reviewers accepted Kratzer's relative modality as an analogy only; a grammatical frame restricts structures, not worlds.)
- **Initial frames** are the September census's construction ids where they fit (`independent_argument`, `predicative_complement`, `dependent_det`, `dependent_internal_mod`, `independent_partitive`, `existential_displaced_subject`, `external_determination`, `predeterminer_mod`), plus a fixed preposition phrase (*at school*, *by bus*), the bare-role predicative (*became president*), the vocative, the set-denoting use (*a he or a she*, *the real me*), and the nominal in modifier function (*dog houses*).
- **The set-denoting use is graded, not discarded.** Packets name it as a frame, so a coder records *somethings* or *ten mes* there instead of ruling it out. The columns where it matters most are *takes plural -(e)s*, number meaning, a determiner taken, and pre-head adjectives.
- **Frames stay open during blind coding** (Brett, 24 Oct): coders who don't know the hypothesis may propose a frame or reading (a kinds reading, *thousands of fishes*). **The frame list is frozen before Brett judges any cell.** This answers the reviewers' worry that splitting frames after seeing the data makes the features data-dependent: the proposals come from coders blind to the point.
- **A frame must be justified by a contrast within one word** (*at school* against \**School closed* against *the school*), not by two different words.
- **Split a property by frame only where some form's value changes across frames**, judged on the frozen list. Elsewhere the property has one column with its frame named here.
- **Register is not a frame**; it goes in the variety field. Nor does it lower a grade (Brett, 24 Oct: "I'm disinclined to consider register"): a use that is formal, archaic, literary, informal, regional, or rare is y with a variety note, never m. Coder m's that gave only register or rarity as the reason are reclassified y by `analysis/cell-ledger/apply_register_rule.py`; those whose notes also cite degradation stay m.
- **The echo question is a construction of its own** (Brett, 24 Oct: "echo questions should be a construction here and elsewhere"). A use available only in echo questions (*You read all WHOSE letters?*) is graded in that frame, with a note beginning "echo:", not as an m in the default frame.
- **Columns are designed to cover the facts, not to recover the 2021 senses** (Brett, 24 Oct: "what I meant is mostly irrelevant. The question is ... how should these columns be set up to cover the relevant syntactic, semantic, phonological and morphological facts"). Where a 2021 column mixed facts, it is kept as the record (type `superseded`) and replaced: head selection by "as a determiner, can take a count singular / plural / non-count head" and the same three for the NP after *of* in a partitive ("requires" and "combines with" become derived); non-affirmative by "is a polarity item" and "sanctions polarity items" (*CGEL* ch. 9 §4.4, p. 834), with "positively oriented" beside them (§4.2, p. 829); semantic number by agreement (an NP it heads takes a singular or plural verb) and reference (it refers to or quantifies over single individuals, or pluralities together). Existential is redefined as quantificational force (*CGEL* p. 423: *some*, *any*, *no*), negative is narrowed to outright negation, and free choice gets a fused-relative frame. All of these are recoded blind.

## 6. Merging

- Two columns merge only if one follows logically from the other, in the same frame: a form couldn't have one value without the other.
- Distinct properties stay, however strongly they correlate (reflexive morphology and needing a binder; degree meaning and inflected comparatives). Their correlation is what the analysis measures. The family structure (section 9) keeps one mechanism from counting as several.

## 7. Blind coding

- Coders see the forms described in plain words (*which*, interrogative, as in *Which book?*), never the matrix's category-bearing names (`which_int_det`, `one_pron`, `her_dep`), and never the hypothesis.
- Each coder works in an empty directory outside the repository, with no tools: Claude subagents of a type with no file access, told to use no web tools; Codex read-only on an empty workspace; Muse with shell, write, and web disabled. The manifest records what each blinding rests on (agent type, or instruction).
- At least two model families code independently. Their codings, not Claude's draft, are the proposals Brett judges.
- **Calibration**: every pilot includes cells Brett coded in 2021; the coders' agreement with them is the reliability figure.

## 8. Provenance: the cell ledger

One row per cell in `analysis/cell-ledger/cells.csv`: form, super-column, column, frame, value, variety, evidence type, locator (page; record path and line; paper and line), verbatim quote, proposer, status, judged by, date. Quotes are checked by script as exact substrings of the cited file. The matrix is generated only from judged cells.

- **2021 cells** keep their values with provenance "Brett 2021" and aren't reopened, except: the 17 forms where "does not inflect" contradicts the inflection columns; the compound-genitive inconsistency (compounds n for having a genitive form, y for inflecting for case); the y-to-m pass (section 10); **the pro-form column**, which Brett judged miscoded on 24 Oct (y for every pronoun form including interrogative *who* and dummy *it*, n for every determinative, against *CGEL* p. 1462, which the combined manuscript cites) and which is recoded on a stated definition; and **cells where all blind coders agree against the 2021 value**, which go to Brett as possible errors (34 after the pilot and wave 2, gender and the particle column aside: `reviews/external-blind-coding-wave2-20261024/POSSIBLE-2021-ERRORS.md`).
- **2026-10 changes** keep their record provenance: 9 applied with corpus records, 19 proposed and awaiting Brett.

## 9. Analysis commitments

- **Scoring follows the sampling design, not any rival's categories** (Brett, 24 Oct). Each taxonomy labels every form its own way and the score never consults the labels; predictive accuracy is reported for the closed inventory (listed in full), for the noun sample, and for the two combined under each defensible weighting.
- **Copies of a form must not change the verdict** (Brett, 24 Oct: "does having 40 nouns lead to a different outcome than having 4000 nouns with the same properties?"). Within each rival's model, its own subcategories each enter the level above as one unit (grouping by subcategory is what each rival claims; scoring the contest between rivals is what mustn't use any rival's subcategories), so more nouns with the same properties make the noun estimates more certain without giving nouns more say; and scores are means per form within each design stratum, not sums. Tests that weight categories by their size (category utility, clustering) don't have this property and are run in size-neutral form. A check before any result is read: duplicate the noun rows tenfold and confirm the verdict doesn't move.
- **The held-out unit is the group, not the form** (a side agent's note, 24 Oct). The rivals differ only above the subcategory level, so holding out one form tests nothing that separates them: the other seventy determinatives predict it whichever tree is in use. Three tests do separate them. (1) **Leave one group out**: remove a whole subcategory and predict its cells from the rest through each tree (under the coordinate tree a held-out determinative group is predicted from the Noun node, under Hudson's from pronoun, under *CGEL*'s from the root), in turn for every group, with the groups defined on the undisputed core (forms every rival assigns alike). (2) **Disputed forms**: fit each tree on the core and predict the forms the rivals classify differently (the compound determinatives, which Hudson counts as pronouns; Van Eynde's adjectival determinatives). (3) **Small subgroups**: hold out the four determinative subgroups the desk count found pronoun-like (compounds, demonstratives, wh-forms, *we*/*us*/*you* before a noun), the test of multiple inheritance. Leave-one-form-out stays only as a check of within-group fit. A fake-data simulation (Gelman et al. 2020 §4.1) checks that these tests recover a known generating tree, and at what noun and column counts, before the blind wave runs. First round (24 Oct, `analysis/tree-recovery-sim/RESULTS.md`): the held-out group must be predicted through the tree's covariance with the between-node spread estimated from the data; predicting it from the plain mean of its siblings favours whichever tree gives it more siblings, and was biased toward the coordinate tree. Done properly, the test recovers the nested and separate trees at any sample size but the coordinate tree only with large noun-side samples (about 1 in 3 datasets at the current 12 common nouns, 2 proper nouns, 6 adjectives; 19 in 20 at 100, 24, 24), so small samples tilt it against the coordinate analysis. Second round (`analysis/tree-recovery-sim/REAL-PATTERNS.md`): when each simulated column copies which categories are n throughout on a real column, the test picks the nested tree whatever generated the data (nested 0.97 to 1.00; coordinate 0.00 to 0.10), because of the 77 columns with coded noun and adjective rows, 11 are properties only pronouns and determinatives can have and none a property only common and proper nouns have. On the columns that apply to every category the bias goes, and recovery reaches 0.90 or more with about 115 such columns (we have about 22). **So the column set, not the noun sample, decides the test.** Remedies, both to run: a column frame, properties drawn from a source no rival chose and balanced across categories; and the test on the columns every category can have, with the rest reported separately.
- **Main test**: a hierarchical ordinal model of the cells, with forms partially pooled within subcategories and columns within super-columns; the taxonomy is the pooling structure (nested: pronoun and determinative share a parent below Noun; coordinate: they don't). Taxonomies are compared by predictive accuracy with **whole forms held out** (leave-one-out by form, not by cell), and derived columns excluded so that nothing leaks.
- **Multiverse**, on Brett's standing rule: m as y, as n, and as ordered; variety cells as y and as n; the similarity coefficient for any distance-based check as simple matching and as Jaccard.
- **The count of shared derived properties is a heuristic**, not corroboration: grammatical properties aren't independent characters descending on a tree. Polarity (which state is derived) comes from *CGEL*'s diagnostic statements (the defining and negative properties of nouns, ch. 5 §1, and adjectives, ch. 6 §2), with the adjective rows as a check.
- **Uneven search effort is reported**: the determinatives' edges were searched hard for the fusion paper, the nouns' weren't. The m-as-y arm is the one this can't move.

## 10. Passes

1. **Blind coding** of new cells by three model families (section 7).
2. **The y-to-m pass** over the 2021 y cells: one question each (fully available in the ordinary use, or degraded?), flagging candidates with a reason; Brett judges only the flagged.
3. **Adversarial notes**: every n a coder gives carries its strongest y considered, so new n cells reach Brett with the best case against them.
4. **Corpus records** are mapped to cells, each with a proposed value, a reason, and the record path.
5. **Brett judges**, a super-column at a time, after the frame list is frozen.

## 11. Forks for Brett

1. **Gender: settled, both** (Brett, 24 Oct: "Gelman would say do both"). Gender is coded under both definitions, by reference (*CGEL*'s terms: what a referring use selects) and as fixed in the word (the 2021 coding and Reynolds 2025), and the analysis runs both as multiverse arms.
2. **The *no*/*none* alternation: inflection, coded as such** (Brett, 24 Oct: "It's a morphological property, so I don't think keeping the label out of the column is the right move"; his investigation continues). *CGEL* p. 410 puts the pairs in one table, "A handful of items have distinct inflectional forms for dependent and independent uses": dependent *my your her our their no*, independent *mine yours hers ours theirs none*, "used in (pure) determiner function" and "in head function" respectively. It also treats the pair as one lexeme (p. 812), lists it among the determinatives with inflectional variation (p. 356), and uses the form choice as a headedness diagnostic (*No*/\**None more*, p. 432 n. 49). Coding: a labelled column "inflects for dependent and independent use (CGEL p. 410)", y for *no*/*none* and the genitive pronoun pairs; the pronoun-specific "distinct independent genitive form" column stays (the new one isn't entailed by it). GPT Pro's allomorphy alternative (24 Oct) is kept as a sensitivity arm only if Brett's investigation turns against inflection.
3. ***Multiple*, *countless*, and *poor*** were added to the reference set in the pilot, to give the quantity adjectives three members and the fused-head adjectives two. Keep?
4. **The semantic column whose sense can't be recovered** from the coded forms (`proportional`): define, or drop. (`distributive` and `joint` are now defined as allowing a distributive and a collective reading, which fits their 2021 cells: *each* and *many a* are y for the first and n for the second.)
5. ***oneself*** is n for reflexive though it has the *-self* form; if reflexive is morphological, that's a slip.
6. **Sixteen columns whose definitions departed from the 2021 senses** in wave 2's calibration (listed with proposed fixes in `notes/2026-10-24-codebook-and-pilot.md`). One, pro-form, is y for exactly the 65 pronouns and no determinative: it encodes the category and should be excluded like the fused-head label. Six need Brett's definitions: *not* (why the genitives?), *at least*, *quite* (*quite the*?), coordination with independent genitives, coordination with other determiners (*the*?), and attributive against pre-head modifier.
7. **What may enter the analysis without a cell-by-cell judgment** (raised by a side agent, 24 Oct). The ledger feeds only judged cells to the analysis, and today's columns and the planned noun sample add several thousand cells. Proposal: a status **accepted**, distinct from judged, for two kinds of cell. (a) **Facts drafted by rule** (`analysis/codebook/morphology-facts.csv`), accepted once Brett approves the rules and settles the cells they flag. (b) **Cells where all three model families agree**, with no m and no "set:" or "echo:" note, accepted subject to an audit: Brett judges a random sample stratified by subcategory, so the audit shows whether agreement is as reliable for nouns as for determinatives, and a subcategory whose audit fails goes back to review. Splits, m's, frame notes, and flags still come to Brett. The analysis runs on judged cells alone and on judged plus accepted, so any reliance on accepted cells is visible. Evidence so far: on the calibration forms in settled columns, cells where all three coders agreed matched Brett's 2021 cells 371 times in 390 (wave 2, an upper bound, since some of its anchors named the forms coded); in wave 3, whose columns had been redefined, 100 in 112. A sibling project has already designed this: `papers/development/diagnostic-validity` (DECISIONS, 2026-06-10, F6) draws a simple random gold sample of cells with a fixed seed before any LLM rating exists, codes it blind, and uses it to correct estimates built on LLM judgments (design-based supervised learning, or prediction-powered inference). The same design fits here, with one caution to check: such corrections apply to aggregate estimates, so the taxonomy comparison would have to be stated as one.
8. **The noun sample** (Brett, 24 Oct: "the number of pronouns and determinatives is bounded; it's the full universe. Will the number of nouns we analyze bias the results?"). Proposal (revised 24 Oct after the simulation: 100 common nouns, 24 proper nouns, 24 adjectives; first proposed as 40): common nouns drawn at random from SUBTLEX-UK, stratified by Zipf band and by count, non-count, and plural-only, plus a few proper nouns, with the seven quantity nouns kept as a labelled boundary stratum rather than pooled into the noun estimate; and the comparison rerun on resampled noun sets of 12, 25, and 50. Scoring (corrected 24 Oct, after Brett: "if we score each subcategory separately doesn't that assume what we're checking?"): not by subcategory, since the rivals don't share subcategories (Hudson counts the compounds as pronouns, *CGEL* as determinatives, Van Eynde splits determinatives between adjective and noun). Scores are kept apart by the sampling design instead, which no rival supplies: the closed inventory, listed in full, and the drawn nouns and adjectives. The headline reports each, plus their combination under each defensible weighting. SUBTLEX-UK is the frame `diagnostic-validity` already uses (DECISIONS, 2026-06-10, amendment F1(a)/F2: Zipf bands, since a within-tag rank couldn't fill its bands); a copy is in that project's `data/`.
