# Reconsidering exclamative *what* and degree *such* under D-noun
<!-- SUMMARY: CGEL calls them adjectives on its three determinative criteria; the paper's own precedent of complex determinatives (many a, a few) and Van Eynde's lexical analysis make determinative the better fit; decision for Brett · status: analysis note · updated: 2026-09-17 -->

Brett asked (2026-09-17) whether degree *such* and exclamative *what* (*such a disaster*, *what a disaster*) should stay adjectives, as the manuscript has them following *CGEL*, or be determinatives under D-noun. Determinative *such* (*such contract as may be executed*) and interrogative *what* (*what book*) are separate lexemes and already determinatives; this note is only about the pair *CGEL* calls adjectives.

## What *CGEL* says, and why

*CGEL* pp. 539–540 (Ch. 6 §2.4.2) gives three criteria for determinatives: (a) mutual exclusiveness with the articles (*a good book* but *\*a this book*); (b) admissibility of count singular NPs as head; (c) occurrence as fused determiner-head in the partitive construction. Exclamative *what* \enquote{fails all of the determinative criteria (a)--(c)} and its \enquote{distribution ... is comparable to that of the adjective such} (p. 540). Hence \enquote{The adjectives such and exclamative what occur as external rather than internal modifier in construction with a} (p. 435), with the hedge that in *such promise*, *what promise* \enquote{there is no determiner here and no reason to say that the adjective is in predeterminer position}.

Two things *CGEL* also says bear on the decision. Criterion (a) admits \enquote{the complex forms a few and a little} (p. 539), and *CGEL* includes \enquote{certain complex expressions (such as a few, many a, cardinal numerals ...)} among determinatives (p. 356); *many a* \enquote{is syntactically inert: nothing can intervene between many and a ... Like a, many a always functions as determiner} (p. 394). So the article inside a complex determinative does not count against criterion (a).

## The case for determinative

1. *What a* and *such a* have the profile of *many a*: always Det of a count singular NP, syntactically inert (nothing intervenes; Van Eynde 2007 (42): *\*what long a speech*, *\*such long a speech*), and the article is selected by the word. On the complex-determinative analysis they satisfy (b) and (a) is moot, exactly as for *a few* and *many a*. Criterion (c) fails, but the articles fail (c) too.
2. In non-count and plural NPs (*what promise*, *such people*, *what fools*) the word is NP-initial with no other determiner, where *much promise* and *many people* have Det. *CGEL*'s \enquote{internal modifier} there is a consequence of the adjective decision, not independent evidence for it.
3. The adjectival profile is absent. Neither word has grade, degree modification (*\*very such*, *\*so what a*), or, for exclamative *what*, predicative use; degree *such* has predicative *such that*, which doesn't discriminate (*they are many*).
4. Van Eynde (2007, §5), analysing the Big Mess Construction (*how long a bridge*), argues that *such a* and *what a* are not instances of it: \enquote{what and such are invariably lexical}, they \enquote{lexically select a nominal which is either unmarked or introduced by the indefinite article}, and they are functors, the type his grammar gives determiners, not the independent adjuncts of the Big Mess. He needs two entries for *such*: the article-selecting degree one and an unmarked \enquote{kind} one \enquote{just like the adjectival modifiers} (*many such problems*). The kind entry is the determinative *such* Brett already distinguishes.
5. In the 2021 matrix the row *such* (determinative) is *the*'s nearest neighbour inside the dependent-only cluster; degree *such* and exclamative *what*, if determinatives, would be dependent-only restricted members of the same kind as *the*, *a* and *every*, and *what a* / *such a* would join *many a* and *a few* as complex determinatives.

## What changes if adopted

- §2.1's footnote (L97) stops citing *such*/*what* as adjectives before *a*; they become restricted members in §3.1, with the change from *CGEL* stated and pp. 539–540 answered by the complex-determinative precedent.
- The typicality register loses its only source for adjectives in predeterminer function (the cell becomes unsourced), which is the honest result.
- Nothing else in the argument moves; *CGEL*'s [34] contrast (*so many mistakes* against *\*so numerous mistakes*) is untouched, since *many* was never at issue.
- Whether degree *such* and kind *such* are one lexeme or two follows Van Eynde's two entries or the paper's own lexeme practice; either way the paper should say.

## Against

*CGEL*'s criteria were designed to draw exactly this line, and the reanalysis reads (a) as satisfied through the complex form rather than by the bare word. A referee holding to the bare-word reading will call it stipulation. The reply is that *CGEL* itself applies the complex-form reading to *a few* and *many a*, so the criterion is already read that way for two items.

## Recommendation

Adopt: degree *such* and exclamative *what* as restricted determinatives, *such a* and *what a* as complex determinatives beside *many a*, with one paragraph in §3.1 stating the change and its grounds. It costs a sentence in §2.1 and gains a cleaner restricted-member class whose defining property (dependent-only use, article-requiring for count singulars) the 2021 clustering already shows.
