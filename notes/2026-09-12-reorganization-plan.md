## Judgment

Reorganize using Claude’s separation of the category argument from the internal-structure comparison. Lead the category case with the quantificational-common-noun connection, followed by the determiner-function comparison. Borrow Grok’s early statement that ordinary Head is available under either taxonomy, and GLM’s proposal to make the weighing principle prominent before the evidence.

The manuscript’s main question remains whether determinative belongs within Noun. Its answer rests on the combined profile, whose constructional connections carry particular weight. The structural comparison shows how the proposal is implemented and what it costs. Separate D with ordinary Head shares the unification, NP determiner realization, and peripheral-modification grammar. Those gains cannot be counted again as discriminating evidence for D-noun over that alternative.

This is an organizational clarity repair. The principal difficulty is that the current evidence sections mix observed distributions with their competing representations. A successful revision lets readers state the category rationale before the structural comparison and then recognize which gains and costs distinguish which accounts. If doing that requires new empirical premises or a different basis for nounhood, the proposed repair has exceeded its scope.

## Wording for the opening distinction

Two decisions are at issue: whether determinatives belong within Noun, and whether their expressions receive ordinary Head structure. The proposed hierarchy licenses that structure by inheritance; a separate primary D can also receive it through a rule shared with Noun. The category proposal rests on the distribution of nominal connections, while the structural comparison tests its implementation and costs.
