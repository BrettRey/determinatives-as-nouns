## Held for Brett (analysis or argument, not wording)

1. **Kind *such* (l. 311), misattributed.** The text says the kind sense (*such contract as may be executed*, *many such problems*) "is the determinative *such* of *CGEL*'s inventory". *CGEL* p. 435 treats *many such problems* as the adjective in its kind sense; its only determinative *such* is a legal-register use meaning roughly "any" (n. 52: *such other date as the parties may agree*). *such contract as may be executed* looks like that legal use. The repair depends on whether kind *such* joins the determinatives in this account. If it does: "the kind sense of *such* (*many such problems*), an adjective in *CGEL* \citep[435]{huddleston2002}, joins them, along with its legal-register determinative *such* (435 n. 52)". If it doesn't, say kind *such* stays adjectival. Related (B): l. 309 gives *such* and *what* Det function in *such promise*, *what fools*, where *CGEL* has internal modifiers and "no determiner here" (p. 435); the paper departs on function as well as category there, unannounced.
2. ***CGEL*'s "third approach" (reader A).** p. 421, directly after the ellipsis discussion the l. 54 footnote cites, sets out "change of function without change of category" (the determinative as head: *[Many] would agree with you*) and rejects it because it "does not handle cases like" *I prefer cotton shirts to [nylon]* and *Mary earns [double]*. That is Table 1's separate-D-ordinary-Head row, considered and rejected in print by the paper's framework grammar. The manuscript never mentions it (searched: 421, nylon, cotton, double, third approach, change of function, Many would, without change of category; only hit l. 54's 420–421 for ellipsis). §4.3's reason for keeping fusion for adjectives answers the substance, but not as a reply to *CGEL*. A sentence at l. 50 or in the l. 54 footnote, answered in §4, would close it.
3. ***Kim isn't much of an actor* (l. 131).** Offered as "a quantity NP in predicative function"; *CGEL* p. 415 has *much* there as a degree quantifier for a property expressed by the predicative NP, and the construction as "strongly non-affirmative" (\**Ed is much of a husband*). In a paragraph about when the subject condition lapses, that restriction matters. The same sentence's "flexibility of modified *much* that Reynolds 2024 notes" concerns *much* modifying adjectives (pp. 5, 17), not a predicative complement (reader E).
4. ***Henrietta … blue* (l. 137).** *CGEL* analyses *blue* there as a fused modifier-head (p. 417) and argues fused heads aren't restricted to coordination (p. 421); the sentence reads as though *CGEL* called it coordination-licensed reduction.
5. ***the few* (l. 782).** *CGEL* p. 416: non-cardinal fused modifier-heads are "hardly admissible unless accompanied by a following modifier", which is why its examples are *these few here*, *the many who did*. The manuscript's *the lucky few* attestations both have relatives. Add the condition, or defend bare *the few*.
6. ***a certain* (l. 311).** "No complex determinatives remain" omits *a certain*, which *CGEL* tentatively treats as one (p. 393: "it may be best to treat a certain as a complex determinative"). The negative-claims pass may raise the same point.
7. **Head genitive in the "stated separately" list (l. 738).** *CGEL* defines the head genitive once, by "the inflection of the head noun", and lists *everyone's* beside *Edward's* (p. 479); ll. 147 and 730 already put it that way. It doesn't belong among rules *CGEL* states separately.
8. **Payne et al. 2010 (l. 506).** On the cited pages they use the continuity argument to keep these words *out of* the pronoun subclass of Noun, because their premodifiers are adverbs ("If these words were pronouns, the attributive modifier function would have to admit adverbs as well as adjectives", p. 41). l. 512 answers it in substance; the paper doesn't say the argument is theirs. A charitable-engagement point.
9. **Reynolds 2024 survey (l. 700).** The source calls it "a highly selective comparison" from the author's judgments, includes *no*/*any*, and concludes that nothing sets *more* apart as an adverb, not "any of them". Suggested: "compares a selection of the determinatives … (*more, less, enough, that, much, a little, no, any, all*) … with no pattern that sets *more* apart as an adverb".
10. **Reynolds 2026 on cardinals (ll. 480, 756).** The numerals paper retains *CGEL*'s two uses rather than arguing for them, and its Table 1 classes *Ten came* and *the ten (of them)* as determinatives in fused Det-Head and Mod-Head. This paper prefers ordinary Head. Under the corpus-awareness law that divergence needs an adjudication line in DECISIONS.md ("deliberately distinguished"). Not written here: it's Brett's position to state.
11. **Smaller:** *CGEL*'s trees draw a one-word fused head as D, not DP (pp. 26, 331, 412); l. 752 *CGEL*'s §20.4 is about proper *names*; l. 293 "while recording" reads as a concession where *CGEL* gives the restricted expansion as its reason; l. 275 *the* modifies the comparative, not "comparative AdjPs" (p. 1132); l. 77 Payne et al. 2010 supports only the NP-as-determiner half (Payne et al. 2007: 567 states both); Solt's "adjectives of quantity" is her name for the class (p. 221), not a category assignment; l. 329's "two parents" could cite Payne et al. 2007's discussion of the single-mother condition.
