# Source reread B: CGEL citations, manuscript ll. 209–841
<!-- SUMMARY: source-reread of every huddleston2002 citation and uncited CGEL attribution in §2.6 to the appendix · status: complete · updated: 2026-09-19 -->

**Summary.** Of 43 cited contexts, 30 SUPPORTED, 7 PAGE, 6 NUANCE, none OVERSTATED or MISATTRIBUTED. Among the uncited attributions, one is MISATTRIBUTED: l. 311 says the kind sense of *such* is "the determinative *such* of *CGEL*'s inventory", but CGEL treats kind *such* (including *many such problems*) as an adjective, and its determinative *such* is a minor legal-register use meaning roughly "any". Three uncited attributions are NUANCE: the complex-determinative residue at l. 311, DP versus D in CGEL's fused-head trees, and the head genitive at l. 738.

**Source and offset.** I used CGEL's text layer split one file per printed page (`scratchpad/cgel-pages/pNNNN.txt`, where pNNNN is the printed page). I spot-checked against `literature/huddlestonpullum2002.pdf` with `pdftotext -layout` at PDF pp. 411–412, 443, 489, 540–541 and 569–572, using a constant offset of +20 (PDF = printed + 20). Each check matched the running head: printed 391/392, 423, 469, 520/521, 549 and 551. I read the manuscript at SHA-256 2c6c7953…0afe, which matches the dispatch hash.

Quotations are verbatim from the text layer, with the printed page given.

## Uncited attributions to CGEL in ll. 209–841

- **U1, l. 311: "the kind sense of *such* (*such contract as may be executed*, *many such problems*) is the determinative *such* of *CGEL*'s inventory." MISATTRIBUTED.** CGEL says the reverse. p. 435: "Such differs from what in that it can follow certain determiners other than a: one such device, all/several/many such problems … In this latter construction, however, it has only the 'kind' sense", in a section headed "Adjectival predeterminers" that calls it "The adjectives such and exclamative what". p. 1546: "Here such is an adjective functioning as modifier in NP structure. It may be concerned with either degree or kind." CGEL's determinative *such* is only p. 435 n. 52: "a minor use of such found primarily in legal register where it is a determinative rather than an adjective: … [such other date as the parties may agree]. The NP here is count singular, so such (meaning roughly 'any') must be in determiner function" (see also p. 356 n. 16). *Such contract as may be executed* matches that legal use; *many such problems* is CGEL's adjective. Suggested repair: "*CGEL*'s own determinative *such* is a legal-register use meaning roughly 'any' (*such other date as the parties may agree*; 435 n. 52); it treats kind *such* (*many such problems*) as an adjective like degree *such* [435, 1546]." Then say whether kind *such* also joins the determinatives under this analysis.
- **U2, l. 311: "no complex determinatives remain … *CGEL*'s other members of that class, cardinals above a hundred". NUANCE.** The cardinal range matches p. 356: "certain complex expressions (such as a few, many a, cardinal numerals expressing numbers greater than 100)". But CGEL also tentatively adds *a certain*. p. 393: "it may be best to treat a certain as a complex determinative". p. 385 also counts numerals "involving fractions (one and a half)" as syntactically complex. The manuscript never mentions *a certain* anywhere (grep "a certain" found only the compound-determinative example *a certain someone*). Suggested repair: name *a certain* [393] and say how it is handled, or soften "no complex determinatives remain".
- **U3, ll. 331, 388 (Fig. 1 caption), 413: "in *CGEL*, its DP jointly fills Det of NP and Head of Nom"; "the DP headed by *some* fills Det–Head". NUANCE** (word versus phrase). CGEL's own trees draw a one-word fused head as D. p. 412 [7a]: "Det-Head: D few"; p. 26 [13a] has "Det: D some"; p. 331 [10a] has "Det: D those". Its prose allows "a determinative (or a phrase headed by a determinative, i.e. a DP)" (p. 330). The DP layer is defensible but is not CGEL's drawing. Suggested repair: a footnote such as "CGEL's simplified trees show a one-word fused head as D (p. 412); I show the DP its treatment of determiners implies (p. 330)."
- **U4, l. 738: "Several rules *CGEL* states separately for nouns and determinatives … and the head genitive in *everyone's* and *Edward's*". NUANCE.** For the head genitive this is inaccurate. CGEL states the rule once, as "marked as such by the inflection of the head noun", and lists "[everyone's] responsibility" and "[somebody's] initiative" beside "[Edward's] daughter" (p. 479). The problem is that the definition names the head *noun* while applying to determinatives, which is how l. 147 and l. 730 put it correctly. Suggested repair: remove the head genitive from the "states separately" list and add a clause: "and the head genitive, which CGEL defines by the head noun's inflection while listing *everyone's* beside *Edward's*". The other three items are SUPPORTED: number-transparent nouns (pp. 349–350, 501–503) versus fused determiner-head agreement (p. 507); DP-internal *almost all* (p. 431) versus NP-peripheral modifiers (pp. 436–437); and the separate "Determinatives" and "NPs" subsections of AdjP modification (pp. 549–550).
- **l. 218** (caption: "Interrogative and relative forms follow *CGEL*'s inventory"): SUPPORTED (p. 356 [5xiv]; pp. 422, 429). CGEL also has interrogative/relative pronoun *what* and relative pronoun *which* (p. 422). The cell "who, whose" gives examples and doesn't claim to be a full list.
- **l. 287** ("*CGEL* … can … preserve [category continuity]"): SUPPORTED. p. 356: "it is generally more appropriate to handle these differences in terms of function rather than category".
- **l. 303** ("In *CGEL* itself … *such a mess* has *a* in Det and *such* as predeterminer modifier"): SUPPORTED (p. 331 [9ic]; p. 435 [5ia] "external modifier").
- **l. 401** ("*CGEL* permits an NP headed by a nominal with no determiner" for *She left*, *Kim left*): SUPPORTED (pp. 26, 330, 429, 516).
- **l. 556** ("*CGEL* also uses fusion with nouns"): SUPPORTED (p. 410, independent genitive pronouns in fused heads).
- **ll. 627, 633** (CGEL distinguishes DP from genitive NP; Det: {DP, NP, PP}): SUPPORTED (p. 330; pp. 355, 357).
- **l. 689** ("*CGEL* restricts NP modifiers [in attributive uses]"): SUPPORTED. p. 551 heading: "Pre-head NP modifiers not permitted", with the single exception at p. 552 n. 8. Consider adding the citation.
- **ll. 732, 736, 754, 760** (CGEL's output categories: the compound and cardinal inputs change category; conversion for one subcategory and a secondary use for the other): SUPPORTED (p. 423 n. 43; p. 385; pp. 520–521, where no category change is stated for proper names).
- **l. 818** (Table: "*CGEL*: Pronoun within Noun; determinative separate"): SUPPORTED. p. 22: "we regard pronouns as a subclass of nouns, not a distinct primary category."

## An unacknowledged departure from CGEL (not a citation error)

l. 309 gives *such* and *what* Det function in *such promise* and *what fools*. CGEL analyses these as internal modifiers with no determiner. p. 435: "In non-count or plural NPs, as in the [b] examples, they are in internal position: there is no determiner here". l. 303 correctly says that for *such a mess* "only the category of *such* is at issue". For the plural and non-count cases, though, function is at issue too, and the text doesn't say so. Worth one clause.
