## reynolds2021 (empirical source)

**Reported results, read before the discussion** (pp. 8–11): matrix of 138 word forms (73 determinatives, 65 pronouns) × 232 features (139 morphological, 3 phonological, 36 semantic, 54 syntactic), each cell coded "may exhibit the feature" or "never does" (p. 8). k-groups with k = 2: "Only three CGEL determinatives were assigned to the k-groups pronoun cluster, the personal determinatives *you*, *we*, and *us*" and six pronouns went the other way; agreement 129/138 (p. 9). Dendrogram (Figure 1, p. 10): *the* and *a* are inside the lower (determinative) box; *the*'s nearest leaves are dummy *there* and *it*, which the article flags ("The two dummy pronouns *it* and *there* are enclosed in a smaller red triangle inside the blue triangle", p. 9). DISCO F = 11.67, p ≤ 0.001 (p. 11). The appendix code runs k-groups with `nstart = 1`; no sensitivity to initialization or feature selection is reported anywhere in the article (searched the `.md` for *initiali*, *nstart*, *random start*, *feature selection*, *unsupervised*: only the code line matched).

**Interpretation (pp. 12–13):** results "appear to be consistent with the analysis set out in CGEL. They are not, however, entirely inconsistent with claims, such as HUDSON's (2004), that determinatives are a subcategory of pronouns or vice versa" (p. 12); extending to open categories would need "a defensible sampling procedure" (p. 13).

**Corpus-awareness flag.** Reynolds 2026 analyses cardinal determinatives with *CGEL*'s fused heads (Table 1: "FUSED DET-HEAD Ten came"; "FUSED MOD-HEAD The ten (of them) came", p. 12; *twenty-two* "in fused-determiner head function", p. 4). DECISIONS.md (row "Cardinals") adopts the 2026 categories but has no line on this difference in how the phrases project. Suggest a **deliberately distinguished** entry. The difference is between implementations the paper already compares; it isn't a contradiction the argument depends on.
