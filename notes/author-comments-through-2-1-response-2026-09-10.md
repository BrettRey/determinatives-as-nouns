# Responses to your comments through §2.1

Codex — 10 September 2026

My replies are written here as visible prose. The [annotated manuscript](determinatives-as-nouns-review-2026-09-10.md) contains your original comments. The assessment below proposes the substantive changes; the diagram line weights and two underlines are already applied to the LaTeX source.

## Ordinary headedness and inclusion within Noun — comments c2 and c7

Your objection holds within the grammar this paper proposes. Once determinative is a subtype of Noun, the existing Noun–Nom–NP projection rules apply to it. Ordinary headedness is inherited. Presenting it as an additional, comparably optional proposal misstates that relationship.

The qualification is that inclusion plus the projection rules supplies the inference. Category membership alone does not logically prohibit a construction-specific fusion analysis. But that logical possibility needn't divide your proposal into variants. Requiring fusion specifically for independent determinatives would need an additional justification.

The fourth account in §7.2 does permit the analogue of the asymmetry you identify in Figure 1. It combines ordinary nominal projection inside a determining phrase with an extra fused structure for independent use. Following the fragment's rules, it replaces the fused DP with an NP headed by a determinative noun: the extra outer structure surrounds an already projected NP. This is formally constructible, but the paper supplies no independent reason to require it for simple *some*.

### Proposed replacement in §1

> In the D-noun analysis, determinatives inherit the nominal projection rules of Noun. In independent uses such as *Some left*, the determinative heads a Nom that heads an NP. Lexical restrictions still govern which uses are available. The placement of determinative alongside pronoun is a further proposal, assessed in §5.

The strongest separate-D rival still belongs in the comparison. It can license the same ordinary projection across N and D, so that structural benefit alone doesn't establish nounhood. The nominal profile and CGEL's rationale for including pronouns remain necessary to the taxonomic argument.

The article restrictions in §6 and the use permissions in §7 also remain. The Head rules don't grant every noun every use. Likewise, inclusion within Noun doesn't establish coordinate placement alongside pronoun; the provisional status of that placement in §5 remains appropriate.

## Independent genitives and the April paper

The April passages require a correction to my earlier assessment. In the April 29 source, §4.1 presents independent *mine*, *yours* and *ours* as ordinary heads, but attributes that analysis to CGEL. The next paragraph invokes CGEL's fused genitives to establish compatibility with nounhood. These passages do not establish a consistent commitment to retaining genitive fusion in your own grammar.

CGEL distinguishes anaphoric *mine* referring to a possessed entity, which it analyses as Det–Head, from predicative possessive *mine* in *it's mine*, which it analyses as pure Head (pp. 410–411). Correcting the April attribution did not warrant adopting CGEL's anaphoric analysis as yours.

The September claims that fusion is already needed for genitives (§4.7) and remains available (§7.1) therefore need attention. I recommend keeping CGEL's analysis explicitly attributed as a comparison and removing the unargued commitment to retaining it in the D-noun proposal. An ordinary-Head analysis of independent genitives would require an explicit statement of your own position, rather than attribution to CGEL.

## The DP paragraph — comment c6 and suggestion s7

I recommend your change in emphasis. Pullum and Miller belong here alongside Bruening. The existing reference is the English 2022 paper, whose author order is Pullum and Miller; the French 2022 paper has Miller and Pullum.

I would avoid calling the English paper non-generativist: it allows generative grammars with dependency as well as phrase structure. The intended contrast can be expressed by citing its broader headedness argument alongside Bruening's rejection within a generative analysis.

### Proposed replacement in §2.1

> The question of lexical classification is distinct from the headedness question associated with Abney's (1987) DP hypothesis. Under that hypothesis, D heads expressions such as *some apples*. I retain noun-headed NPs and reject the DP analysis for English, following the arguments of Pullum and Miller (2022) and Bruening (2020).

Rejecting the DP hypothesis does not itself put determinatives within Noun. This distinction remains necessary, but the paragraph can state your position directly without reopening ordinary Head versus fusion as two versions of your proposal.

## The auxiliary footnote — comment c1

I recommend deleting it. The precedent concerns inclusion of a distinctive subcategory, which the body already states. *Must* illustrates why a full verbal inflectional paradigm is not required of every verb. CGEL p. 75 records its lack of a preterite and the modals' lack of secondary forms.

“Completely defective” would be too strong if it meant no morphological contrasts at all, since CGEL also recognizes negative *mustn't*. That qualification needn't enter the paper. The subsequent nominal evidence already supplies the argument for this particular extension.

## Smaller wording changes — suggestions s1–s5

The relational wording is more precise. Taking s2 and s3 together requires a small grammatical repair:

> This distinction separates what kind of word *some* is from what syntactic relationships its phrase participates in.

The additions *though* and *here* also fit.

Palmer p. 24, §40 supports the historical contrast in s5: he discusses the traditional division into qualitative and determinative adjectives before grouping the latter with pronouns. He also reports divergent classifications. I recommend “against the traditional adjectival classification” rather than wording that implies an uncontested established analysis.
