## 2. The secondary-use schema: list or property (B5; P2, D10)

**This is the one real decision in the set.**

The rule's input class is a property followed by a list: "a Noun lexeme whose primary use identifies or quantifies over individuals, a name, a personal pronoun, a compound determinative, or a cardinal". Read by its property it over-generates (*every* and *each* quantify over individuals, and the next sentence has to exclude *every* by hand). Read as a list it only restates the cases already observed.

On 19 September the projectibility audit (P2) proposed a property that would project: **each input carries its own description of the individuals**, a name, person and gender, *-one/-body/-thing*, a cardinality. It predicted that personal pronouns would have the use, which the paper then left open. Today's searches confirmed it: *a he or a she* (24 lines), *a they*, *one me for ten mes*, *the good me*. That's the paper's one projection that was stated before it was tested and passed. The property also excludes the articles and *every* for a reason instead of by listing.

Proposed (also *name* → *proper noun* in the category list, D10; "the bearers of the name" stays, as it's semantic):

> The rule can be stated once, with its input class stated {++by a property++}: a Noun lexeme {~~whose primary use identifies or quantifies over individuals~>that carries its own description of the individuals it identifies or quantifies over~~}, a {~~name~>proper noun (a name)~~}, a personal pronoun {++(person and gender)++}, a compound determinative {++(\mention{-one}, \mention{-body}, \mention{-thing})++}, or a cardinal {++(a cardinality)++}, has a secondary use in which it denotes the set of individuals fitting {~~its~>that~~} description, …

> The articles and \mention{every} {++carry no description of their own without a following nominal, and++} have no such use (\ungram{\mention{an every}}). {++Stated by that property, the rule predicted the pronoun input rather than listing it.++} Within Noun the rule has four inputs. …

Only *an every* is starred; *an each* hasn't been searched, so it isn't added.

**Open question for you, not for the manuscript yet:** *the many* and *the few* take an article and denote a set (*the needs of the many*). Does the property include them (a vague cardinality), exclude them (no description of individuals), or need a clause about plural inflection, which they lack? The 19 September source reread (B8) already flags bare *the few* against *CGEL* p. 416. A referee will ask. I haven't proposed wording.
