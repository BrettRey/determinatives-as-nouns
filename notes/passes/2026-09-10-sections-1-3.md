## Implemented decisions

- **Opening and figures (s1–s6):** the nominal projection is explicitly shared with common nouns, proper nouns and pronouns; Figure 1 labels dependent and independent *some* in both analyses. Palmer's own “determinative adjectives” terminology is restored. The opening states the language-specific scope and retains Palmer, Postal and Sommerstein prominently.
- **Earlier accounts (c1–c9, s7–s8):** §2.2 compares English evidence and coverage. Postal's reflexives and *we men*, his representational levels, and Sommerstein's *one/it* and reflexive counterarguments now have substantive treatment. Hudson's inventory exclusions and mutual dependency are described as concrete differences. The unsupported uptake addition for mutual dependency is not adopted. Spinillo's redistribution and residual article category remain, with category continuity separated from a category diagnosis. Anderson, Déchaine–Wiltschko and Van Eynde are removed from this focused comparison; no claim that their frameworks are intrinsically invalid is inserted. Their bibliography entries remain available.
- **Broader profile (c10, c12, s9–s22, s31):** Table 2 now distinguishes external syntax, meaning, inflection, word formation, determination, internal modification and complementation. The prose explains the range and restrictions behind those entries. Grade, restricted proper-noun number and *something's* are included. “Nominal postmodifiers” becomes the unambiguous “NP-internal postmodifiers”. Case and reflexive number are distinguished in the pronoun column.
- **Determiner comparison (s23–s30, s32, s34–s36):** *my preferences*, *Kim's preferences* and *people's preferences* replace the book examples, with the proper-noun tree and its caption updated. *Someone's preferences* has the requested Payne et al. citation. The text retains the author's qualification that most determinatives resist case marking.
- **Modifier objection (s37, c13 and the subsequent conversational correction):** §3.3 identifies systematic internal AdvP premodification as the strongest internal-syntactic objection. Peripheral NP modification, adverbial postmodification and the attributive *almost textbook* exception are distinguished. CGEL's integrated relatives with personal pronouns support a qualified comparison of modifier ranges. The rejected exclusivity claim is not inserted. Section 7.3 now states the corresponding loss in generality of the modifier rule.
- **Outward derivation:** new §3.4 compares potential D-base nominalizations (*nothingness, oneness, thisness, fewness, muchness*) and adverbial *mostly*, the input ranges of *-ness* and *-ly*, and noun/pronoun compounding. Direct derivation, prior conversion and numeral constituency remain distinct questions. The treatment acknowledges mixed nominal and adjectival affinities; it claims neither a uniformly productive D rule nor a morphological proof of Noun membership. The abstract reflects that balance.
