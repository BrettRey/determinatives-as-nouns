## Terminological hygiene

The operative contrasts are determinative/category versus determiner/function; Noun/superordinate category versus its subcategories; lexical N or D versus phrasal NP or DP; Nom versus NP; lexical membership versus ordinary or fused headedness; inclusion versus coordinate or nested rank; independent use versus argument or predicative permission; shared coverage versus preferred grammatical organization; and syntactic projection versus inductive projectibility.

Each remains explicit in the later comparison. In particular, separate D with ordinary headedness projects Nom and NP; nominal D can retain fusion; a category does not confer unrestricted permissions; and the matrix separates supplied profiles without determining rank. The matched fragment excludes predicatives explicitly. Its use-permission condition follows each NP's own lexical head. Article permission and common-noun determination requirements remain separate.

Two repairs were made. The first Nom gloss had defined it as a noun and its internal dependents, inadvertently prejudging the later separate-D projection alternative. It now identifies the phrase level below NP without stipulating a noun head. The general fusion gloss compressed different structural levels into an ambiguous phrase; it now states joint realization under structural and adjacency conditions, followed immediately by the unchanged concrete Det-of-NP/Head-of-Nom analysis. Payne, Huddleston and Pullum 2007, introductory definition and §1.3, support this formulation.

The mechanical term-gloss check returned two candidates: frontmatter determinative and projection. Determinative is explicitly introduced in §1; nominal projection is explained by the adjacent Nom/NP gloss and Figure 1. These are heuristic first-use findings, not unresolved category/function errors. No automatic override was added. Other local technical terms, including structural saturation, productivity, restrictor and fragment, are explicitly delimited. No canon-term drift was found. Manually prefixed initials were removed from two Lyons textual citations because bibliography disambiguation already supplies J.; the PDF previously rendered J. J. Lyons.

## Level and category audit

The target is a descriptive organization of synchronic English grammar. It is specified before the fragment supplies formal rules. Grammatical judgments, annotation categories, coded feature rows and shuffled-partition statistics are kept distinct. The two corpus examples attest sentences; the supplement explicitly denies that their annotations independently validate either taxonomy. The matrix supplement distinguishes a reported decomposition from initialization-sensitive clustering, and inventory comparisons from population inference.

Searches for filler predicates and agency attributed to grammars, corpora or categories revealed no unmarked explanatory mechanism or mereological claim. Inheritance is a formal placement of shared grammatical properties, not a biological or learning mechanism. The argument does not substitute the feature map for an explanation of nounhood. No remaining level drift was identified within this pass's scope.

## Lakoffian metaphor

| Recurring frame | Possible imported entailment | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Categories as containers; inclusion and boundaries | Membership decides every property; boundaries must be primary | The paper explicitly retains lexical and constructional variation, separates primary from subordinate boundaries, and leaves nesting open. Containment states the taxonomic proposal; it does not establish it. |
| Trees, heads, projection and inheritance | Shared organization entails common causal origin or automatic transfer of every permission | The rules and lexical conditions specify the technical senses. The prose expressly blocks unrestricted transfer. Tree links encode syntactic relations, not causal arrows. |
| Economy, costs and reuse | Unique minimum description length; fewer conditions or smaller trees in every case | The local-economy correction is preserved. Both ordinary-Head taxonomies share the phrase-inventory benefit; dependent determinatives gain a Nom layer. No unique numerical minimum is claimed. |
| Fragment and profile | A small comparison stands for the entire grammar; similarity decides rank | Fragment coverage and its exclusions are stated. The hierarchy section denies that profile distance decides rank. |

No accidental mixed frame or prohibited load-bearing metaphor was found. Formal tree vocabulary is appropriate here and was retained.
