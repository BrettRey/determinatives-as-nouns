## Substantive decisions

*Who* remains a pronoun, with positive evidence from *who/whom/whose*. Interrogative and relative *what* are treated as determinatives across dependent and independent constructions, with non-personal interpretation specified for the independent construction. Relative *which* receives the corresponding treatment. The absence of case is not made a sufficient reason to exclude a word from pronoun. Exclamative *what* remains an adjective following CGEL.

The accessible local pro-form-gender versions still classify *what* as a pronoun in places. This revision therefore argues for the reassignment here; it does not attribute an already established anti-pronoun argument to that paper. Its gender account is cited for the distinction between interpretation and lexical classification.

Quantificational common nouns now provide a concrete comparison in both directions. CGEL's *lot* and *plenty* already combine quantity meanings, selected complements, and restricted dependents. Their overlap with determinatives supports the broad comparison without erasing the common-noun/determinative distinction. The text preserves the contrast between *a lot of delegates* and *many of the delegates*, including the exclusion of the corresponding non-partitive *many of delegates*.

Hudson's reciprocal selection argument is addressed directly: both constituents impose conditions, while the phrase-structure account can retain a single Head. The fragment now walks through those two checks. This is an explicit alternative representation of the selection facts, not a claim to have refuted Hudson's entire dependency theory.

Spinillo's article grouping is assessed on her actual discussion of shared restrictions, descriptive content, phonological dependence, modification, and genitive cooccurrence. She explicitly recognizes *every*'s differences from the articles. The revision weighs the dependence-related restrictions together and examines the wider quantificational connections of *a*, including fixed *many a*. It doesn't treat that construction as free modification of ordinary *a*.

The new degree-modifier discussion adds both evidence and a cost. Quantificational NPs already modify adjectives, but determinatives have additional positions. The D-noun account has to preserve the contrast between predicative *a great deal better*, its exclusion in *some a great deal better proposals*, and permitted *much better*. That cost is carried into the final grammatical comparison. *No/none* is correctly treated as a paradigm, and *enough*'s post-head restriction is explicit.

## Reader and argument checks

The paper's progression now runs from the fourfold claim through the profiles and nominal constructions to subcategory rank, difficult members, and the matched grammar. The revised introduction and conclusion agree about the primary contribution. Functions are distinguished from construction types; noun heads from NPs and Nom; pro-form interpretation from pronoun category; independent-use permission from nominal projection; and ordinary determinative headedness from fused-relative syntax.

The comparative argument remains a defended classificatory judgment. Correlated manifestations of independent use are not counted as independent evidence. Distributional separation in the old matrix does not establish a taxonomic rank. Shared formal structure receives equal credit under separate D, while premodifier and degree-modifier restrictions are charged to the broader-Noun proposal. No causal-maintenance or biological-kind claim has been added.
