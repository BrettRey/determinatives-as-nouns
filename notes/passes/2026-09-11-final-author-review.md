## Points requiring interpretation

Your peripheral-modifier suggestion has a real basis. CGEL p. 431 explicitly notes overlap between DP modifiers and peripheral NP modifiers, and uncertainty over some attachments. Under D-noun, *almost* can therefore be analysed as peripheral to the smaller NP *every*, with *almost every* functioning as Det. The existing internal-attachment tree remains one possible representation. The dependent example alone cannot decide between them.

This does not yet justify treating every pre-head modifier as peripheral. In *the very few who objected*, *very* falls below the external determiner. A uniformly peripheral analysis would need another embedded NP or a further structural change. The manuscript now makes that qualification explicit, adds the peripheral slot to the NP schema, and narrows the grammatical cost attributed to adverbial premodification.

The original account of numeral morphology was too vague. Your [numeral paper](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1360674325100518), §5.4, derives fractional nouns from cardinal nouns. That derivation does not independently establish the nounhood of determinative cardinals. Section 5.5 also treats *two thousand and twenty-seventh* as a coordination whose final coordinate is adjectival. The revised discussion preserves both distinctions.

The explanation of *mine* now states CGEL’s motivation. Anaphoric *mine*, understood as “my car”, combines a possessive relation with an understood nominal description. Predicative *mine* can express possession directly, as “belongs to me”, without that description. CGEL allows contextual ambiguity, so the prose no longer implies that every predicative occurrence excludes fusion.

The *who/which/what* section is secondary to the superordinate-Noun argument. I retained a shorter inventory clarification, including your examples and the qualification that no single property is definitive. The abstract no longer presents this boundary revision as a main contribution; exclamative *what* is confined to a footnote.
