# Rhetoric and humour

The main article and both supplements were read after review triage. This pass assesses the present wording; the substantive criterion and comparator questions remain explicitly open in the objection ledger. No further rhetorical rewrite was made.

## Rhetorical figures

The three-way separation of inclusion, ordinary headedness and internal rank is earned: the fragment varies those commitments rather than merely listing them. Its recurrence in the conclusion answers the opening's three questions. The four-way comparison uses a real crossing of two dimensions; its symmetry does not imply equal support or a demonstrated winner.

The three restricted-membership conditions and their matching First/Second/Third application in §6 are deliberate, useful parallelism. Their evidential adequacy is an argument question, especially the different kinds of paradigm invoked. Removing the parallel wording would make that question harder to inspect.

The corrected comparison of fusion and ordinary headedness in §4.4 preserves two positive groupings: few with adjectival fused heads, or few with ordinary nominal heads. It awards neither a spurious economy advantage. The contrasting compound accounts also have a real structural basis: one separates domains through DP–Nom structure and the other assigns their restrictions within Nom.

Several contrasts distinguish a common observation from an inference it cannot alone sustain. Here the alternatives are explicitly represented, so these are not imagined opponents. The introduction and conclusion already identify the strongest separate-D alternative. A general deletion of contrastive clauses would damage the comparison.

The questions opening §§4.4, 5 and 6 organize genuine unresolved choices. The question in §7.3 exposes the central parity argument, but does not itself justify the weighting it proposes. That limitation is recorded in the objection ledger. Sharper phrasing cannot substitute for the broader adjective comparison and an explicit allocation criterion.

No unexpressed rhetorical pattern calls for an added tricolon, chiasmus or antithesis. The existing examples and matched rules give the argument its shape. The source now stops at concrete consequences rather than adding a second conclusion to each paragraph; the earlier scar-tissue and redundancy cuts remain appropriate.

## Humour

No passage performs a joke or a comic attack on an opposing theory. *Enough is enough* and the other familiar expressions are linguistic data. *The idle rich* supplies an adjectival comparison; its social connotation is not offered as a premise. The opening sequence some/me/apple/Brett is concrete and mildly personal, but does not trade on wit to make the classification seem obvious. Nothing needs cutting or an added joke for the Journal of Linguistics audience.
