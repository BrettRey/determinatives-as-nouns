## Test 2: constituency of *hardly anyone present*

**The two groupings.**
- **Fusion** (Payne, Huddleston & Pullum 2007, ex. (13d), p. 582, in `literature/Payne_Huddleston_Pullum_2007_Fusion.md`): *hardly anyone* is a DP filling the fused Det–Head, and *present* is a modifier (the "restrictor") in the nominal outside it. The grouping is **[[hardly anyone] present]**.
- **Ordinary Head** (combined manuscript §4.5): peripheral *hardly* attaches to an NP whose head nominal is *anyone present*. The grouping is **[hardly [anyone present]]**. The manuscript already states this difference: "ordinary Head groups *anyone present* in the inner NP, whereas fusion groups *hardly anyone* in DP". It doesn't test it.

**The diagnostic: one peripheral modifier over two coordinated restricted compounds, against a common-noun control.**

| | Example | Ordinary Head | Fusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) compound | *Hardly anyone present or anything said surprised her.* | *hardly* over coordinated inner NPs *[anyone present] or [anything said]*: ordinary constituent coordination | *anyone present* isn't a constituent (*anyone* is inside the DP, *present* outside it), so this needs non-constituent coordination |
| (b) common-noun control | *Hardly any guest present or any speech given surprised her.* | In the dependent case, *hardly any* is a phrase in Det (§4.4, as in *almost every experienced teacher*), so *any guest present* isn't a constituent here either: non-constituent coordination | Same: *hardly any* is a DP in Det, so non-constituent coordination |

**The prediction that separates them.**
- Ordinary Head predicts (a) better than (b): constituent coordination against non-constituent coordination.
- Fusion predicts (a) and (b) alike, since both are non-constituent.

So the evidence is the *difference* between (a) and (b), not the acceptability of either. This answers Codex's condition that the diagnostic distinguish the constituents independently: non-constituent coordination exists in English (*John gave Mary a book and Sue a pen*), so a raw (a) proves nothing, but the minimal pair holds that factor constant.

**Weak points to settle before collecting data:**
1. Coordinating two quantified compounds under one *hardly* may be odd for semantic reasons that affect (a) and (b) differently, since *any* + noun allows a non-quantificational reading more easily. Several lexical sets are needed: *anyone*/*anything*; *everyone*/*everything* with *almost*; *nothing*/*no one* with *almost*.
2. Is *hardly any guest present* really grouped [[hardly any] [guest present]] on the ordinary-Head account? The manuscript's Figure for *almost every experienced teacher* says yes for dependent uses. Confirm against §4.4 before relying on it.

**Data:**
- **Judgments.** Use the house acceptability instrument (`tools/llm-acceptability-judgments/`; certificate "descriptive_only" as of 2026-08-29, so any external claim needs the certificate stated) for 6–8 minimal pairs across the lexical sets, plus fillers. Brett's own judgments as a check.
- **Corpus (COCA, then iWeb).** Look for attested shared-modifier coordinations of restricted compounds. Searches for Brett:
  1. `almost everyone * and everyone`
  2. `almost everything * and everything`
  3. `hardly anyone * or anyone`
  4. `hardly anyone * or anything`
  5. `nearly everyone * and everything`
  
  Expect sparse results, mostly *almost everyone here and everyone there*-type lists. A few attestations of the (a) type are enough to show it's grammatical; the contrast with (b) needs the judgments.

  **Result (15 Oct):** all five strings returned nothing in COCA (Brett). The corpus gives no evidence either way; the test now depends on judgments.

**Payoff if it works:** a constituency argument for ordinary Head that doesn't depend on licensing conditions at all. That's the kind of evidence a non-CGEL syntactician accepts.

## Test 3: the repairs across the inventory

**The point.** If *the some who* were the only externally determined independent determinative lacking an internal-modifier use, fusion could list it as an exception, as CGEL lists *the both of us*. If the pattern recurs, exceptions stop being a repair and become the rule. Ordinary Head predicts that external determination depends on what the word means after *the*, not on whether it has a modifier use.

**The lead case: *much*.** CGEL p. 393: "Except for *much*, the plain forms can also occur as modifier following a definite determiner: *her many virtues*, *those few outstanding mistakes*, *the little money that remains*." So:
- *the little that remains*: *little* has a modifier use (*the little money*), so fusion licenses a modifier-head. Both analyses allow it.
- *the much that remains*: *much* has no modifier use after a definite determiner (CGEL's own statement), so fusion has no licence. Ordinary Head allows it, since *much* gives a quantity after *the* just as *little* does.

CGEL doesn't discuss independent *the much*: a search of `literature/00-CGEL.md` for "the much" finds only *much* modifying an adjective (*the much weaker*). If *the much that has been written* is attested with the same reading as *the little that remains*, fusion needs a second exception. The structure is the same as with *some*.

**The inventory** (CGEL criterion (a), p. 539: determinatives that "cannot combine with the articles"; *both*, p. 377; *much*, p. 393). Ordinary Head's prediction is that the word occurs after *the* only if it adds something once *the* has identified the set.

| Determinative | Internal-modifier use (CGEL) | Fusion licence after *the* | Ordinary Head expects | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *few*, *many*, *little*, *several*, cardinals | yes | yes | yes | controls |
| *some* | no (p. 539) | no | yes, with an identified set | attested (*the some who*; COCA 5 clear, iWeb 7) |
| *both* | no (p. 377) | CGEL lists *the both of us* as exceptional (%) | yes (dual set) | in CGEL |
| *much* | no after a definite determiner (p. 393) | no | not testable: plain *much* is "largely restricted to non-affirmative contexts" (p. 393), so its absence after *the* is predicted either way | COCA: 0 for `the much that` (Brett), but one literary hit inside the `the little that` lines, licensed by the parallel with *the little* |
| *all* | to check | to check | possible (*the all who*: iWeb, posthaven.com) | COCA: 0 for `the all who` and `the all that`; THE ALL OF 81, of which Brett sent one: *the all of me that's broken* (fiction) |
| *any*, *each*, *every*, *either*, *neither*, *enough*, *no* | no (*enough*: p. 397) | no | unlikely: their meaning (free choice, distributivity, duality, sufficiency, negation) adds nothing after *the* | negative controls. COCA: *the any*, 94 lines, no independent use; *the enough*, 35 lines, 3 conversions ("sufficiency"), no independent use |

**Searches for Brett** (COCA first, then iWeb or NOW if thin; I'll screen the lines):
1. `the much that`, then `the much which`
2. `the little that` (control, frequency and readings)
3. `the both of`
4. `the all who` and `the all that`. Noisy: *the all-*, and *the all that is* used as a name.
5. `the any` and `the enough`, as negative controls; expect noise only.

**Payoff:**
- If *the much that* behaves like *the little that*, the *some* case generalizes. Fusion then needs exceptions for *both*, *some* and *much*, every determinative that takes *the* without a modifier use, while ordinary Head needs none.
- If *the much* is absent, *some* and *both* stay isolated, and the argument rests on them, as now.
- The negative controls matter either way. They show that ordinary Head doesn't predict *the* + any determinative, only those whose meaning survives identification.

## Results so far (15 October)

COCA, run by Brett; screens in `corpus/exclusion_tests/2026-10-15-brett-supplied-the-little-much-any-enough.md`.

1. **The *much* case was a design error.** The row above originally said ordinary Head expects *the much that remains*. It was written from CGEL's "Except for *much*" sentence (p. 393) without the sentence eight lines above it: plain *much* is "largely restricted to non-affirmative contexts". *The much that remains* is affirmative, so its absence follows from polarity under either analysis, and the case can't discriminate. Payoff (b) below applies: the inventory argument rests on *some* and *both*.
2. **The negative controls hold.** *The any* and *the enough* give no independent uses. Their non-noise lines are conversions (*the any symptom definition*, *the enough that defines me*), which name a category or property and don't quantify; with *somes*, they show what conversion looks like, and *the some who* doesn't look like it.
3. **The reason given for *enough* as a control is doubtful.** "Sufficiency adds nothing after *the*" is hard to maintain when *the enough that is already here* can mean "the sufficient amount". The meaning-based criterion for ordinary Head needs restating before the paper uses it.
4. ***Both*:** *the both of* has 1437 COCA hits, 99% with a personal pronoun after *of* (CGEL p. 377 says the partitive is restricted to them). A frequent construction fixed to one frame is what a listed exception is for, so it costs fusion little.
5. ***All*:** one fictional line, *the all of me that's broken*, in the frame of *the some of us who*. Too thin to count.
6. **Outcome.** Payoff (b) holds: the inventory doesn't show that exceptions become the rule. *Some* stays the one productive dissociation. Test 3 survives only as a check that the *some* case isn't an artefact (controls empty; conversions look different), which fits a footnote or the appendix rather than a section.
