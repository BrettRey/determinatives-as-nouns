## New material

**S (structural):**
1. **Answers to the three repairs** (new section, after the *the some who* case): conversion (against: *somes*, label *any*, nominal *enough*, totality *all* name something and don't quantify, unlike *the some who*); a construction-specific rule; a weaker licensing condition.
2. **Noun ellipsis** (new section, replacing the l. 77 footnote): *some [e] who say it*. Inward check first (`lit`), then a light outward search. Payne et al. (2007: 583–584) already discuss null-headed analyses (Larson & Marušič 2004, Leu 2004).
3. ***Once*, *twice*, *thrice*.** Payne, Huddleston & Pullum (2007) is about these three: compound determinatives in fused determiner-head, "analogous in many respects to expressions like *someone* and *somewhere*" (p. 565). The combined manuscript never mentions them. A JL paper titled after theirs has to give the ordinary-Head treatment of their central case, probably in the compounds section.
4. **Introduction rewritten for S:** a fair statement of the fusion analysis and its motivations (Rapoport's rules), the separate D held fixed, and the plan.
5. **Figures redrawn with D.**
6. Test 3 footnote; the premodifier-neutrality point; the qualified grouping sentence.

**T (taxonomic):**
1. The open problem: an observation the separate-category rival fails to predict, beyond parsimony. Without it the comparison rests on the profile and on statement economy.
2. Short background on the two Head analyses (definitions only), so its "holds however a grammar analyses the internal structure" claim has something to refer to.
3. The four-way audit as an appendix (Table 1, Table 5, the coverage audit's four columns, D-noun with fusion).
4. A French abstract if it goes to Corela.
