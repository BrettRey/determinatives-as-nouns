# Fusion of functions revisited: Independent determinatives in English (revised abstract)

Draft, 2026-10-15. Merges the current and NBER-style versions after GPT pro's and GLM-5.3's comments: the NBER order, with the terms that distinguish the hypotheses kept, the category held fixed, and the agreement contrast shown.

*The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language* (*CGEL*) analyses independent determinatives, as in *take some* and *the lucky few*, with fused functions: one word realizes Head together with Determiner or Modifier. Holding *CGEL*'s separate determinative category fixed, I argue that ordinary Head relations do better. Both analyses make the determinative the ultimate head; they differ in the relations in between. On the ordinary analysis, the determinative heads a nominal that heads the noun phrase, so *the lucky few* has the structure of *the lucky survivors*. The discriminating case is *the some who say it*. For determinatives, *CGEL* licenses a fused modifier-head through an internal-modifier use, as *the few mistakes* licenses *the few*, but *some* has no such use, and corpus checks find *the some people* only as typos. Converting *some* to a noun, a construction-specific rule, or a weaker licensing condition would each add a stipulation that ordinary Head avoids. Ordinary Head also lets the complex determinatives *a few* and *many a* be analysed as peripheral *a* and predeterminer *many*, as expansion (*a good few*) and agreement (*a few are*, *many a man is*) suggest. Fusion remains where it does independent work, with adjectives (*the idle rich*).
