# Fusion of functions revisited: Independent determinatives in English (current abstract)

Extracted from the LaTeX draft on 2026-10-15 (245 words).

*The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language* (*CGEL*) analyses independent determinatives, as in *take some*, *some of the wine*, and *the lucky few*, with fused functions: one word realizes Head together with Determiner or Modifier. Holding *CGEL*'s separate determinative category fixed, I argue that ordinary Head relations do better. In bare, partitive, and externally determined uses alike, the determinative heads a nominal that heads the noun phrase, so *the lucky few* has the structure of *the lucky survivors*. For determinatives, *CGEL* ties the fused modifier-head to a use as internal modifier: *the few* is licensed by *the few mistakes*. Attested *the some who* has no such counterpart: *some* has no internal-modifier use, and in the corpus lines checked, the string *the some people* turns up only as typos and a stray *the*. The available repairs (converting *some* to a noun, a construction-specific rule, or a weaker licensing condition) each add a stipulation that ordinary Head avoids. Ordinary Head also lets *CGEL*'s complex determinatives *a few*, *a little*, *a great many*, and *many a* be analysed as peripheral modification by *a* and predeterminer modification by *many*, as their expansion (*a good few*) and agreement (*a few are*, *many a man is*) suggest, and it keeps the modifier contrasts *CGEL* draws in *almost every* and *hardly anyone present*. Fusion remains where it has independent work, with adjectives (*the idle rich*). This isn't the DP hypothesis: a determinative in Det remains a dependent of the noun it determines.
