# Determinatives as nouns in English (current abstract)

Extracted from the LaTeX draft on 2026-10-15 (185 words).

English determinatives (*the*, *some*, *every*, *few*, …) are a fourth subcategory of Noun beside common nouns, proper nouns, and pronouns, not the separate primary category of *The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language* (*CGEL*). *CGEL* already keeps pronouns within Noun despite their differences, as auxiliaries are kept within Verb, and the same comparison of whole profiles places determinatives there too. Connections with nouns recur across the inventory: quantificational common nouns share partitives, number transparency, and restricted dependents with determinative quantifiers, demonstratives inflect for number, and compounds take head genitives, while the adjectival profile of grade and degree modification is confined to four quantifiers. Within Noun, two things the grammar states separately need stating only once: the head genitive, and a secondary-use schema covering proper nouns (*an Ophelia*), personal pronouns (*a he*, *the real me*), compound determinatives (*a special someone*), and cardinals (*in threes*) with no change of category. A separate category matches that coverage only by restating each rule over two categories or by adding a feature that coincides with Noun membership. The classification holds whatever a grammar says about how independent determinatives are headed.
