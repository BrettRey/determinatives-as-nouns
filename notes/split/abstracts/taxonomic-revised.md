Is *the* a noun? English determinatives (*the*, *some*, *every*, *few*) are a fourth subcategory of Noun beside common nouns, proper nouns, and pronouns, not the separate primary category of *The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language* (*CGEL*). *CGEL* already keeps pronouns within Noun despite their differences, as auxiliaries are kept within Verb. Comparing whole profiles, I find links to nouns running through the class. Quantity nouns like *lot* and *number* share partitives, number transparency, and restricted dependents with *some* and *many*; *this* and *that* inflect for number; and *everyone* takes the head genitive. The adjectival profile of grade and degree modification is confined to four quantifiers. Within Noun, one secondary-use schema covers *an Ophelia*, *a he*, *a special someone*, and *in threes* with no change of category, and corpus evidence bears out its prediction for pronouns (*the real me*). A separate category matches this coverage only by restating rules over two categories or by adding a feature that coincides with Noun membership. The classification holds however a grammar analyses the internal structure of these phrases.
