**Review for *Journal of Linguistics*** — reviewer perspective: NP structure and the fusion-of-functions apparatus (Payne, Huddleston & Pullum 2007, 2010; *CGEL* Ch. 5).

*Note on locating the argument:* my standing brief refers to a "§3.1 discriminator argument." In this draft §3.1 is "The existing standard of nounhood"; the three discriminators (paradigm productivity, structural saturation, internal modification) are now dispersed across §4.1–§4.4 and, importantly, are no longer presented as discriminating between analyses. My comments track that redistribution.

---

**1. Main claim.** English determinatives should be included within Noun as a fourth coordinate subcategory alongside common nouns, proper nouns and pronouns, on the same grounds *CGEL* already uses to include pronouns; ordinary nominal headedness for independent determinatives is offered as a separable further proposal.

**2. Strengths.**

(i) The 2×2 fragment in §7.2 (Tables 4–5) is the paper's real methodological contribution. Separating taxonomy (D inside/outside Noun) from headedness (fusion/ordinary Head), holding lexical permissions constant, and then stating plainly that "those benefits don't uniquely favour inclusion within Noun" is more disciplined than most work in this area. The formalisation of use permissions as $f\in U_h \wedge C_h(f,c)$ is clean and does keep structure and lexical restriction apart.

(ii) §6 on the articles is the best-argued section. The three conditions on retaining a defective member — paradigmatic integration, participation in the category's contrasts, locality of the missing use — together with the *my/mine* precedent and the explicit defeater ("a cluster of synchronic restrictions shared by *the*, *a* and *every*, absent elsewhere in Noun") is exactly how such an argument should be framed against Spinillo.

(iii) The paper does not repeat the standard distributional fallacy: §4.1 concedes *the rich* and the comparative cases at the outset, and §4.4 states its own criterion as "a comparative reason, not a necessary-and-sufficient membership test." The two supplements are candid, including the disclaimer that the 2021 matrix contains no common or proper nouns and so cannot test the superordinate category at all.

**3. Substantive weaknesses.**

*(a) The paper's criterion for positing a category node undercuts its own conclusion. Severity: high; repairable.* §5 rejects a Hudson-style intermediate pronoun-plus-determinative node on the ground that "an intermediate pronoun-plus-determinative category would be useful if it collected generalizations otherwise repeated… The intermediate category would need additional generalizations." Apply that criterion one level up. The strongest rival — separate D with ordinary Head, §7.2 — replaces the head restriction with $\mathrm{Head}{:}\{\mathrm{N}_h,\mathrm{D}_h\}$ and states the Nom and NP schemata *once*. Nothing is repeated. So the Noun node, on the paper's own test, collects no generalization the rival fails to state once, and §7.3's "inheritance through Noun versus a projection rule spanning N and D" looks like a choice between a named supertype and an unnamed disjunction. The paper needs either (α) to identify generalizations Noun collects beyond the projection schema — plausible candidates are Det-eligibility of NPs (§7.1), partitive complement licensing (§4.3), the *-one/-body/-thing* compound family (§4.5), and the pro-form gender system of Reynolds 2025 — and show these are stated twice in the rival; or (β) to demote the thesis explicitly to a claim about grammar organisation that the fragment leaves undetermined. As it stands §8's "I favour inheritance" is a preference between notations.

*(b) The pronoun precedent, which carries the taxonomic argument, presupposes the headedness proposal it claims to be independent of. Severity: high; repairable.* §1 and §3.1 rest the case on *CGEL*'s justification of pronoun membership "by the functions of pronoun-headed phrases." But under nominal-D-with-fusion — a cell the paper retains as viable (Table 5, row 4) — a determinative never heads an NP in argument function; a DP (or NP$_D$) realises Det–Head *within* a Nom. One can reply that the fused constituent does bear Head of Nom, so a determinative-headed projection does have nominal functions; but that reply is not made, and it requires a formulation of the pronoun rationale under which the head of an NP need not be an N. Until §3.1 states the rationale in a form testable across all four cells and reports the result, the abstract's "Ordinary headedness is a further proposal" is not sustained: the taxonomic argument appears parasitic on it.

*(c) Fusion is engaged too thinly for a paper proposing to displace it across a category. Severity: moderate–high.* §4.2 compresses the 2007 account to "one constituent jointly realizes Head and a dependent function, subject to structural and adjacency conditions," and never enumerates or tests those conditions. (I have not re-read the 2007 paper for this review, so I put this as a checkable demand rather than a claim about its content: the author should list the conditions the analysis imposes and show, construction by construction, what ordinary headedness preserves and what it must restate.) Two consequences are visible in this draft. First, Table 3 and §4.4: the claim that ordinary headedness "unifies *few* across its bare and externally determined uses, without alternating Det–Head and Mod–Head" understates the rival. The paper accepts *CGEL*'s Mod analysis of *few* in *the few mistakes*; so under ordinary Head, *few* is Det (*few survivors*), Mod (*the few mistakes*) and Head (bare *few*; *the lucky few*) — three unrelated functions — whereas fusion derives Det–Head and Mod–Head *from* the two dependent functions. The trade-off is not "one alternation versus none," and the comparison should be stated symmetrically. Second, §4.5/Figure 6: the DP–Nom boundary does not merely *separate* the modifier domains, it constrains order and constituency. The flat Nom on the left of Figure 6 stipulates the linearisation and must add a separate condition excluding pre-head AdjP; the author should say what blocks \**hardly experienced anyone* and \**anyone hardly*, and whether $\mathrm{Mod_{pre}}$ must now be subdivided by head subcategory — i.e. whether the DP/Nom distinction returns under another name.

*Smaller required items.* "Productivity" (§4.1) is redefined as "the availability of independent use among established members," which restates the datum rather than testing it, and the corpus supplement disclaims productivity estimation; either ground the term or drop it. *The lucky few*, which carries §4.4 and Table 3, has no bare-*few* attestation in the author's own corpus and may be semi-lexicalized (cf. *the happy few*); widen the evidence base (*the many who did*, *the other two*, *these few here*). Finally, in the audit supplement, re-executing the supplied scripts to "reproduce their outputs byte for byte" establishes determinism, not independent verification; the wording should be corrected, and the status of the unposted correction notice stated in the article, not only the supplement.

**4. Key question.** What generalization does the Noun node collect that $\mathrm{Head}{:}\{\mathrm{N}_h,\mathrm{D}_h\}$ fails to state once — and why does the answer not equally justify the intermediate category rejected in §5?

**5. Recommendation: Revise and Resubmit.** The thesis is serious and the fragment methodology is worth publishing, but the taxonomic conclusion is currently unsupported by the paper's own stated criterion and the engagement with fusion is too compressed for this journal.

*Required repairs:* (a) and (b) above, plus the symmetrical restatement of the *few* comparison, the ordering/exclusion facts in §4.5, the "productivity" terminology, and the supplement wording. *Optional extensions:* coordination diagnostics (the corpus records two Coordinate D nodes) as a possible empirical discriminator; an ellipsis/anaphora comparison of *I'll take some* with *the red ones*; and pursuit of the Van Eynde-style agreement evidence beyond English.
