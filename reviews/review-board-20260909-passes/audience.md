**Informal report to a colleague — Reynolds, "Determinatives as nouns in English" (draft, Sept 2026), plus matrix-audit and CGELBank supplements**

Ten-second impression: the title and abstract promise a CGEL-framework argument for putting determinatives inside Noun, with a matched fragment and acknowledged limits on the coordinate-vs-nested question. That looks exactly like the kind of source a short account of English noun classification needs. Sixty-second skim: the alternatives table, the pronoun-precedent move, the fragment with four cross-classified accounts, and the supplements on the corpus counts and the 2021 matrix reanalysis.

**(a) Main claim — ANSWERED.** English determinatives (the, a, this, some, every, many…) belong in Noun, alongside common nouns, proper nouns and pronouns — preferably as a fourth coordinate subcategory. Two further points are carefully separated: ordinary headedness (determinatives head their NPs, contra CGEL's Det–Head fusion) is a separate proposal that even a separate-D grammar could adopt; and the coordinate arrangement is provisional, since the fragment's judgments don't distinguish it from Hudson-style nesting (determinative within pronoun within Noun).

**(b) Most useful evidence — ANSWERED.** Three converging construction families: (i) widespread independent use of determinatives in argument positions (Some left, I see some), with an attested CGELBank example (test-drive both back to back); (ii) partitives (some of the wine), showing wine can't head the whole expression and some plays the head-like role; (iii) modification profiles (almost every, hardly any, the lucky few, hardly anyone present with its post-head restrictor). Also the CGEL precedent: pronouns and even dependent genitives like my are already admitted to Noun on comparable functional grounds, so differences from common nouns can't be an exclusion test. The matched fragment then shows all four taxonomic/headedness combinations cover the data — honesty about what's decided vs not.

**(c) Strongest alternative — ANSWERED.** Two candidates are clearly characterized. CGEL itself: determinative as a separate primary category, independent some via Det–Head fusion (using machinery already needed for adjectival the idle rich and compounds like hardly anyone present, where fusion neatly separates modifier domains). And Hudson: determiners inside pronoun inside Noun. The paper's answer to CGEL is that ordinary headedness unifies bare, partitive and externally-determined uses under one Head relation; to Hudson, that no extra generalizations are captured by the intermediate category — but it concedes the fragment can't discriminate.

**(d) What classification choice it settles — PARTLY, deliberately.** It settles: determinatives are nominal (in Noun) rather than a primary category coordinate with Noun — argued via the pronoun precedent and the inheritance-vs-cross-category-projection economy comparison. It explicitly does not settle: coordinate vs nested subcategories, and ordinary headedness vs fusion. For my purposes the settled part (determinatives are nouns) is the citable core.

**(e) Where corpus and old matrix matter — ANSWERED.** CGELBank supplies inspectable attestations: 220 trees, 387 D nodes, with 65 Det–Head occurrences individually reviewed and a per-form function table. Crucially the documentation is candid: the labels encode the very analysis under debate, so the corpus illustrates constructions but can't confirm them; gaps (no bare few, either, neither) don't establish ungrammaticality. The matrix audit is a correction trail on Reynolds 2021: 232 reported features vs 155 in the public file, a dropped feature in the published clustering code, seed sensitivity (129 vs 125/138 matches), and a clear statement that even perfect determinative/pronoun separation is compatible with shared nounhood — the matrix can't test rank within Noun since it has no common or proper nouns. This is why §6 can't settle (d)'s open question.

**Verdict:** I would cite it, specifically for the proposition that English determinatives are best treated as a subcategory of Noun within CGEL-style grammar, with the caveat (which the paper itself states) that their exact position within Noun — coordinate with, or nested under, pronoun — remains open, and that ordinary NP headedness is an independent, separable proposal. I would not cite it as evidence that the determinative/pronoun distinction is empirically robust at the matrix level, given the audit's own correction record.
