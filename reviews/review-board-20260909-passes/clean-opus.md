## Reader's assessment: "Determinatives as nouns in English"

### The headline problem: a thesis the paper declines to defend

This is a careful, well-read, honest paper that argues itself to a standstill. The claim in the title and abstract is that English determinatives belong within Noun. But by the end of §7 the paper has conceded (i) that a separate-D grammar can share nominal projection and reduce the phrase-type inventory just as well (§7.2), (ii) that the fragment's judgments don't distinguish coordinate from nested subcategories (§5, §7.2), (iii) that fusion and ordinary headedness both cover every construction examined (§4.6), and (iv) that the fragment doesn't "prove a unique minimum description length" (§7.3). What survives is a preference. A JoL reader will reasonably ask what fact about English is explained by D-noun and left unexplained by CGEL, and the paper's own answer is: none identified.

I don't think the honesty is the problem — the hedging is admirable, and the paper is much better for separating taxonomy from headedness. The problem is that the honesty has not been converted into a position. You have two viable routes. Either (a) find a genuinely differential prediction and lead with it, or (b) reframe openly as a paper about *criteria* — arguing that CGEL's own membership rationale, applied consistently, entails inclusion, and that consistency of criteria is itself the finding. Route (b) is defensible and is close to what §7.3 actually does, but it requires the paper to stop presenting itself as an empirical argument and to defend a metric for allocating generalizations. At present it does neither wholeheartedly.

### The logical core is under-argued

The best thing in the paper is the two-step structure that emerges in §6, and it should be made explicit and central: determinatives *as a category* meet CGEL's distributional grounds for nounhood; *the*, *a*, *every* are integrated members of that category; therefore they are nouns. That is inheritance-by-membership, and it has real consequences the paper never confronts. If category membership can be inherited by items that fail the entry criterion, then the criterion for Noun is no longer distributional — it is "belongs to a category most of whose members are distributionally nominal." That is a substantive theoretical commitment, and it is the one thing here that would genuinely interest JoL's readership. It needs a section, not the current three-condition checklist. It also needs a defence against the obvious rejoinder: the same reasoning would let a single independently-usable member drag any closed class into Noun.

Relatedly, §3.1 never states the CGEL criterion precisely enough to be applied. It is glossed as "the phrases they head have the functions of common- and proper-noun-headed phrases," but §4.1 immediately shows this is insufficient (*the rich*, *the most important of her criticisms*). The paper then substitutes a vaguer "constructional profile." Nail down the criterion in §3.1 and hold every later section to it.

### Missing evidence that could actually discriminate

The empirical base is thinner than the topic warrants, and several classic tests are simply absent:

- **Coordination.** Nowhere in the article. Can a determinative-headed phrase coordinate with a genitive NP in Det (\*_Kim's and every book_)? Like-category coordination is the standard argument, and your own 2021 matrix apparently coded a coordination feature. This is the most conspicuous gap.
- **Agreement in partitives.** *Some of the students **are** here* / *Some of the wine **is*** — if *some* is the syntactic head (Figure 5), agreement tracks the embedded complement, not the head. That is a prima facie problem for ordinary headedness and deserves discussion rather than silence.
- **Nominal morphology.** \*_some's_ vs *someone's*; \*_thes_. Under inheritance, why don't determinative nouns inflect? "Local restriction" is available but should be shown to be non-vacuous.
- **Blocking derivations.** What excludes \*_the some apples_, \*_the the apple_? Under the fragment's rules, Det is optional inside every NP, so these must be filtered by lexical conditions the paper never states.
- **The corpus is barely used.** The supplement documents 220 trees, 387 D nodes and 65 inspected Det–Head contexts, and the article extracts two sentences. Either mine it (which lexemes attest independent use? what modifier profiles occur?) or demote the supplement.

### The fragment doesn't do its job

§7.2 is meant to be the technical payoff, but all the discriminating content is hidden inside $C_h(f,c)$, "checks the lexical and constructional conditions in context." Since the entire comparison is about *where restrictions are stated*, an unanalysed condition function guarantees the comparison cannot be decided. Spell $C_h$ out for the seven forms in Table 6 and the four constructions, so a reader can count statements under each of the four accounts. If that can't be done in the space available, cut the fragment to a page and argue in prose — the current version has the appearance of formalization without its benefits.

Two smaller structural points: the phrase-type economy argument (§7.1) belongs to *headedness*, not taxonomy, since §7.2 concedes separate-D achieves it too — presenting it under the taxonomic thesis overstates the case. And the coordinate-vs-nested choice, admitted to be undetermined, should not be in the abstract as a preference.

### Supplements, self-citation, apparatus

The matrix audit is scrupulous and I respect it, but its role here is peculiar: it appears only in a footnote, and its content is that the author's own quantitative work is fragile and its public file misreported. Say this in the body, plainly, and state that no quantitative evidence bears on the thesis. More seriously, the audit records a correction notice *prepared but not posted*. Publishing an article that footnotes an unposted correction to a live public dataset is not tenable; post it first.

Four self-citations, two unpublished, with substantive moves in §5 and §7.3 conditional on them. Reduce the dependence or make those sections self-contained.

Engagement with the DP literature is too thin for JoL: Abney and Bruening get a sentence each; there is nothing on Baker (2003), who is the obvious antagonist for any claim that shared projection settles category membership, and nothing on the partitive literature despite §4.3.

### Presentation

The prose is abstract and heavily nominalized, with a great deal of meta-commentary about what each section will compare. There are essentially two numbered examples. Triple the numbered, judged data; cut a third of the signposting. The five-model AI acknowledgement should be reduced to a policy-conforming sentence.

**Verdict:** a genuinely thoughtful paper that needs to decide what it is arguing, formalize the one thing it claims is decisive, and bring coordination and agreement data to bear.
