## 1. §5.5 secondary-use paragraph and shared-rules recap — AGREE

The paragraph "The grouping has a further consequence" restates §4.6, and less fully: it omits cardinals, which §4.6 includes. Its one limiting sentence (CGEL's categories force a category change; separate D can share the schema) is already covered by §4.6's closing paragraph and §5.5's feature paragraph.

The recap partly duplicates §5.5's second paragraph. It does carry two things that must survive:
- the four rules, each with its section pointer;
- the qualification that these rules are available under either ordinary-Head taxonomy.

**Keep:**
- the feature-alternative paragraph;
- "doesn't establish a strict description-length advantage";
- "additional consequences, not tests set in advance";
- the final reversal-condition paragraph.

**Replace the "further consequence" paragraph with:**
> "The secondary set-denoting use of §\ref{sec:secondary-use} is one such consequence: within Noun one secondary use covers names, compound determinatives, and cardinals, whereas with \textit{CGEL}'s categories the compound and cardinal inputs change category; a separate-D account can share the schema as just described."

**Compress the recap to:**
> "Several rules \textit{CGEL} states separately for nouns and determinatives can likewise be stated once: number transparency for \mention{lot}, \mention{number}, and \mention{some} (§\ref{sec:quant-nouns}); NP and determinative degree modifiers of adjectives (§\ref{sec:enough}); peripheral attachment in \mention{almost every} and \mention{only you} (§\ref{sec:payne}); and the head genitive in \mention{everyone's} and \mention{Edward's} (§\ref{sec:inflection}). The complex determinatives reduce to peripheral \mention{a} and predeterminer \mention{many} (§\ref{sec:complex-determinatives}). These generalizations are available under either ordinary-Head taxonomy; the proposed account states their domain through Noun membership, the separate-D alternative through the inherited feature, and the profile of §\ref{sec:proposal} supplies the case for lexical-category status."

Do not strengthen "stated once" beyond what the original claimed.

### 2b. Admissibility formula and glossary — AGREE, with replacement

The formula mostly translates the three-check prose. It does add three things the prose lacks:
- the biconditional, so the checks are sufficient as well as necessary;
- indexing of permissions to the lexical head's lexeme;
- the possibility that checks consult the surrounding structure, including the target.

**Replace the formula and glossary with:**
> "An occurrence is admissible in an external function just when all three checks succeed. Permissions are indexed to the lexeme of the phrase's lexical head, and the form and condition checks may consult the surrounding structure, including the target: independent \mention{none} is selected in \mention{none of the students}, and \mention{apples} is the target for \mention{some}'s phrase in \mention{some apples}."
