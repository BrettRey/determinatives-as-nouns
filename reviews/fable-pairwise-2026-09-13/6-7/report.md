# Pairwise review: §6 Coordinate or nested subcategories × §7 Conclusion

§6 settles the residual placement question (coordinate vs. Hudson-style nesting under pronoun) and deliberately detaches it from the main package; §7 summarizes the whole paper and gives §6 one closing sentence, so the relationship is summary-to-source with no dependency running the other way.

## Findings

**1. CONTEXT CHECK — pro-form gender is conceded at 814 in a way that may undercut 826.**
§6, 814: "pro-form gender extends to non-nominal expressions" is used to show gender doesn't isolate a pronoun+determinative node. But a property that reaches non-nominal expressions also can't isolate Noun, and §7, 826 claims "partial inflectional corroboration" for the nominal grouping. Whether this is a problem depends on §sec:inflection (§2): if the inflectional corroboration there rests on number (cf. abstract, "Number and referential contrasts"), 814 is harmless; if it rests on pro-form gender, §6's own concession weakens §7's summary. Check §2; if gender carries weight there, either narrow 826 to number or repeat the 814 concession where the evidence is introduced.

**2. QUALIFIED CONCERN — the stated basis for the coordinate preference shifts between 822 and 832.**
§6, 822: "The coordinate preference rests on the more specific profile"; the definite phrase most naturally picks up 820's "their more specific patterns remain distinct: person, case, and reflexivity ... quantification, determination, and degree." §7, 832 instead grounds the preference in a negative: "the comparisons here don't establish the additional shared rules that nesting would express." These aren't contradictory, but the premise §6 names is the weaker one on Hudson's own terms: under nesting, personal pronouns and determiners are sister subtypes of a broader pronoun, so distinct organizing principles are expected and don't discriminate; only the absence of P+D-specific shared rules does (which 820's final sentence and 832 state correctly). Also, "the pronoun system" at 820 is really the personal-pronoun subsystem; CGEL's interrogative/relative and reciprocal pronouns aren't organized by person or reflexivity. Repair: reword 822 to match 832 (e.g., "rests on the profile comparisons above, which turn up no pronoun–determinative rules that a further node would carry"), and qualify 820 to "personal pronoun."

**3. QUALIFIED CONCERN — "degree" at 820 vs. "four gradable quantifiers" at 826.**
§6, 820 says "degree organize[s] much of the determinative system"; §7, 826 restricts the grade-based profile to "the four gradable quantifiers," and 826 also lists "degree uses" among the *nominal* connections. If "degree" at 820 means degree modification generally (cf. 810, "accepting *almost*"), the claim is fine but ambiguous; if it means grade, "much of" overstates. One clarifying word at 820 ("degree modification") resolves it.

**4. CONTEXT CHECK — §7 adds a nominal connection the abstract lacks.**
§7, 826 credits quantificational common nouns with "selected complements, number transparency, restricted dependents, and degree uses"; the abstract (0029) lists only the first three. Verify that §2 actually argues degree uses of quantificational nouns as a nominal connection; otherwise 826 quietly enlarges the positive case at the point of summary.

**5. CONTEXT CHECK — labels and a within-section agenda.**
822's "§sec:det-uniform" should resolve to the fragment section (§5, 614–798), given "the fragment's structures." Separately, 806 sets a three-part comparison agenda (modifier selection, grade, quantitative meanings); §6 discharges modifier selection at 816–818 but treats grade and quantity only by assertion at 820. If §2 supplies those comparisons, a pointer at 820 would close the loop; if not, 806 promises more than the section delivers.

**6. Minor — abstract vs. 822/832.** The abstract's "alongside common nouns, proper nouns, and pronouns" reads as committing to coordinate rank, whereas 822 ("leaves this further choice open") and 832 ("a further preference") detach it from the proposal. Consider "within Noun, with ..." in the abstract.
