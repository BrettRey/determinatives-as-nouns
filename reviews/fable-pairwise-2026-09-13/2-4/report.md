# Pair review: §2 (Grounds for a broader Noun category) × §4 (Ordinary Head, fusion, and modifier attachment)

§2 argues that determinatives belong within Noun by comparing their functional, complementation, inflectional, and referential profiles with the established noun subcategories; §4 pairs that categorization with ordinary Head (rather than fusion) and works out the modifier attachments that §2.4 and Table 2 presuppose, with §2 forward-referencing §4 (0095, 0130, 0253, 0285) and §4 back-referencing §2 (0534, 0598, 0600).

## Findings

### 1. The ordering datum that "guides" §4.4 gets no ordinary-Head structure, and §2's description of it doesn't match §4's treatment of `very`
- **Status:** DEMONSTRATED as a missing link; the analytic resolution is a QUALIFIED CONCERN.
- **§2:** 0249 — "Here `a` belongs to the complex determinative; `very` occurs within it and `quite` outside it." **§4:** 0534 — "The selection and ordering contrast in §2.4 guides the attachment analysis"; 0558 — degree `very` "modifies the Nom headed by `few`"; 0474 — in `the few`, "`the` fills Det"; 0524–0526 — ordinary Head "unifies bare and externally determined `few`", "the uniform treatment of `few`".
- **Gap:** §2's only ordering datum locates `very` *inside a complex determinative*; §4's machinery attaches `very` inside Nom below a separate Det. Applied to `a very few`, that yields [NP [Det a] [Nom [Mod very] [Head few]]] with `quite` NP-peripheral (the fig:every/fig:few configuration), i.e. `a` is a Det, not part of a lexical item. §4 instead illustrates with `the very few people/who objected` and `the very many` and never returns to `a very few`. The "uniform treatment of `few`" is also quietly limited: on §2's wording `few` in `a few` belongs to a different lexeme.
- **Why it matters:** This is the one point where §4 says §2 supplies its evidence. A reader can't tell whether D-noun keeps CGEL's complex determinative (then Nom-level attachment can't express a modifier "within" it) or dissolves it into Det + Head (then something must preserve whatever contrast motivates CGEL's complex-determinative status and its restriction to `few`/`little`).
- **Repair:** After 0558, one or two sentences giving the ordinary-Head structure of `a very few` and the status of `a few`; if it stays a complex determinative, reword 0249 so `very` isn't "within" it. CONTEXT CHECK: §3 (restricted members) or §5 (sec:det-uniform) may already cover complex determinatives.

### 2. Cardinal examples carry weight in §2 that §4 itself withdraws
- **Status:** QUALIFIED CONCERN (+ CONTEXT CHECK)
- **§2:** 0128 `two that I have seen` (relative on a determinative); Table 2 row 0296 "Accepts determination: `these three`"; 0251 and row 0299 `almost ten` as peripheral modification of a determinative. **§4:** 0420 `almost ten` as the exemplar "plain determinative-headed NP"; 0528 — "It doesn't independently decide whether `three` is determinative or has a common-noun use"; 0564 — the internal approximative permission exists only for "externally determined independent cardinals" (`the almost thirty who came`).
- **Gap:** If cardinals have both uses (0528), `these three`, `two that…`, and `almost ten` don't show which use is present, so they don't independently support Table 2's determinative rows. Conversely, since the only internal approximative permission is cardinal-bound, and 0562 already grants internal `almost` on nouns (`an almost textbook case`), a common-noun reading of `the almost thirty` would reduce §4.4's residual determinative-internal AdvP permissions to the degree series — a simplification the text doesn't note. Table 2 (0297/0299) omits the 0564 permission despite 0285's "explained in §sec:payne".
- **Why it matters:** The same items shift evidential status between sections, and Table 2 is the weighing instrument.
- **Repair:** Prefer non-cardinal exemplars in §2 (CGEL's `these few here`, cited at 0476; `several that I saw`) or mark cardinal items as conditional on reynolds2026numerals; either add the 0564 permission to Table 2 or note there that internal approximatives arise only on cardinals. CONTEXT CHECK: §5 (sec:costs) for the cardinal analysis.
