# Pair review: §2 "Grounds for a broader Noun category" × §6 "Coordinate or nested subcategories"

§2 assembles the profile comparison that puts determinatives inside Noun (culminating in Table `tab:existing`, 287–308), and §6 takes that result as given and argues that determinative should be a fourth coordinate subcategory rather than nested under pronoun; §6 therefore depends on §2's table and on §2's subgroup qualifications, without §2 depending on §6.

## Findings (priority order)

**1. QUALIFIED CONCERN — §6's coordinate preference is stated negatively although §2 supplies positive evidence against an intermediate pronoun node.**
§6 concludes only that the comparisons "don't establish a further shared set of grammatical rules that requires it" (820), and at 806 says extending the pronoun analysis "requires comparing its modifier selection, grade, and quantitative meanings with the existing pronouns' properties" — a comparison never then applied. Yet §2 already records properties that determinatives share with (quantificational) common nouns but not with pronouns: partitive *of*-complementation and number transparency (134–140), and Table row 298 (Pronoun "Normally absent" vs Determinative "Partitive *of*-PPs; comparative *than*-phrases"); Det filled by NPs headed by all three existing groups (114–120). On §6's own criterion (808: whether shared properties "warrant another level"), a nesting under pronoun would have determinatives inherit "complementation normally absent" and override it, while their closest kin (abstract, 29; 134) sit outside the intermediate node. Whether such overrides are costly is a judgment, so this is a missing link rather than a contradiction. Repair: one or two sentences at 820 citing §2.2 and row 298.

**2. QUALIFIED CONCERN — §6 doesn't engage the rows of §2's table where pronoun and determinative pattern together against common and proper nouns.**
Row 302 opposes "gender-sensitive forms" (pronoun, determinative) to referent construal (common, proper); row 304 has interrogative/relative forms carried by the head for pronoun (*who*) and determinative (*which*, *what*) but "through another constituent" for the other two (also 279); row 305 has both closed; row 301 shares deixis and interrogation. §2's footnote at 277 leaves the pronoun/determinative boundary open for *what*/*which*. §6 addresses only context-dependence and pro-form gender (814) and determination/modification (816–818), and its rebuttal that pro-form gender "extends to non-nominal expressions" doesn't touch the within-Noun asymmetry §2's own table records. Since 808 defines the test as generalizations applying to the two groups together, 820's "don't establish" is asserted without meeting the paper's own candidates. Repair: address each briefly (e.g., closed inventory isn't a rule; gender-sensitive determinative forms are the compounds, whose nominal properties §2 already attributes to the nominal component at 271; interrogative forms distribute across both groups without a shared rule), or soften 820.

**3. DEMONSTRATED — evidential scope mismatch on modification.**
818: "Their adverbial modifier patterns differ from the restricted adjectival pattern of personal pronouns," a class-level contrast. §2 confines the degree series to four items (247; 297 "Degree AdvP on four quantifiers"; 314 "a narrower group"), treats approximatives as peripheral with pronoun counterparts (251; 299 "*only you*"), and lists adjectival modification on determinatives too (297 "*the lucky few*"; 130 "*something reliable*"), which parallels *poor old me* (816). §2 at 267 explicitly refuses to let a subgroup property distinguish "whole categories." Repair: rest 818 on determination (296) and on the four gradable quantifiers explicitly.

**4. DEMONSTRATED (minor) — "determiner" used as a category label at 804** ("a determiner is a pronoun whose valency…"), whereas the paper reserves it for the function (118; footnote at 114, "Det"). A gloss that this is Hudson's word-class term would avoid confusing exactly the audience the paper addresses.

**5. CONTEXT CHECK.** (a) 822 cites §`sec:det-uniform` for both inheritance paths supplying the fragment; confirm §5 states no rule at a pronoun node. (b) 253 "available under either ordinary-Head taxonomy": check §4 (`sec:payne`) for what the two taxonomies are; if coordinate vs nested, the reference belongs to §6. (c) Check whether Appendix A already presents Hudson (802–806) to avoid duplication.
