# Pairwise review: §5 (The matched fragment and comparative costs) – §7 (Conclusion)

§5 builds the shared constraint fragment and compares the four categorization/Head implementations, favouring D-noun with ordinary Head; §7 restates the whole paper's case, and its third and fourth paragraphs (830, 832) directly recapitulate §5's structural and cost findings, so the pair is summary-to-source rather than a dependency.

No epistemic, authorization, coordination, or oversight risk is evident in this packet.

## Findings

### 1. QUALIFIED CONCERN — §7 hardens §5's provisional cost verdict
- §5 760: "don't establish unrestricted attributive use", "require a closer comparison of lexical, constructional, and register conditions under all four implementations", "doesn't **yet** establish an added cost, or a corresponding advantage for retaining DP".
- §7 832: "Nominal degree modifiers already occur in attributive AdjPs, so their distribution doesn't establish an added cost of NP projection."
- The wording difference is visible: §7 drops "yet", the pending comparison, and the reciprocal DP clause. It also places the point in the paragraph about "the package", although §5 730/760 and Table rows 774–777 make it a property of all three NP-projecting implementations, including separate D with ordinary Head, so it doesn't discriminate for D-noun.
- Its weight is a judgment, but a reader of §7 alone takes as settled what §5 presents as pending, and may read it as a point for the package.
- Repair: "on the present evidence, doesn't establish an added cost of NP projection on any NP-projecting implementation".

### 2. QUALIFIED CONCERN — §5's distributional test is run only for adjectives; §7 inherits
- §5 730 sets the criterion: cost "if their distribution differed systematically from that of degree determinatives". §5 741 lists *enough*/*much*/*little*/*no* modifying "adjectives, adverbs, verbs, and some PPs" and asserts "These permissions must survive recategorization"; the NP parallels supplied (743–757) are all adjective-internal. §7 832 prudently scopes to "attributive AdjPs" but concludes about "their distribution".
- Not a contradiction: 741's own principle (permissions are lexical; cf. 622 "Noun membership alone grants none") arguably makes the test moot, but then 730's criterion is stronger than what §5 discharges.
- Repair: add one NP parallel per function in §5 (verb/adverb modification by *a lot*, *a bit*), or narrow 730 to adjective modification.

### 3. CONTEXT CHECK (needs §6 = sec:inheritance, and sec:form-selection) — *no/none* ~ *my/mine* vs "no additional shared rules"
- §5 647 and Table rows 637–640: "The no/none and my/mine pairs distinguish forms within a paradigm" — one Form mechanism (658) with non-trivial instances only in a determinative and a pronoun; common nouns show no dependent/independent form alternation.
- §7 832: "the comparisons here don't establish the additional shared rules that nesting would express."
- If form selection is stated lexeme-by-lexeme, §7 stands; if sec:form-selection or §6 treats the alternation as a class-level regularity, §5's own Table supplies a candidate determinative–pronoun shared rule that §7 says wasn't found. Check whether §6 addresses this pair; if not, one sentence at 647 saying the alternation is stated per paradigm closes it.

### 4. QUALIFIED CONCERN (exposition) — §7 reports NP projection's cost side but not its benefit or defeater
- §5 665–676 present Det: {NP, PP} vs CGEL's {DP, NP, PP} as the fragment's positive consolidation; §5 798 states when "the shared-projection proposal … loses its advantage".
- §7 830–832 recap Head-alone structure and the degree-use check but never mention shared projection of determinative-headed and genitive Det phrases or 798's condition. NP projection is defended in §7 but never motivated.
- Repair: one clause in 830, e.g. "and lets determinative-headed and genitive determiners share the NP category".

### 5. CONTEXT CHECK (needs §2 = sec:quant-nouns) — "degree uses" in the quantificational-noun list
- §7 826 lists "selected complements, number transparency, restricted dependents, and degree uses"; the abstract (0029) lists only the first three. Within the pair, degree uses attach to the quantificational-noun comparison only at §5 743. Check that §2 includes them; otherwise align abstract and §7.
