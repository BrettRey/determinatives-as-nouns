# Pair review: §2 (Grounds for a broader Noun category) × §5 (The matched fragment and comparative costs)

§2 builds the profile argument that determinatives belong within Noun (nominal connections weighed against the four-quantifier adjectival profile); §5 fixes a matched fragment across four implementations and argues D-noun with ordinary Head adds no cost, with its final preference (796) resting on §2's profile.

## Findings

### 1. QUALIFIED CONCERN — §5 twice treats structural alignment as evidence the pair elsewhere disclaims
- §5 line 723: "under D-noun they follow from Noun membership … I take that alignment to favour D-noun." Against: §5 line 689 "Their agreement on phrase structure leaves the lexical grouping open to the profile argument"; line 796 "Reducing the number of primary categories alone wouldn't establish the preference"; §2 lines 108 and 255 ("External functions alone therefore don't establish nounhood"). Line 723's "follow from Noun membership" also jars with line 622 "Noun membership alone grants none of these permissions" (structures vs permissions is a real distinction, but the reader must supply it).
- Why it matters: the paper's declared logic (abstract; §2 line 314; §5 line 796) is that the profile decides and the structures fit. Line 723 reads as an independent structural argument for the category, which §2's own caveats would undercut.
- Repair: reword 723 so the alignment removes an objection rather than supplies evidence, and say explicitly that Cat(h)=N vs Cat(h)∈{N,D} (684–685) is settled by §2.

### 2. QUALIFIED CONCERN + CONTEXT CHECK — §2.3's explanatory demand is not discharged or assigned in §5
- §2 line 241: "The controls sharpen what the broader Noun proposal has to explain"; line 312: "the proposed ordering … remains to be tested." §2 lines 110 and 237 make article-free independent use the local contrast separating `some` from fused `the rich`; Table tab:quant-controls then attests article-free `numerous were injured` (192), `countless were left…` (204), `there are countless` (226).
- §5 fixes "The inventory, constructions, and readings" (764) and retains "adjectival fusion" in all four implementations (768), but never says how bare `numerous`/`multiple`/`countless` are analysed or that they are a shared cost.
- Why it matters: §2 invests a table and ~20 footnoted attestations in these words; §5 is where the proposal's grammar and costs are stated. The reader can't tell whether the attestations threaten the boundary the fragment presupposes.
- Check §3 and §6. If unhandled, one sentence in §5.4: the quantity adjectives remain adjectival with fusion under all four accounts, so their article-free uses are a shared, not differential, cost.

### 3. QUALIFIED CONCERN (low) — category-level "availability" vs lexeme-level permissions
- §2 line 93: independent use concerns "availability … across a lexical category"; line 231 states the aim of implications that "reduce the permissions needing separate statement." §5 line 622: "Noun membership alone grants none of these permissions"; Table tab:permissions excludes five of nine forms from Subj/Obj lexeme by lexeme.
- Compatible (703 shows common nouns also carry lexical conditions), but §5 never notes that no implementation derives an implication, so §2's stated aim silently lapses.
- Repair: one sentence in §5.1 that permissions are stated per lexeme under all four accounts and §2's generalization is descriptive.

### 4. Minor — degree `enough`
- §2 line 124 and Table line 300 call `I hadn't prepared enough` an adjunct; §5 line 741 says `enough` "modifies … verbs." Function vs relation; align to "adjunct in clause structure."

### 5. CONTEXT CHECK — Subject–Det
- §2 line 114 fn drops CGEL's Subject–Det "here" without argument; §5 line 647 scopes out subordinate-clause genitives, the likeliest test. Neither points to a defence. Check §4/Appendix A; the §5 category comparison (665) holds regardless of function label, so a pointer suffices.

- Category and Head questions stay separate: §2 line 95 defers internal structure; §5 line 689 leaves grouping to the profile; both match the abstract's "Neither choice requires the other."
- §2 line 142's promise (degree modifiers "including their attributive distribution") is discharged at §5 lines 743–760, which cross-refers back (743). Shared CGEL 549–550 examples (142/743), `a very few` (249/699), and approximatives (251–253/697) are preview-then-development, not waste.
- Table tab:permissions matches §2: `some` plural-count/non-count target (635 vs 136–138); `few` plural count, `the few` (649 vs 296); no independent `every`/articles (701 vs 93, 152); `a few` complex determinative (699 vs 249); comparative complements separate from partitives (693 vs 245, 298); degree AdvP only on the four graded quantifiers (784 vs 247, 297); `that day` common-noun-headed (651 vs 300).
