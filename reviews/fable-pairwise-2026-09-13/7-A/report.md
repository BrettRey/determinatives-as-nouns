# Pairwise review: §7 Conclusion × §A Earlier accounts and logical alternatives

§7 restates the package (Noun membership, restricted members, Ordinary Head, coordinate rank) with its evidential ranking, while §A positions that package against prior proposals and tabulated logical alternatives; the sections are complementary and non-dependent, and friction is limited.

## Findings

### 1. CONTEXT CHECK — the common-noun nesting alternative §A tabulates is not visibly closed by §7
- §A line 859: "Pronoun within proper noun; determinative within common noun" (logical alternative). §7 line 826: "The strongest positive comparison comes from quantificational common nouns"; §7 line 832: "Coordinate rank alongside pronoun is a further preference: the comparisons here don't establish the additional shared rules that nesting would express."
- Issue: §7's anti-nesting reason is stated only for pronoun (Hudson, §A line 853). Yet on §7's own ranking the densest shared rules (complements, number transparency, restricted dependents, degree uses) lie with common nouns, which is exactly the nesting §A lists at 859 and §7 never mentions.
- Why it matters: the pair leaves the most evidence-driven nesting option open, while the paper's own materials supply the counter-reason — line 826 "parts of the determinative inventory" and line 828 "Articles needn't independently exhibit the whole profile" show the overlap is partial on both sides, which is what blocks nesting the whole class.
- Check: whether §6 (sec:inheritance, lines 800–822) assesses common-noun nesting, not only Hudson's. If it does, extend line 832 to "alongside pronoun and common noun" and attach the partial-overlap reason; if not, row 859 is an undischarged comparison needing a sentence in §6 or §A.

### 2. QUALIFIED CONCERN — Palmer's defining criterion is the paper's adjectival counterweight, unremarked
- §A line 864: Palmer contrasted determinative adjectives with qualifying adjectives "which permit predicative use, comparison, and adverbial modification". §7 line 826: "The connected adjectival profile of the four gradable quantifiers deserves weight on the same terms" (abstract line 29: "grade, degree modification, and comparative complementation"). §A line 870: the inventory's "contrasts in grade ... extend beyond their proposals".
- Issue: on Palmer's criterion as reported, the four gradable quantifiers are qualifying adjectives and fall outside his grouping with pronouns. §A doesn't note this, and "extend beyond" implies predecessors were silent on grade, whereas Palmer used grade as the boundary, in the opposite direction.
- Why it matters: this is an exposition judgment, not a textual contradiction, but the appendix's novelty claim is imprecise for the one predecessor who applied the relevant test, and §7's counterweight is that test.
- Repair: one clause at 864 or 870, e.g. "Palmer's criterion would detach the four gradable quantifiers; §2 instead treats grade as an internal contrast within one category."

### 3. CONTEXT CHECK — antecedent of "These predecessors" and the caption's promise
- §A line 870: "These predecessors ... leave the full determinative inventory uncategorized"; table lines 853–854 show Hudson and Spinillo do categorize it. Caption line 842: "The prose gives sources and inventory qualifications", but the prose cites no source for CGEL, Hudson, or Spinillo.
- Issue: the claim holds for the four prose-discussed accounts only; the following sentence separates Hudson/Spinillo, but the antecedent is loose. Sources presumably sit in §3/§6.
- Check: confirm §3 and §6 cite Hudson and Spinillo; then read "The four accounts above" and narrow the caption.

### 4. CONTEXT CHECK — proposal label and appendix linkage
- §A line 855 names the proposal "D-noun analysis"; §7 line 830 says "determinative nouns"; the abstract uses neither. Nothing in the packet cross-references sec:historical.
- Check: that §1/§2 introduce "D-noun" (else align the row label with §7's wording), and that some main-text section points readers to §A.

## Checks that pass
- Coordinate taxonomy: §A 855 ("coordinate within Noun … Proposed taxonomy") matches §7 832 and abstract line 29 ("alongside"); §7's hedge "further preference" and §A's deferral of Hudson to sec:inheritance (870) are compatible, and §A doesn't overstate.
- Restricted members: §7 828 (articles via integration; no/none form-selection) is consistent with §A 854 (Spinillo retains the, a, every) and the deferral to sec:articles at 870; both sections describe a categorized inventory with internal differences.
- Level discipline: §A 849–850, 866, 868 confine Postal and Sommerstein to intermediate/underlying representations and say so ("differs from a proposal about the lexical taxonomy of the surface words"); §7's claims are all surface categorization and Head relations, so no equivocation between levels — the pair's main strength.
- Footnote 868 (Payne et al.: anaphoric one a common count noun distinct from determinative one) is compatible with §7: both fall within Noun, so the distinction becomes subcategorial.
- Repetition: none wasteful; §7 doesn't re-survey rivals, §A doesn't re-argue the profile.
