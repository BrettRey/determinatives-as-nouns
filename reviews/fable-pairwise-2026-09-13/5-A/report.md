Appendix A's columns are "Proposed relationship | Level or status" (846); CGEL (852) and Hudson (853) are recorded by taxonomy only, so a reader can't place them in Table `tab:economy`. For Hudson this matters most: 651 uses his dependency/external-headedness distinction and temporal-adjunct data as support for common-noun headedness ("The noun can remain Head while both constituents impose conditions", 653), and 725 treats his nesting as fragment-neutral, yet nowhere in the pair is Hudson's own answer on headedness stated. CONTEXT CHECK: if his paper answers its title question ("Are determiners heads?") in favour of determiner headedness, as many readers will recall, then 651 redeploys his data against his conclusion without saying so, and 853 omits the dimension on which his account differs most from D-noun ordinary Head. Check §6 (`sec:inheritance`) and the source. Repair: one sentence after 862 (or a Head-relation note in the CGEL and Hudson rows), and one clause at 651 stating Hudson's own conclusion.

**2. QUALIFIED CONCERN — Palmer's diagnostic excludes the quantifiers §5 constrains most carefully.**
A 864: qualifying adjectives "permit predicative use, comparison, and adverbial modification"; determinative adjectives, by contrast, don't. §5 693 (degree AdvPs, comparative complements) and 784 ("internal degree-AdvP permissions on *few*, *many*, *much*, and *little*") record exactly those properties for the abstract's "strongest adjectival counterweight". On Palmer's criterion these four are qualifying adjectives, so his grouping doesn't reach the inventory the fragment handles. A 870 ("extend beyond their proposals") gestures at this but never says Palmer's own test excludes them. Not a contradiction; the appendix doesn't claim coverage. Repair: one clause at 864 or 870 naming the mismatch.

**3. CONTEXT CHECK — three "logical alternatives" are neither compared in §5 nor assigned elsewhere.**
A 856 ("Noun, pronoun, and determinative separate"), 857 ("Determinative within Noun; pronoun separate"), 859 ("Pronoun within proper noun; determinative within common noun") are listed "For comparison". §5 holds pronoun within Noun fixed (685 Cat(h) ∈ {N, D}; *she*/*her*/*mine* in Table `tab:permissions`) and asserts neutrality only for nesting determinative inside pronoun (725). A 870 defers only Hudson's nesting and Spinillo's redistribution. Check whether §6 evaluates 856/857/859; if not, either drop them or add a sentence stating why they are set aside (e.g., 859 would require overriding common-noun defaults; cf. 703 "The article's exclusion from argument use remains a separate condition").

**4. DEMONSTRATED (minor) — duplicated Payne et al. citation with inconsistent labels.**
§5 788: "determinative, anaphoric common noun, and generic pronoun"; A footnote at 868: "distinct from determinative *one* and personal pronoun *one*".
