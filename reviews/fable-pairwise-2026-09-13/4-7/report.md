# Pairwise review: §4 (Ordinary Head, fusion, and modifier attachment) × §7 (Conclusion)

§4 argues that ordinary Head fits the nominal grammar at least as well as CGEL's fusion across bare, partitive, externally determined, modified, compound, and genitive cases while deferring the cost verdict to §5; §7 (mainly 830–832) restates that structural contribution as a "regularity" that "complements the profile case" and names the package's fit as the reason to prefer it.

## Findings

**1. CONTEXT CHECK (with a wording concern visible in the packet) — the degree-modifier cost claim.**
§7 832: "Nominal degree modifiers already occur in attributive AdjPs, so their distribution doesn't establish an added cost of NP projection." §4 distinguishes two classes: the AdvP degree series (*very*, *too*) with internal attachment (558, 564) and "NP degree modifiers" (*this much*, *a lot fewer*) that are "internal NP modifiers ... with permissions specific to the head and construction" (560); its defence of adverbial modification rests on Payne et al.'s noun-modifying adverbs and concedes that "Its broader availability with determinatives remains a difference of distribution within the proposed category" (562). Problems: (a) "nominal degree modifiers" matches neither §4 label; (b) the attributive-AdjP justification appears nowhere in §4; (c) §4 concedes a distributional difference where §7 denies an "added cost of NP projection" — reconcilable only if "cost of projection" is distinguished from a retained subcategory restriction (832), a distinction the conclusion asserts rather than draws. This is §7's only explicit cost verdict on §4's material. Check whether §5 (sec:costs) or §2 defines the term and supplies the argument; otherwise align the label with §4/§5, add a section reference, or restate in §4's own terms (562).

**2. CONTEXT CHECK — §7 asserts a preference that §4 explicitly defers.**
§4: "Contextual recovery alone doesn't decide" (449); partitives leave "the choice ... open" (457); "Both cover the example" (524); the uniform-*few* advantage's "value depends on any additional conditions or lost generalizations elsewhere. Section costs compares those costs" (526); cardinal unification "conditional" (528); the fragment follows (612). §7 830: "The package is attractive because its categorization and its Head relations fit together ... neither possibility removes the reason to prefer the combination argued for here." Within §4 the fit rests on one sentence (418: inheritance versus "admitting both Noun and D under those constraints"), and §7 reports no net result from §5 beyond the degree-modifier line. Not a contradiction — §7 relies on §5 — but check that §5 delivers a net verdict and articulates the fit advantage, and add one clause in §7 stating it. Minor related point: §7 830 places partitives inside the "regularity" although §4 457 assigns them no discriminating weight; fine if "regularity" is read descriptively (one Head label, per 524), but a reader may take it as three converging arguments.

**3. QUALIFIED CONCERN — the package is wider in §4 than §7 reports.**
§4 606–610 replaces CGEL's fused analysis of anaphoric *mine* with ordinary Head, letting "the independent-genitive construction" supply interpretation and agreement (610), and says "This extends ordinary headedness to an existing noun subcategory" (608); §4 596 similarly leans on "the compound construction" for the restrictor permission. §7 830 summarizes ordinary Head as covering "bare, partitive, and externally determined determinatives," and 832 says only that the package "retains lexical and constructional restrictions." The conclusion therefore doesn't register that the proposal revises an analysis outside the determinative inventory (a pronoun) or that constructional stipulations now carry what fusion encoded. A reader costing the package from §7 will undercount commitments. Repair: mark the *mine* extension in §4 as optional or required, and add a matching clause in §7.

**4. QUALIFIED CONCERN (low) — the unrepresented cell.**
§7 830: "determinative nouns can participate in fusion." §4's table (510–519) shows only "Separate D with fusion" and "Ordinary determinative Head" (caption: the latter "applies to both taxonomies"); D-noun-plus-fusion is inferable only from "CGEL also uses fusion with nouns" (606). One sentence at 418 or in the caption noting that fusion's function labels carry over under D-noun (DP → NP\textsubscript{D}) would close the gap.
