# Pairwise review — §5 "The matched fragment and comparative costs" × §6 "Coordinate or nested subcategories"

§5 builds a matched constraint fragment to compare the four implementations and favours D-noun with ordinary Head; §6 takes Noun membership as settled and argues for coordinate placement of determinative beside pronoun rather than Hudson's nesting, with §5.725 handing the question forward and §6.822 handing the fragment back.

## Findings

**1. DEMONSTRATED (exposition) — Hudson's headedness is characterized in opposite directions.**
- §5.651: "determiner and common noun depend on each other, although only one connects the phrase to its surroundings" (the one is unnamed); then "His temporal adjunct evidence supports common-noun headedness".
- §6.804: "a determiner is a pronoun whose valency permits the relevant common-noun dependent" — the common noun depends on the determiner, i.e. the determiner heads.
- A reader takes from §5 that Hudson's evidence favours noun-headedness and from §6 that Hudson's determiner takes the noun as dependent. Whether "supports" reports Hudson's conclusion or the paper's reuse of his data is left open. It matters because §5.653 builds on it ("Reciprocal selection is compatible with this headedness") and §6 leans on Hudson's taxonomy.
- Repair: in 651, name the externally connecting element in Hudson's account (consistent with 804) and mark the common-noun inference as the paper's own if it is. The attribution itself can't be verified from this packet.

**2. QUALIFIED CONCERN — §6 doesn't test the shared paradigm §5 itself sets up.**
- §5.647: "The no/none and my/mine pairs distinguish forms within a paradigm (§form-selection)"; Table rows 637–640 give the determinative pair and the pronoun pair identical Det-vs-Subj/Obj distributions.
- §6.808 sets the test: an intermediate category is warranted if it "supported grammatical generalizations applying to pronouns and determinatives together"; the candidates surveyed are context dependence and pro-form gender (814) and determination/modification restrictions (816–818).
- Missing link: a dependent/independent form alternation keyed to Det vs argument use, crossing a determinative and a pronoun pair, is exactly a candidate under 808 — and it is the core of Hudson's criterion as §6.804 states it. §6 never mentions it, so 820's "don't establish a further shared set of grammatical rules" rests on an incomplete candidate list drawn from the paper's own fragment.
- Repair: one or two sentences noting the alternation extends to genitive common/proper nouns (§5.665 "Kim's apples" as Det NP; independent "Kim's"), so it sits at Noun/lexical level — the same rebuttal shape §6 uses at 814 and 816 — or conceding it as the residual case for nesting.

**3. QUALIFIED CONCERN — heterogeneity counts differently in the two sections; inheritance model unstated.**
- §5.782: "differences among existing noun subcategories in determination and modification remain in place" — declared no cost of D-noun; §5.622 makes permissions lexical.
- §6.818: "Nor are those restrictions uniform within the proposed intermediate category… couldn't simply pass the personal pronouns' rules down."
- Reconcilable via 808 (the restrictions can't be the positive shared rule), and 820 frames it so, but 818 reads as a negative argument. Its force depends on whether the taxonomy inherits monotonically or by default: §5.616 places the fragment in constraint-based description (Pullum 2020), while §5.725 and §6.822 speak of "inheritance path(s)". Under default inheritance (Hudson's own setting), non-uniformity is expected; under monotonic inheritance, 725's "without changing this fragment's judgments" presupposes precisely what 818–820 conclude. Consistent, but the dependency should be visible. Linguistic/architectural judgment, not a textual contradiction.
- Repair: state once how category-level statements and lexical permissions combine; recast 818 as "these restrictions can't serve as the generalization 808 requires".

**4. QUALIFIED CONCERN (minor) — §6 elides an adjectival pattern §5 records.**
- §5.691: "independent few also permits definite determination and adjectival premodification, as in the lucky few."
- §6.818: "Their adverbial modifier patterns differ from the restricted adjectival pattern of personal pronouns" (poor old me, 816).
- "The lucky few" and "poor old me" are both restricted adjectival premodification of an independent form; the contrast as written hides a resemblance a §5 reader will notice, and it is again a candidate for 808's test. Repair: acknowledge it, then dismiss via 691 ("don't transfer to every or the articles") and 816's proper-noun parallel.

**5. CONTEXT CHECK (§2 incl. §inflection).** Abstract: "Number and referential contrasts supply further nominal connections"; §6.814 cites §inflection for pro-form gender. If §2 motivates Noun membership partly through properties shared specifically by pronouns and determinatives (deixis, gender, definiteness), §6's 808 test should address those explicitly rather than only context dependence and gender.
