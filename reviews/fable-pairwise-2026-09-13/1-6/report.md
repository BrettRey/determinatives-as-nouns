# Pairwise review: §1 (The question and the alternatives) × §6 (Coordinate or nested subcategories)

§1 states the thesis, fixes the determinative/determiner terminology and the two independent choices (Noun membership, Head analysis), and defers Hudson and the rank question to §6; §6 discharges that deferral by arguing that no pronoun–determinative generalization warrants an intermediate node, leaving nesting coherent but unmotivated. The sections fit well; the friction is in evaluative symmetry and framing, not in substance.

## Findings

**1. QUALIFIED CONCERN — one sentence in §6 reads as a uniformity requirement §1 disclaims.**
- §1 l.77: precedents "motivate comparing the whole grammatical profile, rather than requiring every member to display each nominal property"; pronouns are in Noun "despite differences ... in inflection and dependents."
- §6 l.818: "A broader pronoun category couldn't simply pass the personal pronouns' rules down to determinatives."
- Read alone, l.818 counts failure of rule inheritance against a category, yet §1 has just said such failure doesn't count against Noun (CGEL's Noun can't pass common-noun rules down to pronouns either). The criterion §6 actually applies is the positive one at l.808 ("useful if it supported grammatical generalizations applying to pronouns and determinatives together") and l.820 ("don't establish a further shared set of grammatical rules that requires it"), which is symmetric with the case for Noun membership. So the reasoning is consistent; the phrasing at l.818 isn't.
- Repair: retarget the sentence at the candidate motivation, e.g., "so these restrictions supply no generalization for an intermediate node to carry," optionally adding that heterogeneity as such is tolerated within Noun (§1).

**2. QUALIFIED CONCERN (with CONTEXT CHECK on Appendix A) — "nesting" is silently detached from Hudson's headedness.**
- §1 l.71: "I retain noun-headed NPs and reject the DP analysis"; Table 1 caption l.54: "All keep *apples* as Head in *some apples*."
- §6 l.804 (the paper's own paraphrase): "a determiner is a pronoun whose valency permits the relevant common-noun dependent" — i.e., *apples* depends on *some*, a determiner-headed dependent use that fits none of Table 1's rows. §6 nonetheless frames the alternative as Hudson's (l.802 "Hudson instead puts...") and concludes "Nesting remains a coherent alternative" (l.820), "Both inheritance paths supply the fragment's structures" (l.822).
- The option left open is nesting *plus* the paper's noun-headed NPs, an abstraction from Hudson, not Hudson's account; l.822's compatibility claim holds only for the abstraction. A Word-Grammar-aware reader may take l.820 as partial endorsement of a Head relation §1 rejects.
- Repair: one clause at l.804 or l.820 stating that Hudson's dependency headedness is set aside with the DP hypothesis (§1) and only the taxonomy is at issue. CONTEXT CHECK: Appendix A may already say this; a cross-reference would then suffice.

**3. QUALIFIED CONCERN — rank stated as thesis in §1, delivered as preference in §6.**
- §1 l.38: "I argue that all four are nouns: determinatives form a coordinate subcategory alongside common nouns, proper nouns, and pronouns."
- §6 l.820–822: "I therefore place determinative alongside pronoun ... Nesting remains a coherent alternative"; "The coordinate preference rests on the more specific profile; accepting Noun membership and ordinary Head leaves this further choice open."
- §1 mitigates this itself (l.46 "Two choices," l.50's package excludes rank, l.83 "further question"), so there is no contradiction, but the headline folds rank into what is argued while §6's support is largely the absence of a positive case for a node, plus the different organizing dimensions at l.820.
- Repair: either qualify l.38 ("and, more tentatively, a coordinate subcategory") or make the parsimony default explicit at l.808 so the preference reads as principled rather than residual.

**4. DEMONSTRATED (minor) — "determiner" used as a category label.**
- §1 l.42: "I reserve *determiner* for the syntactic function."
- §6 l.804: "a determiner is a pronoun whose valency permits..." (elsewhere §6 uses "determinative" for Hudson's class, l.802).
- Reporting Hudson's word-class term unflagged breaches the distinction §1 makes central; a CGEL reader momentarily sees a category/function conflation.
- Repair: "a determiner (Hudson's label for the word class) is a pronoun..."
