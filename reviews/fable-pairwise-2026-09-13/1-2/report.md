# Pairwise review: §1 (lines 36–83) × §2 (lines 85–316)

§1 fixes terminology, separates the two independent choices (Noun membership; Head analysis), and sets the weighing criteria; §2 discharges the first choice by comparing determinative profiles with the existing Noun subcategories and with adjectives, then hands off to §3.

## Findings

**1. QUALIFIED CONCERN — the weighing doesn't visibly apply §1's criteria.**
§1 79: three considerations (breadth, recurrence, specificity) "apply equally to nominal and adjectival connections." §2 314: "I give greater weight to the recurring nominal profile," listing "Argument and determiner functions" first. Two gaps: (a) specificity isn't run on the adjectival side, though 245 calls grade + degree + comparative complementation "a connected profile" and grade is the most category-specific property in play; (b) the argument-function leg is exactly what 2.3 erodes—article-free independent uses of *numerous/multiple/countless* (192–193, 204, 225–226), with 237 conceding existential occurrence "leaves fusion available" and 241 warning of circularity—yet 314 doesn't discount it. The operative reason (the four gradable quantifiers also carry Det and independent uses, so grade/degree is additive) is present at 314 but not tied to §1's criteria. Repair: two sentences at 310–314 mapping each criterion to each profile and shifting weight explicitly to Det function and number transparency (136, 239).

**2. QUALIFIED CONCERN — Det-function "positive comparison" vs the paper's own principle.**
§1 69: categories and functions "cut across one another" (why a function doesn't fix a category). §2 120 upgrades shared Det to "a positive comparison" whose semantic "fit" supports Noun membership; 122 then grants PPs fill Det with quantity meaning (*up to twenty minutes*), and 255 states "External functions alone therefore don't establish nounhood." The PP case counters the fit inference just drawn. Repair: reword 120 as defusing the separate-category objection at 118 (Det isn't alien to Noun), or say why PP determiners don't equally "fit."

**3. QUALIFIED CONCERN / CONTEXT CHECK — cardinals' place in the inventory.**
§1 42 defines determinative as "articles, demonstratives, and quantifiers." §2 uses cardinals as determinative data: "two that I have seen" (128), "almost ten" (251; Table 299), "these three" (296); footnote 281 cites reynolds2026numerals for "cardinal nouns." Licit under CGEL's list, but the label invites the question whether cardinals are determinatives, a separate subcategory, or common nouns here. Repair: one clause at 42; confirm against reynolds2026numerals.

**4. QUALIFIED CONCERN — the pronoun precedent is stated as function-based.**
§1 77: CGEL includes pronouns "on the basis of their phrases' functions." §2 108/255 deny that functions alone establish nounhood. Parity would license for determinatives the inference §2 rejects. Repair: after checking CGEL 327–328, qualify 77 so the precedent is for whole-profile comparison, not function-sufficiency.

**5. CONTEXT CHECK — Table tab:existing's modification rows lean on §4.**
§1 83 orders profiles (§2) before structures (§4); §1 50 says the commitments are independent. §2 285 and rows 297/299 encode "proposed determinative attachments, explained in §sec:payne," which 253 says are "available under either ordinary-Head taxonomy" (not neutral toward fusion). 314's verdict doesn't cite these rows, so no circularity is demonstrated; check that §sec:payne doesn't argue from Noun membership, or mark the rows as forward-looking.

**6. Minor within-§2 items bearing on the profile §1 promises.**
Table 295 gives common/proper nouns "genitive Det" only, but 116 lists plain-case *what size shoes*, *Sunday morning*. Footnote 114 drops CGEL's Subject–Det without notice, while §1 40 presents Noun inclusion as the departure. Lines 87 and 140 both say ordinary count nouns are "an inadequate comparison group"; keep one.
