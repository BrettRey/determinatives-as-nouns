## Pair review: §2 "Grounds for a broader Noun category" × §3 "Restricted members and the scope of the grouping"

§2 builds the category-level case that Determinative belongs within Noun, mainly from members with independent uses; §3 extends the result to members lacking such uses (articles, *every*) via "integration into the determinative system" and files *no*/*none* under form selection — a direct dependency, with §3 discharging §2's closing promise.

### 1. The *my*/*mine* precedent sits on the wrong side of §3's own distinction — QUALIFIED CONCERN
- **§3 326** "supplies a precedent for restricted membership"; "Dependent *my* and independent *mine* belong to one pronoun paradigm". **§3 346–348** "Form selection remains necessary, just as with *my*/*mine*"; "two distinctions: between members' permitted constructions, and between the forms selected within a paradigm". **§2 271, 281, 303** pronoun case paradigms; *no*/*none* "concerns form selection"; Inflection row.
- *My* is the dependent form of a lexeme that also has an independent form; §3.2 explicitly assigns it to the form-selection distinction. Articles are restricted at the other level: a lexeme with no independent alternant. The precedent therefore shows only that Noun tolerates a form confined to Det, not that a lexeme lacking any independent form is a noun. Line 326 half-concedes this ("their nounhood depends on the category-level argument") but still presents *my* as the precedent for articles.
- For readers tracking category/function, the precedent looks like the case §3.2 separates from the articles' case.
- Repair: one clause at 326 stating the precedent is form-level (as 346 says) and that articles differ in lacking an independent form; or relocate the precedent to 3.2.

### 2. Collapsing three absences into one contradicts §2.3's evidential stance — QUALIFIED CONCERN
- **§3 338** "Absence of bare, predicative, and partitive uses all limits occurrence without a following nominal; they aren't three independent reasons". **§2 231** "implications between independently specified properties"; **233** "Crossings would count against a single ordering"; **Table 172–173** *certain*/*various* dissociate (G partitive, R bare); **152** predicative "value lies in combination with other properties".
- §2 keeps the independent frames as independently specified, shows they dissociate, and holds predicative use apart as a separate baseline. §3 then treats the three absences as one property to blunt Spinillo. Not a contradiction — an item lacking every frame can be described by one restriction — but the connecting premise is unstated, and predicative use is silently added to the frame set.
- The rebuttal of the strongest published alternative rests on this move.
- Repair: rephrase 338 as "for items lacking every independent frame, the absences follow from a single restriction whatever the implicational structure among the frames in §2.3", and say why predicative absence belongs to the same restriction.

### 3. The positive evidence for articles ∈ Determinative is thinner than "integration" suggests — QUALIFIED CONCERN
- **§3 328** "occupy the same Det position"; "connections extend beyond shared position"; **330** "*The* also participates in degree modification"; **332** "doesn't establish that ordinary *a* freely accepts modifiers". **§2 114–122** Det filled by genitive NPs, plain-case NPs, PPs; "Number, countability, and other selectional conditions distinguish the determining expressions"; **255** "External functions alone therefore don't establish nounhood"; **118–120** Det specialization as "a distinction within Noun".
- By §2's own showing, Det position, definiteness contribution and selectional conditions are shared with NP and PP determiners, so they cannot distinguish "article ∈ Determinative" from Spinillo's separate category. §3 concedes this and leans on 330 and 332; 332 is hedged by the paper itself, and 330 asserts that correlative *the* is the article lexeme without saying whether the cited CGEL pages identify it as such (if they treat it as a distinct determinative, the connection is with a homonym). The retention is in practice default (CGEL inventory) plus rebuttal (336–338).
- The abstract and 320 promise "integration"; readers should see where the weight actually sits.
- Repair: (a) tie 328 to 118–120 so the articles' definiteness marking counts as part of the recurring nominal profile rather than as position alone; (b) at 330, state or qualify the identification with the article; (c) say explicitly that retention is the default absent a stronger separatist case.

### 4. "*Every* permits *almost*/*nearly*" relies on an attachment analysis deferred to §4 — CONTEXT CHECK (Section 4, sec:payne)
- **§3 336** "It also permits *almost*/*nearly*", used at 338 as crossing "the proposed article boundary". **§2 251** "*almost every* … counterparts in established NP modification: *almost the whole class*, *hardly a day*"; **253** attachment "develop[ed]" in §sec:payne; **285** modification rows are "proposed determinative attachments"; **Table 299**.
- §2 supplies article-containing counterparts and marks the attachment as an analytical commitment; §3 uses the permission as a lexeme-level property distinguishing *every* from *the*/*a*. Whether it separates *every* from the articles rather than NPs with quantity content from those without depends on §4.
- Check §4; if it gives *every*-level attachment, add a pointer at 336; otherwise note that the article counterparts require extra quantity content (*whole*, *hardly a*).

### 5. "Hudson" and "the D-noun analysis" — CONTEXT CHECK (Section 1 / Appendix A)
- **§3 340**; §2 calls the proposal "the broader Noun proposal/grouping" (241, 316). Confirm both names are introduced earlier and that "D-noun analysis" is not a second label for the paper's own proposal.

### Checks that pass
- Promise at **316** ("still needs an account of restricted members") is discharged at **320**; forward reference at **281** to sec:form-selection is discharged at **346**.
- *No*/*none* is consistently "forms of one lexeme" (**93, 281, 303, 324, 346**); *every*'s status is consistent (**152, 324, 336**), as is *each*'s independent use (**93, 336**).
- Complex determinatives: *a* inside *a few* (**249**) and *many a* as Det-only (**93 fn, 332**) agree.
- Argument shape is symmetrical: §2 (**314**) keeps the gradable four within Determinative; §3 (**338**) keeps the article trio, on the same principle that subgroup restrictions don't warrant a primary boundary.
- Synchronic method is maintained (**342**), matching §2.
- Recapitulation at **324** (of 320) and **336** (of 251) is brief and purposeful, not wasteful.
