## The main revision question: §6’s case for coordinate rank

§6 should weigh the strongest pronoun–determinative connections that the preceding sections actually supply. These include the table’s distinction between gender-sensitive forms and referent construal, its head-based interrogative/relative forms, the closed inventories, and the construction-sensitive *my/mine*–*no/none* parallel (§2, lines 277–305; §3, line 346; §5, lines 634–645). The current discussion of context dependence, gender beyond Noun, and restricted modification does not quite answer all those candidates on their own terms (§6, lines 814–820).

None automatically establishes an intermediate category. The useful revision would identify their lexical reach, explain what grammatical generalization each might support, and assess that contribution before preferring coordinate rank. The common-noun affinities developed in §2 also belong in that comparison. This is a question about the justification for the further rank preference; it does not overturn the argument for Noun membership.

The modifier comparison needs matching precision. The four gradable quantifiers’ internal degree modifiers differ from personal pronouns’ restricted adjectival modifiers. But both groups permit peripheral NP modification, and *the lucky few* shares a restricted adjectival pattern with *poor old me*. §6’s broad “adverbial modifier patterns” contrast can obscure those distinctions. Its final inheritance objection should concern the positive generalization available to an intermediate category, since the paper elsewhere accepts heterogeneous categories.

## Two questions requiring authorial judgment

**What does the structural comparison add?** §§4–5 promise an assessment of the trade-offs; §5.4 describes them more explicitly than it states their qualitative balance (lines 526, 723, 762–792). A short verdict could say what the modifier arrangements contribute to the preference and connect that result to §5.5. The review does not establish that the accounts tie, require a numerical economy score, or show that structural fit can only remove an objection. Logical separability remains compatible with a mutually supporting category/structure package. The useful request is for a more concrete explanation of the contribution already claimed. See [4–5](4-5/report.md) and [1–5](1-5/report.md).

**What evidence does comparative *the* supply about the article?** §3 uses *the bigger the better* as a connection beyond ordinary Det function (line 330). CGEL discusses the determiner and comparative uses together, but its footnote also distinguishes their historical origins and notes a surviving distributional difference (pp. 1131–1132, fn. 19; pp. 1135–1136). Neither the shared heading nor the etymology settles synchronic lexeme identity. The paragraph should make the relevant commitment clear if the degree use is to support the article’s integration. Its opening “independent uses” would then be more precise as “independent argument uses”. This is a qualified source/analysis question, not a verified homonymy correction. See [2–3](2-3/report.md), [3–5](3-5/report.md), [3–7](3-7/report.md), and the source check in the [verification record](verification.md).

## Findings that did not survive checking

- **Hudson’s headedness:** several reports infer external D-headedness from the common noun’s internal dependency. §5 explicitly distinguishes those relations, and Hudson’s primary text confirms the distinction. That alleged contradiction is not present.
- **Inflection versus form selection:** §3 explicitly treats *no* and *none* as inflectional forms. Their construction-sensitive selection does not conflict with the Inflection row.
- **Pronoun dependents:** one report attributes to CGEL a pronoun-headed analysis of *we men*. CGEL instead distinguishes the relevant personal-determinative construction from pronouns with supplementary apposition. That proposed table addition is unsupported.
- **Missing restrictions:** §5 preserves the articles’ and *every*’s use restrictions, independent *none*, and the special status of complex *a few/a little*. Omitted packet context accounts for several apparent gaps.
- **Functions and profiles:** acknowledging adjective and PP overlaps does not cancel all positive evidential value of a recurring nominal combination. Several proposed “repairs” would remove an argument the text has carefully qualified.
- **Empirical scope:** the quantity-word study explicitly leaves its implication ordering unestablished. The 2021 clustering does not settle category rank. The reports give no reason to start a new matrix study as part of this review.

Several proposed rebuttals were also unsound. *My* and *mine* are both genitive, and unchanged *Kim’s* does not instantiate their form alternation. Nor does partial or nonuniform overlap by itself rule out a broader category under this paper’s method. Those shortcuts should not enter the manuscript.
