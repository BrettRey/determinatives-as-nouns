## Pairwise review: §2 *Grounds for a broader Noun category* × §7 *Conclusion*

§2 builds the positive profile case for placing determinatives in Noun and weighs it against the adjectival counterweight; §7 summarizes that case together with the Head proposal, so the pair is claim-to-summary, with §7 also asserting outcomes that §2 explicitly defers to §§3–6.

### 1. QUALIFIED CONCERN — the quantity-adjective controls disappear from the Conclusion
- §2: 146–241 (longest subsection); 231 "neither establishes an implication scale"; 233 "don't establish that adding a relative improves acceptability"; 241 "Categorizing every new independent use as determinative solely because it passes this test would make the comparison circular. I assess Noun membership through the recurring combination"; 312 "the proposed ordering … remains to be tested".
- §7: 826 "Broader constructional and referential connections … favour the nominal grouping"; no mention of Table 1 or its result.
- Table 1 (174–176) shows *numerous*/*multiple*/*countless* attested in all six frames—as full a range as *many*. §2 therefore downgrades independent use to one component of a combination. §7's "constructional connections" reads as if independent use counted directly, and the one open item §2 flags is dropped.
- Repair: one sentence in §7 recording that independent use is shared with quantity adjectives, so it weighs only in combination, and that the ordering remains untested.

### 2. QUALIFIED CONCERN — *no/none*: inflection or form selection?
- §2 Table 2, 303: Determinative Inflection cell reads "Number; grade; genitive; *no/none*". §2 text, 281: "The *no/none* alternation concerns form selection", listed separately from inflection. §7, 828: "*no/none* retains its form-selection conditions".
- The table files under Inflection what both prose passages treat as something else; visible mismatch, not a judgment call.
- Repair: relocate the item (separate row or footnote). CONTEXT CHECK §3 (`sec:form-selection`) for how form selection is defined relative to inflection.

### 3. QUALIFIED CONCERN — where the weight lies
- §7, 826: "The strongest positive comparison comes from quantificational common nouns." §2, 314: "I give greater weight to the recurring nominal profile"; 310: "Their significance depends on how each pattern connects with the rest of the inventory"; 138/144 confine the comparison to particular constructions. The abstract says "closest".
- "Strongest" invites a load-bearing reading §2 declines. §7 also registers the adjectival profile "on the same terms" but omits §2's disposition (314: the four quantifiers stay within determinative, restrictions localized).
- Repair: "closest/most specific", plus one clause on the four quantifiers.

### 4. CONTEXT CHECK — "degree uses" (826) and attributive AdjPs (832)

Verify `sec:enough` delivers both the nominal-degree-modifier parallel and the attributive-distribution claim before §7 lists them among the strongest evidence.

### 5. CONTEXT CHECK — restricted members and the membership criterion

Verify §3 builds on it, so the criterion isn't applied strictly to *numerous* (241) yet waived for articles without saying why.
