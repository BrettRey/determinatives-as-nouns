# Pairwise review: §1 "The question and the alternatives" (36–83) ↔ §3 "Restricted members and the scope of the grouping" (318–348)

§1 fixes terminology, separates the two independent choices, sets the whole-profile method and its three criteria, and promises that §3 "completes the case for the intended inventory" (83); §3 discharges that promise by arguing that the restricted members (*the*, *a*, *every*, *no*/*none*) stay within determinative against Spinillo, so that §2's category-level Noun argument transfers to them. The pair is promise/discharge plus shared methodological premises, and it mostly coheres.

## Findings

**1. QUALIFIED CONCERN — the *my* precedent (326) instantiates the *other* of §3's own two distinctions.**
§1 (77) grounds inclusion "despite differences" at category level (pronouns within Noun). §3 (326) offers *my*/*mine* as "a precedent for restricted membership within Noun" for the articles. But §3 (346) reuses *my*/*mine* as the model for form selection within a paradigm ("just as with *my*/*mine*"), and 348 explicitly separates "members' permitted constructions" from "forms selected within a paradigm." *My*'s restriction is of the second kind (the lexeme has an independent form); the articles' restriction is of the first (no form of THE or A heads an NP independently). As written, the precedent shows only that Noun tolerates an obligatorily dependent *form*, not an obligatorily dependent *lexeme*. The author half-concedes this ("the corresponding positive evidence is integration into determinative"). It matters because 326 is the sole Noun-internal precedent for lexeme-level restriction, and §1's precedents (77: pronouns, auxiliaries) concern property differences, not distributional gaps. Repair: one clause in 326 stating the precedent narrowly and naming the form/lexeme difference that §3.2 later draws; or relocate the precedent after 346–348 so its scope is visible. This is a judgment about analogical strength, not a textual contradiction.

**2. QUALIFIED CONCERN — Spinillo's complement-omission analogy (340) versus §1's headedness commitments.**
The analogy models *some teachers* on verb + omissible complement, i.e., the nominal as complement of *some* — the "D heads *some apples*" structure §1 rejects (71) and the table caption excludes ("All keep *apples* as Head," 54). §3 correctly takes only the category-continuity moral and notes it "leaves the category to be established," but doesn't flag that on the paper's own analysis the analogy fails where structure matters: an intransitive verb keeps its Head function, whereas *some* changes function, determiner → Head (44). A reader applying §1's category/function separation will see the analogy fits the DP hypothesis better than the D-noun package. Repair: one sentence in 340 accepting the continuity point while declining the complement structure, and noting that the function change is what §4 addresses.

**3. CONTEXT CHECK — do the constraints honour both distinctions §3 names?**
§1 (75) assigns retention of *every*'s restriction to §\ref{sec:det-uniform}. §3 (348) raises the bar: a structural account must respect member-level permissions *and* form selection, including *none of the students* / \*\*no of the students* (346), then defers ("once an expression's use is licensed"). Check that §sec:det-uniform encodes the article exclusion and the *no*/*none* partitive selection, not only *every*; if it handles *every* alone, 348 promises what the fragment doesn't deliver.

**4. CONTEXT CHECK — "constructions that many determinatives enter" (320).**
The abstract locates the nominal evidence in "parts of the determinative inventory" and the adjectival counterweight in "four quantifiers." §1 (79) licenses concentrated evidence, so nothing hinges on "many," but if §2's base is essentially the quantifiers, 320 overstates what the articles inherit from. Verify against §2; if needed, name the subset.

**5. Minor — 328 leads with shared Det position.**
§1 (69, *Kim's book*) shows Det position isn't category-diagnostic. §3 concedes ("extend beyond shared position") and supplies 330/332, so this passes; consider not presenting position as the first ground.

## Checks that pass

- **Promise discharge and non-circularity.** 81 (Spinillo in §3) and 83 ("completes the case") are met at 334–340 and 320/338. §3's grounds for articles-in-determinative (definiteness/quantity system, comparative *the*, complex *a*) don't presuppose nounhood, so the two-step inference (§3: articles ∈ determinative; §2: determinative ⊂ Noun) is valid under §1's whole-profile method (77).
- **Criteria applied symmetrically.** §1's three considerations (79) govern 338: the three absences collapse to one property (non-independence), low descriptive content lacks specificity; conversely *the*'s single-word comparative pattern (330) counts, matching 79's "concentrated in a few words."
- **Category/function discipline.** 330 (*the* modifying AdjP) and 336 (*every* after genitives) are instances of §1's cross-classification (69).
- **Synchronic stance.** 40 fn (language-specific) and 342 (grammaticalization as background) align.
- **Recapitulation.** *Every* at 75 → 324 serves distinct purposes (constraint retention vs. inventory scope); no wasteful repetition.

No epistemic, authorization, or coordination risk is evidenced in this packet.
