# Pair review: §2 (Grounds for a broader Noun category) × Appendix A (Earlier accounts and logical alternatives)

§2 builds the positive distributional, inflectional, and semantic case for placing determinatives within Noun, weighing a recurring nominal profile against a four-word adjectival profile; Appendix A situates that proposal among predecessor accounts and lists taxonomic alternatives, and the two connect only through A's closing characterization of what predecessors miss ("grade, modification, complementation, and restricted independent use", 870), which is a recap of §2's dimensions.

## Findings

**1. QUALIFIED CONCERN — Palmer's partition criteria are the properties §2 finds inside the inventory, and A doesn't say so.**
A 864: Palmer contrasted determinative adjectives with qualifying adjectives, "which permit predicative use, comparison, and adverbial modification." §2 245–247 documents comparison (*few/fewer/fewest*) and degree modification (*very few*, *so few*) for *few/many/much/little*; §2 152 records predicative *Its advantages are several* / *Their enemies were many*; §2 314 keeps these words within determinative on the strength of the nominal profile. Applied as stated, Palmer's three diagnostics would move exactly the subgroup §2.4 calls "the strongest adjectival connection" to the qualifying side. A's only acknowledgement is the generic "extend beyond their proposals" (870); it presents Palmer as the earliest ally without noting that his own boundary is cut across by §2's evidence and that the paper resolves the case by a different method (weighing profiles, §2.6) rather than by his diagnostics. Not a contradiction, but the one point where the history bears directly on §2's central weighing is left undrawn. Repair: one sentence after 864 or 870 stating the cross-cut and pointing to §2.4/§2.6.

**2. QUALIFIED CONCERN (check §1) — the adjectival alternative is absent from A's alternative space.**
A 838 promises "further logical alternatives"; rows 856–859 are all Noun-internal arrangements. §2 87 frames the question as nominal connections versus "affinities to adjectives", §2 148 opens with *numerous* versus *many*, and §2 310 weighs "the nominal and adjectival connections". The traditional adjective treatment—reflected in Palmer's very term—is the counterweight §2 spends most of §§2.3–2.4 on, yet a reader of Table 3 would infer the competition is only about nesting. Check whether §1 sets out the adjectival option; if so, add a row or cross-reference in A; if not, the stated alternative space is incomplete relative to §2's own argument.

**3. CONTEXT CHECK (§1, §6) — naming, "coordinate", and undischarged "for comparison".**
A 855 labels the proposal "D-noun analysis" and commits to "coordinate within Noun"; §2 uses "the broader Noun proposal/grouping" (241, 316) and never "D-noun", though its four-column Table 2 and "the four noun groups" (124) presuppose coordination. Confirm §1 introduces the label and that §6 ("Coordinate or nested subcategories") concludes coordinate rather than leaving it open. Separately, the four "logical alternative" rows are marked "For comparison", but A performs no comparison and hands off only Hudson and Spinillo (870). §2 supplies material bearing on at least two of them—140/144 (quantificational common nouns "approach the determinative profile") on "determinative within common noun", and 273 (deixis links demonstratives with pronouns) on pronoun-side nesting—so verify that §1 or §6 actually discharges the comparison.

**4. QUALIFIED CONCERN (low) — Postal's *we men* datum has no counterpart in §2's pronoun profile.**
A 866 cites "combinations such as *we men*" as Postal's evidence for the article–pronoun connection. §2 Table 2 (297) gives the pronoun column "Restricted AdjP premodifiers and relative postmodifiers", omitting nominal dependents, though 285 cites the same CGEL pages (429–430) for *all we who signed up*. Neither section states how the paper handles the construction (CGEL: pronoun head with nominal dependent), and it is the one predecessor datum that could feed §2.1's Det-function argument (114–116). Repair: add nominal dependents to the pronoun row, or a clause in A noting the analysis adopted.

**Minor.** "Level" carries three senses across the pair: taxonomic generality (A 838, Lyons), representational depth (A 849–850, 866), and phrase versus word (§2 255, 285). Table 3's column "Level or status" merges the first two; "Kind of claim" would be clearer.

## Checks that pass

- Lyons's levels point (838) licenses §2's move from "a primary boundary or a distinction within Noun" (118, 120) and its "concentration in a subgroup" caveat (310); the framing is consistent.
- A 870 correctly locates the predecessors on articles and personal pronouns, whereas §2's evidence rests on quantifiers and demonstratives and defers articles (316; abstract). The handoffs—articles/Spinillo to §sec:articles, Hudson to §sec:inheritance—are explicit and complementary, not duplicated.
- §2 152's grouping of *every* with the articles as predicatively excluded is compatible with Spinillo's retained-article set (854).
- A's opening premise—one category across dependent and independent uses (838)—matches §2's practice (103) and §2's own circularity caveat (241).
- No wasteful repetition: A's closing list is a compact, accurate recap of §2's dimensions.

**Risk note.** No epistemic, authorization, or coordination risk is evident in this packet. Corpus and source claims in §2 (183; footnotes 188–226) are the author's own verification records and cannot be checked here.
