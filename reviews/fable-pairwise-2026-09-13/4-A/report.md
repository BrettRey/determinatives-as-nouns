# Pair review: §4 (Ordinary Head, fusion, and modifier attachment) × Appendix A (Earlier accounts and logical alternatives)

§4 argues that ordinary Head for independent determinatives fits the nominal grammar at least as well as *CGEL*'s fusion (deferring costs to §5); Appendix A places the D-noun taxonomy among earlier category proposals and logical alternatives. They touch only at one principle: that keeping a word in one category across dependent and independent uses leaves its taxonomy open (A 838; §4 556).

## 1. QUALIFIED CONCERN — A records predecessors' taxonomic axis but not their structural axis, which is §4's entire subject

- A 846 (columns "Proposed relationship / Level or status"), 866 ("article segments occur at intermediate stages … surface pronouns can receive derivative Noun status"), 868 ("argues in the opposite direction, giving the definite article and personal pronouns underlying NP structure"), 870 ("challenge a fundamental article–pronoun separation").
- §4 358 ("Independent *some* has ordinary Head under D-noun; in *CGEL*, its DP jointly fills Det of NP and Head of Nom"), 420 ("gives *some* that phrase type in Det and object uses alike"), 447 (*She left*/*Some left*), 478 ("requires neither a silent noun nor conversion").
- Gap: the predecessors bundled a taxonomy with a structural treatment of the independent use, and A doesn't say which of §4's options each corresponds to. On my reading (not verifiable from this packet): Postal's surface pronoun is an article whose noun is removed — the silent-noun route §4 478 sets aside and the nearest historical analogue of Det–Head fusion; Sommerstein's NP structure for the article is the analogue of §4's determinative-headed NP (fig:some, D-noun pair); Hudson's taxonomy (A 853) comes with determiner-as-head of the whole NP, the reverse of §4 358 ("*apples* as its ultimate head").
- Why it matters: the abstract claims the category choice and the Head choice are independent yet "fit." A is where the paper could show that predecessors combined them in every direction — the best evidence for independence — and which line D-noun + ordinary Head continues (Sommerstein's). Without it, a reader who knows Hudson may read §4's "ordinary Head" as determiner-headed NPs.
- Repair: a fourth column, or two sentences at 866–870, mapping each account to §4's options (deletion, fusion, NP projection, determiner-as-head), cross-referenced to §\ref{sec:head-relations}; keep the Postal characterization hedged.

## 2. CONTEXT CHECK (§6) — A's "coordinate" commitment vs §4's pronoun–determinative parallels

- A 855 ("Common noun, proper noun, pronoun, and determinative coordinate within Noun. Proposed taxonomy"); A's alternatives (856–859) list nestings in both directions but not Palmer's grouping (848, 864) relocated inside Noun — a shared pronoun+determinative node coordinate with common and proper noun.
- §4 447 ("Applying that structure to *Some left* preserves the same division"), 606–610 (*mine* as ordinary Head "extends ordinary headedness to an existing noun subcategory"), fig:some subscripts N_D/N_common/N_proper.
- §4's structural parallels support grouping determinatives with pronouns at least as well as coordinate placement, and §4 doesn't claim to decide this. Check that §6 considers the intermediate-node option and that its conclusion licenses A 855 as written; otherwise soften A to match §6.

## 3. CONTEXT CHECK (sec:inheritance) — "inheritance" used as a term at §4 418 without definition; A 870 sends Hudson's nesting there

- §4 418: "Under D-noun, inheritance makes Noun's phrase-structure constraints available to determinatives, subject to their restrictions." The argument goes through under Hudson's nested inheritance as well as coordinate inheritance; §4 needn't distinguish them, but sec:inheritance should supply both the definition (matching 418's usage: phrase-structure constraints inherited, subcategory restrictions retained) and the reason coordinate inheritance is preferred.

## 4. Checks that pass

- Category continuity: §4 556 ("keeping *few*, *any* … in one lexical category … The D-noun analysis preserves that continuity") makes no differential claim; A 838 correctly notes the principle is taxonomy-neutral. No overclaim either side.
- Palmer's criteria vs the gradable four: A 864 reports comparison and adverbial modification as qualifying-adjective marks; §4 534, 558, 564 give *few, many, much, little* degree modification. A 870 ("contrasts in grade, modification … extend beyond their proposals") registers this; naming the four would make the concession concrete but isn't required. A's "modifiers of nouns" (864) conflates what §4 476 and Table tab:matched-heads (515–517) distinguish as Det and Mod; harmless as a report of Palmer's terminology.
- Promises and repetition: §4's forward references (420, 506, 526, 528, 612) all go to §5; A's (870) go to sec:inheritance and sec:articles. Nothing is owed between the pair, and there is no overlap to prune.
- Lyons's levels point (A 838) is the premise behind §4 562 ("a difference of distribution within the proposed category") — helpful recap. CONTEXT CHECK (§1–2): if the premise isn't also in the main text, an appendix is a weak home for it.
