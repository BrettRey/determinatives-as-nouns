# Pair 6–A: Coordinate or nested subcategories / Earlier accounts and logical alternatives

§6 decides where determinative sits within Noun (coordinate with pronoun, tentatively, against Hudson's nesting); A tabulates predecessors and logical alternatives, defers Hudson to §6, and so depends on §6 to dispose of the within-Noun options it lists.

## Findings

### 1. Table A's within-Noun alternatives (858–859) are never engaged by §6, and §6's own evidence prima facie favours one of them
**Status:** QUALIFIED CONCERN
**Anchors:** A 842 ("further logical alternatives"), 858 ("Pronoun within determinative"), 859 ("Pronoun within proper noun; determinative within common noun"), 870 (only Hudson and Spinillo deferred); §6 802 ("where within Noun do they belong?" — two answers offered), 808 (criterion: a level is warranted by "grammatical generalizations applying to [two groups] together"), 816 ("proper names also resist free determination and modification"), 818 ("Nor are those restrictions uniform").
**Problem:** By 808's criterion, 816 supplies a generalization holding of personal pronouns and proper names (primary uses) which 818 shows does *not* hold of determinatives. The paper's own reasoning therefore removes this property as support for Hudson's nesting while leaving it as a candidate generalization for a pronoun–proper-noun grouping — the first half of row 859. §6 says nothing about 858 or 859, and 802 frames the question as binary.
**Why it matters:** The deciding section overlooks competitors the paper itself tabulates, and the argument form used against Hudson can be turned toward an unmentioned alternative.
**Repair:** One sentence after 818: 816's restriction is stated for personal pronouns only and for primary uses of proper names, so it is neither pronoun-level nor uniform; applied evenly, 808's criterion yields no intermediate level among the four subcategories — which is the coordinate hierarchy and disposes of 858–859 together. Alternatively, at 870 state that rows 856–859 display the design space and are defended by no author. CONTEXT CHECK for 859's second half: since the abstract makes quantificational common nouns "the closest comparison", a reader will want the exclusion of determinative-within-common-noun located (presumably §2/§5).

### 2. 818's closing sentence treats specific-level difference as a strike against nesting, while A 838 endorses the levels principle
**Status:** QUALIFIED CONCERN
**Anchors:** §6 818 ("A broader pronoun category couldn't simply pass the personal pronouns' rules down"); A 838 ("two expressions may belong together at one level and differ at a more specific level"); §6 804 (Hudson: category constant, uses differ in dependents).
**Problem:** Under the principle A endorses (and Hudson's default inheritance), a pronoun supercategory would carry only what all pronouns share, not the personal pronouns' rules; the sentence targets a version of nesting nobody holds. 820's concession that nesting is coherent shows the real content is 808's "no generalization to capture", not "difference blocks nesting".
**Repair:** Reword to "so the restriction isn't a pronoun-level generalization that nesting would capture."

### 3. Hudson's table row is unqualified, and the caption's promise is discharged only in §6
**Status:** QUALIFIED CONCERN (low)
**Anchors:** A 842 ("The prose gives sources and inventory qualifications"), 853 ("Determiners within pronoun"); A 864–870 (no Hudson source or qualification); §6 806 ("sets aside all, cardinal numerals, and quantifiers restricted to plural or non-count nouns"; "remains unsettled").
**Problem:** A reader of the table takes Hudson's nesting to cover CGEL's inventory; the qualification lives only at §6 806.
**Repair:** Add "(core determiners; see §6)" to row 853 or a clause at 870.

**Status:** CONTEXT CHECK (§3, `sec:articles`)
