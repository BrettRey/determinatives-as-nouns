# Pair review: §4 (Ordinary Head, fusion, and modifier attachment) × §5 (The matched fragment and comparative costs)

§4 lays out the competing Head analyses (ordinary Head vs Det–/Mod–Head fusion) and the modifier-attachment sites in trees and prose; §5 restates the shared restrictions as a fragment, formalizes the Head configurations, and is where §4 repeatedly sends the reader for constraints and costs (420, 506, 526, 528, 612).

## Findings

### 1. QUALIFIED CONCERN — the cost comparison §4 promises isn't delivered as a ledger
- §4 526: "its value depends on any additional conditions or lost generalizations elsewhere … Section~\ref{sec:costs} compares those costs"; 530: "The modifier permissions … now require a closer comparison." §5 723: "comparative costs summarized below."
- §5.5 (762–792) supplies Table tab:economy, statements of what is *not* an added cost (782), and what "remain[s] explicit" (784), then moves to taxonomic consequences (786–792). The one ordinary-Head cost §5 does identify — two approximative attachment sites plus a separately stated internal cardinal permission (697) — is never netted against fusion's side: a single DP-internal premodifier site (719) but an "additional Nom" for Mod–Head (713, 717) and inner-phrase exclusions that "need stating separately" (721).
- Why it matters: every direct comparison in §4 ends in a draw (449, 457, 472, 524), so the reader is told the decision turns on costs; §5.5 never states the balance, and 796 grounds the preference elsewhere ("profile and the ordinary NP structures"). Not a contradiction — "coherent alternatives" (796) is honest hedging — but 526's question is left open.
- Repair: one paragraph in §5.5 listing, per implementation, what each must state beyond the shared fragment (697, 717, 719, 721) and saying whether the modifier comparison is a wash.

### 2. QUALIFIED CONCERN — what "follows from Noun membership" is thinner than the verbs suggest
- §4 418: inheritance "makes Noun's phrase-structure constraints available." §5 622: "Noun membership alone grants none of these permissions"; 678: structural conditions are shared by "the ordinary-Head accounts"; 689: "Both permit the same nominal layers … leaves the lexical grouping open"; 723: "under D-noun they follow from Noun membership … that alignment … favour[s] D-noun"; 796: "their regularity in turn strengthens the grouping."
- On the text's own terms the sole difference is Cat(h)=N vs Cat(h)∈{N,D} (684–685). "Leaves … open" (689) and "strengthens" (796) pull in different directions, and 616 disclaims statement-counting, so the criterion must be reuse-within-category, which 723 only gestures at ("across a primary-category boundary").
- Repair: say plainly that the structural alignment consists in the non-disjunctive category condition, and use one calibrated verb.

### 3. QUALIFIED CONCERN (label inconsistency itself DEMONSTRATED) — determinative-headed NP labels vs §5's Det set
- §4 labels the same phrase type NP (397 *some*; 576 *anyone*), NP_D (485 *the*; 544/546 *every*), and NP[gen] (426); caption 415 defines subscripts only for N.
- §5 672: Det:{NP,PP}; 676: determinative-headed and genitive NPs "still require separate identification"; "The notation NP itself imposes no requirement that the ultimate lexical head be Noun."
- Missing link: §4 420 promises "The fragment compares the remaining selectional conditions"; §5 concedes identification is needed (676) without comparing its content with CGEL's DP/NP[gen] split, and 798 already hedges. If the subscript does the identifying, the "consolidation" (676) is CGEL's DP label renamed for the three NP-projecting accounts.
- CONTEXT CHECK: whether NP_D is defined in §2. Repair: one figure convention; one sentence in §5.2 on what identifies determinative-headed NPs in Det and how that differs in content from DP.

### 4. QUALIFIED CONCERN — §4.6's independent-genitive conditions don't reach the fragment
- §4 608: ordinary Head for *mine* "in both anaphoric and predicative uses"; 610: "the independent-genitive construction supplies the NP's … agreement properties" (*Mine is/are ready*). §5 620 excludes predication; Table 640 grants *mine* Subj; 649 gives number properties for *few*/*some* only; no genitive condition appears in 691–703.
- The person/number override is a constructional condition ordinary Head must state (it belongs in C, 661); §4 612's "The fragment now makes the permissions and restrictions explicit" over-promises here. That a first-person head yields third-person agreement is a linguistic worry about "ordinary" headedness, not a textual contradiction — 352 defines ordinary Head by function assignment.
- Repair: add the condition at 649/691 or exclude agreement at 620.

### 5. Minor — framing and repetition
