Working record for the approved Journal of Linguistics readership. The earlier manuscript is preserved in `notes/snapshots/2026-09-07-pre-rebuild/`. Submission packages are preserved separately.

## Reader contract and route

The reader's question is whether English determinatives belong within Noun and, if so, whether to keep determinative as a subclass coordinate with pronoun. The proposed answer remains a fourfold Noun taxonomy. The explanatory spine is shared nominal structure, retained differences in local licensing, and a comparison of what each hierarchy actually gains. The hardest section is the inference from independent uses to nounhood: the old manuscript partly assumed the structure it offered as evidence. Reader-facing reconstruction is appropriate because local rewriting would retain that circular route. If a worked comparison showed that the old structure already exposed these distinctions, bounded repair would suffice; it does not.

The approved brief explicitly anticipated empirical equivalence between hierarchies and required an account of architectural economy in that event. Accordingly, the rebuild may advocate the fourfold taxonomy while acknowledging that distributional clusters and the present corpus do not uniquely identify it. It may not invent predictions to make the proposal win.

## Source corrections

- **Hudson 2004, pp. 9–10; Hudson 2010, pp. 253–254:** noun already includes common noun, proper noun and pronoun; determiners fall within pronoun through valency, without a separate determiner word-class node. The earlier manuscript's account of common nouns being outside Hudson's supercategory was wrong. The 2004 paper's operational criteria cover a narrower range than CGEL's determinative, but this should not be treated as a definitive, exhaustive current inventory.
- **Brett's clarification, 7 September 2026:** Hudson has said in personal communication that he is not really sure what the determiner list is. The date of that communication is not yet supplied. Keep the distinction between the published operational criteria and a settled inventory; do not make uncertain coverage a decisive objection. No date or verbatim quotation is to be invented.
- **Hudson 2004, pp. 7–9:** the headedness proposal uses mutual dependency; common nouns and determiners can each provide the external head depending on the construction. It is not a blanket D-head analysis.
- **Sommerstein 1972, pp. 197–203:** the definite article and personal pronouns have an underlying pronominal analysis. The argument concerns remote structure, relative clauses, and insertion versus deletion of `one`; it cannot simply be presented as the modern fourfold surface taxonomy. Primary PDF/JSTOR metadata verified. The retrieved 1974 item is a review of Hewson's book, not an independent classificatory paper; it need not be cited here.
- **Lyons 1968, pp. 232–235 and 279:** the first passage distinguishes distribution at different depths of classification; the second links articles, demonstratives and personal pronouns through definiteness and deixis. The specific claim attributed to him in the review, that determiners head nominal expressions and nouns depend on them, remains unverified in the passages checked. The manuscript will cite only the confirmed points. Chapters 6–8 and the subject index were retrieved through Cambridge via U of T; the retrieval exceeded the eventual short discussion's needs and has stopped.
- **CGEL, pp. 410–411:** independent genitives occur both as fused heads and as pure heads. `Mine` in an anaphoric example corresponding to `Kim's car` is fused; the possessive predicative use in `it's mine` is pure Head. The previous manuscript wrongly generalized the latter structure to independent genitives generally.
- **Reynolds 2021:** the paper already explicitly leaves nesting open and warns that DISCO significance cannot determine category status. The new audit must not present those qualifications as novel discoveries about an otherwise unqualified claim.
