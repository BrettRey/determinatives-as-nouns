## Main correction

The previous revision of §5.4 treated the stable Nom–NP Head relation as a reason to favour ordinary Head. That comparison was defective: all four analyses give NP an immediate Nom head in the matched bare, partitive, and externally determined expressions. Both Head analyses also agree on the ultimate lexical head.

The revised comparison identifies the actual difference. Ordinary headedness gives the word a direct Head relation to Nom, with Nom heading NP. Fusion introduces an intervening phrase filling Det–Head or Mod–Head. The preference concerns sharing the full direct Head chain across determinative uses and with established nouns. Fusion receives credit for sharing a construction with adjectives. This corrects my wording from the preceding pass; the trees and formal analyses themselves required no change.

## Philosophical levels

The audit checked predicates against their bearers: word form, lexeme, occurrence, phrase, lexical category, and category profile. Describing an *apple* now supplies “descriptive content,” avoiding the ambiguous “describes a category” (§2.5). Phrasal distributions can legitimately inform lexical classification when the connection through their heads is explicit; they needn’t be intrinsic properties of words to count as evidence.

Existing safeguards remain sound: NP agreement isn’t lexical-head inflection; possessor person isn’t possessed-entity agreement; form alternation isn’t lexeme multiplication; attestation isn’t unrestricted grammaticality or frequency; a constructional cline isn’t a degree of nounhood or a historical sequence; feature clustering doesn’t determine taxonomic rank; and shorter notation doesn’t establish grammatical economy. The historical comparisons distinguish semantic, underlying-representational, and lexical-taxonomic claims. The corpus inventory remains evidence for testing the proposal, not a mechanism explaining it.

No further level error was identified. Noun membership, ordinary Head, and coordinate rank remain comparative preferences with stated alternatives, rather than consequences established by classification or the feature matrix alone. The one-lexeme treatment of *the* is retained.
