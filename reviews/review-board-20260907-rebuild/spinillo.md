# Review for *Journal of Linguistics*

*Simulated review in the assigned reviewer role. I do not speak for the real M. Spinillo, and characterizations of her published work below are my recollection and are marked unverified.*

**(1) The claim.** The paper argues that CGEL's determinative category should be relocated inside Noun as a fourth subclass coordinate with common noun, proper noun and pronoun, so that determinatives inherit ordinary nominal projection in both dependent and independent uses while their distinctive restrictions stay at the subclass and lexeme levels — with the explicit rider that the evidence does not exclude nesting determinatives inside pronoun.

**(2) Strengths.**

*Discipline about what is being asked.* "Classification, headedness and function" cleanly separates three decisions that this literature routinely conflates, and the paper holds the line: "classifying *some* as a noun doesn't make it the head of every phrase in which it occurs" ("The question"). The trees keep *apples* as ultimate head. This is the right frame, and it is executed consistently.

*Accurate reading of the sources it does cite.* The Hudson discussion in "The nearest alternatives" is unusually careful — noting that his 2004 criteria "centre on licensing a singular count common noun," that "he explicitly sets aside such items as *all*, cardinal numerals," and that "CGEL's determinative category is broader." Sommerstein is likewise not flattened into a relabelling proposal. Where this paper engages prior art, it engages it honestly; that makes the omission in W1 more conspicuous, not less.

*Refusal of cheap economy.* "Count the restrictions as well as the labels" and Table 5's retained-cost column are the best pages here: "A phrase-type distinction has moved into the features of a shared category; it hasn't disappeared from the grammar." Similarly, "The articles and other restricted members" blocks the obvious circularity: "the articles can't be called nouns simply because the proposed noun subclass has been defined to include them."

**(3) Weaknesses, by impact.**

**W1 (highest): the closest English prior art is absent, and its absence lets a negative result do positive work.** "The nearest alternatives" asserts that "the label *determiner* doesn't supply a settled common inventory." That is precisely the conclusion of the English determiner-class-dissolution literature of c. 1995–2005 — including, on my recollection (**unverified**), Spinillo 2000 (*AAA* 25(2)) and the 2004 London thesis, which reached it by item-by-item distributional diagnostics rather than assertion. The paper states the conclusion without the diagnostics that earned it, and then treats the resulting instability as compatible with a stipulated inventory taken from CGEL. But an ill-formed class licenses *redistribution* of its members across existing categories at least as readily as it licenses a new coordinate subclass. The fourfold architecture is nobody's prior commitment; it must be argued, not inherited.

*Concrete alternative.* Expand "The nearest alternatives" with a third rival row and ~1,000 words: (i) state the dissolution position and its diagnostics (mutual exclusion in the singular count frame, pre-/central/post-determiner co-occurrence, partitive licensing, independent occurrence); (ii) say for each which the fourfold proposal inherits, reinterprets, or rejects; (iii) add "Dissolution" to Table 2 alongside CGEL, Hudson and Present, with its own column entry for the dependent/independent relation; (iv) state plainly that the paper takes CGEL's inventory as a working boundary and give the reason. Credit for the negative result belongs where it was earned.

**W2: "fourfold" is currently stipulated, not defended.** The paper's own concessions are decisive against its title: "none selects coordination over nesting within Noun by itself" ("Evidence for nominal structure"), and in "Coordinate subclasses and inheritance," a nesting test "would recover a distinction internal to the superclass. It would not show that the superclass is incoherent." The sole argument for rank — "the two subclasses organize different concentrations of grammatical properties" — is an argument for two subclasses, not for their level. What remains is conservatism: "The preservation of an existing classification is an economy relative to that descriptive starting point." *Alternative:* execute in miniature the matched fragment promised in "Consequences for further comparison" — six lexemes (*the, every, some, few, many, mine*) × four constructions, written out under both hierarchies, with inherited constraints counted. That fragment would be the paper's contribution. Failing that, retitle to the defensible claim (determinatives as nouns) and present the fourfold arrangement as the cheapest revision of CGEL.

**W3: the empirical apparatus is over-weighted relative to what it yields.** 220 trees, 387 D nodes, and "no D-token occurrence of bare *few*, *either* or *neither*" cannot speak to lexical breadth, as the paper concedes; and the matrix "has no common or proper nouns," so it cannot test the supercategory claim at all. Both sections end in null taxonomic results. Compress to a short "what the earlier quantitative result can and cannot show" and move the audit to an appendix or a standalone note — where the reproduction discrepancy can be addressed properly rather than in passing.

**(4) Key question.** Given "the label *determiner* doesn't supply a settled common inventory," what independent diagnostic fixes membership in the proposed determinative subclass? The architecture presupposes an inventory the paper declines to determine.

**(5) Recommendation: Revise & Resubmit.** The conceptual apparatus and the honesty about limits are publishable-grade; the paper needs to engage the class-dissolution prior art properly and either defend "fourfold" with a worked fragment or downgrade it to a framing choice.

**Not a viability objection.** These are problems with the *argument as staged*, not reasons the idea is unpromising. The auxiliaries-in-Verb precedent is apt, and a nominal treatment of determinatives is worth pursuing; my objection is that the paper currently gets to *fourfold* by inheritance from CGEL rather than by evidence.

**What would overturn my principal objection.** (a) A `.bib` showing the dissolution literature cited elsewhere in the submission, or a demonstration that its conclusions are incompatible with retaining any determinative class — in which case W1 shrinks to a credit-and-signposting fix; (b) a matched-fragment comparison showing the nested analysis requires strictly more separate statements — which would convert W2 from a fatal gap into a completed argument.
