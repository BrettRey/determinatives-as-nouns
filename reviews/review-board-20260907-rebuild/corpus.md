The manuscript’s reported empirical results are accurate and reproducible within the supplied materials. I found no numerical or corpus-count error requiring correction. The empirical sections are unusually careful about distinguishing annotation, statistical association and evidence for a particular taxonomy. I recommend minor methodological clarifications; the principal unresolved issue is evidential reach, which the manuscript largely acknowledges.

1. **Numerical verification.** I reran the matrix recovery and the complete R analysis in a temporary copy using the authorized environments. All regenerated numerical CSVs, assignments, lineage records and session information matched the supplied outputs byte for byte. The public matrix contains 138 forms and 155 binary features. Recovery yields 232 features; the comparison identifies 77 removed columns, 11 changed cells and two normalized row names. This verifies the relationship between the supplied files, without authenticating the recovered matrix as the historical publication input.

   Independently of the R script, I calculated pairwise Euclidean distances from the binary matrix and reconstructed the DISCO decomposition: between = 30.58286083019, within = 356.41606563553, total = 386.99892646572, and F = 11.66970143585. These agree with the manuscript.

   The rerun also reproduces the public-matrix single-start range of 75–132 matches, eight runs with 129 matches, and the best-objective 100-start fit with 125/138 matches. The corresponding selected sensitivity results are 131/138, 120/138 and 97/138; every reported permutation result is p = .001. None of the 700 single-start fits reached the ten-iteration limit. The observed instability therefore cannot simply be attributed to runs hitting that cap.

2. **Corpus verification.** I reran the inventory against the pinned checkout and independently counted its lexical nodes and local functions. The four dataset checksums match the manifest. There are 220 trees, with no duplicate sentence identifiers, 3,390 overt lexical nodes and 387 D nodes. The function counts reproduce exactly: Det 297, Mod 18, Det–Head 65, Marker 1, Flat 4 and Coordinate 2. Every regenerated corpus output matches the supplied version byte for byte. All twelve corpus-table rows and all four matrix-table rows match the embedded manuscript.

   I read all 65 retained Det–Head contexts. Their heterogeneity supports the manuscript’s warning against treating them as 65 ordinary independent arguments. The numerical fragments, age supplements, floating quantifiers and degree/frequency uses are present. So is the embedded numeral in *about 30 seconds*. Bare *few*, *either* and *neither* have zero D-token occurrences under the stated extraction.

   The quoted *test-drive both*, *so many tell lies … none*, and *something reliable and good looking* examples occur in the cited sentences. The last is annotated with coordinated adjectival postmodification. The manuscript correctly distinguishes this from evidence for *the lucky few*.

3. **Clarifications recommended.** Specify the distance metric in the manuscript’s methods paragraph: the analyses pass unscaled binary matrices to the package and use Euclidean distance, which here is the square root of the number of feature mismatches. “Binary features” and “distance-based” do not uniquely specify this choice. State that feature columns receive equal weight; correlated or repeated diagnostics can consequently influence the geometry.

   Clarify that p = .001 is the minimum attainable value under the implemented 999-permutation calculation, rather than an unusually precise estimate of a population probability. The existing cautions about theory-informed coding and nonrandom word-form rows are appropriate.

   The file named `corpus-det-head-review.csv` preserves contexts but contains no manual construction classifications or inspection notes. This is sufficient for checking examples and counts. If the constructional characterization becomes a substantive result, add explicit classifications and brief reasons for ambiguous cases. At present, the manuscript wisely makes no quantitative claim about their proportions.

4. **Interpretation and remaining limits.** The matrix establishes separation between the supplied profiles, not nounhood or the taxonomic rank of either group. The corpus establishes occurrences under an existing annotation scheme, not superiority of the replacement analysis. The manuscript consistently observes these limits. Its positive grammatical case consequently rests on worked analyses and constructional judgments, especially the claim that nominal projection shares explanatory work after restrictions are retained.

   I did not independently validate the 155 feature judgments, authenticate the original article’s appendix or reported results, verify historical bibliographic claims, or conduct a judgment study. Agreement with the original publication is therefore verified only relative to the historical values supplied in this manuscript.

My assessment would change if independent recoding or matched grammatical fragments showed that the nominal account predicts additional distributions with fewer independently necessary restrictions—or, conversely, loses predictions furnished by fusion or a nested hierarchy. The present reproducible analyses support the paper’s bounded claims; they do not settle that comparison.
