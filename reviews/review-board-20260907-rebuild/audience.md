**Ten-second impression (title + abstract).** Title is clear and states a categorial claim. The abstract, however, spends most of its length withdrawing: "don't uniquely select," "depends on initialization," "identifies gaps," "fewer... don't mean fewer." I finished it able to name the proposal but not able to name what the paper *shows*.

**Sixty-second read (§1).** The claim, terminology and scope are crisp; the disavowal of the DP hypothesis (§1, final ¶) is helpful. But novelty is stated negatively ("can't claim novelty merely for...") and the two questions posed at §1 ¶5 are followed by the concession that the evidence cannot separate them. No paragraph says why a reader outside CGEL/Word Grammar should care.

**Full inspection.**

- **What is the claim?** ANSWERED. §1 ¶3 and Table 2 row 3.
- **What is new?** PARTLY. The differentiator ("retaining the broader CGEL determinative category as a coordinate subclass") appears once, at §1 ¶4, and is never restated as a positive contribution list. Table 2 gives taxonomies but no differing predictions.
- **Why does it matter?** NOT ANSWERED. No consequences are given for description, annotation, acquisition, typology or any downstream analysis. §9.3 says what a *future* comparison would deliver.
- **What evidence supports it?** PARTLY. §4 is constructed judgments; §5.1 is explicitly non-representative with zero tokens for three focal items; §5.2 is an audit that weakens a prior result. The paper is candid about this, but a reviewer must reconstruct the positive case.
- **What would a reviewer need to decide?** PARTLY — and the paper says so (§7 ¶8, §9.1): matched grammar fragments are required and absent. Adjudication criteria are named, not applied.

**Decision.** Clear enough to review *after* one editorial revision: rewrite the abstract to lead with the claim and its demonstrated support, add a stakes paragraph to §1, and supply the analysis materials. Prose quality is high; the obstacle is claim calibration, not clarity of expression.

**Reversal condition.** My principal objection (no stated payoff, no reviewer-checkable materials) would be overturned if the submission package includes the `analysis/` directory with a stated adjudication criterion, if Table 6 row 2 already *is* the 154-feature run under another label, or if the journal accepts framework-internal taxonomic revision as sufficient stakes without external consequences.
