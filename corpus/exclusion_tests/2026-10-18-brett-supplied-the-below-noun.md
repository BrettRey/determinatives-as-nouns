**Question.** CGEL analyses *above* as "primarily a preposition" that "also belongs (by conversion) to the adjective category", attributive in *the above discussion* and a fused modifier-head in *Her conclusion is the same as [the above]*, with the adjective restricted to location in the discourse. It adds: "Unlike *above*, *below* has no special discourse-location use: it belongs only to the preposition category" (Huddleston & Pullum 2002: 1555–1556). Does *below* occur before a noun in the sense of position in the text?

## Reading

*Below* has the attributive use for position in the text. That's the use, and the position before the head, on which CGEL bases its conversion of *above* to an adjective, so CGEL's statement that *below* "belongs only to the preposition category" is too strong. The fused-head use is in a separate record, `2026-10-18-brett-supplied-the-below-fused.md`. The two *the below average* lines are a different construction, a compound adjective heading the nominal.
