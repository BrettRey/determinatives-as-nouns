## the ADJ me (6 types, 656 tokens)

THE REAL ME 350 · THE OLD ME 129 · THE NEW ME 124 · THE BEST ME 20 · THE OTHER ME 19 · THE INNER ME 14

Probably close to clean. Accusative *me* can't be the subject of a following clause, which rules out the correlative and relative parses that swamp the *you* list. Known risk: *ME* as an abbreviation (Maine, myalgic encephalomyelitis), most likely under THE NEW ME. One display page of THE NEW ME would show this.

## the ADJ you (48 types, 5,727 tokens)

Mostly not the use. *You* is nominative as well as accusative, so the list is dominated by:

- comparative correlatives: THE LONGER YOU 732, THE CLOSER 331, THE FASTER 253, THE OLDER 248, THE HARDER 177, THE EARLIER 146, THE HIGHER 135, and most of the other comparatives (*the longer you wait*);
- superlatives and other adjectives with a relative clause: THE BEST YOU 1847 (*the best you can*), THE CLOSEST 122, THE WORST 79, THE LITTLE 17 (*the little you know*), THE GOOD 75 (probably *the good you do*), THE HURT 19 (*the hurt you caused*), THE OTHERS 49;
- prepositional idioms: THE SUDDEN 52 (*all the sudden you*), THE LEFT 37 (*on the left you*), THE OUTSIDE 21, THE MILITARY 32 (*in the military you*), THE BLUE 13 (*out of the blue you*).

Probably the use, mostly or entirely: THE REAL YOU 352, THE NEW YOU 105, THE OLD YOU 56, THE DIVINE YOU 14, THE ORIGINAL YOU 10. Mixed: THE OTHER YOU 102 (*the other you met*), THE ONLY YOU 13, and the self-help use of THE BEST YOU and THE HEALTHIER/HAPPIEST YOU sitting inside the superlative counts. None of these can go in the paper as counts without reading lines.

## Reading

*The real me* 350 and *the real you* 352 make the article-plus-adjective use of personal pronouns routine, not the wordplay the *mes* lines might suggest. Only the *me* list can be cited as a list of counts as it stands. For *you* and *him*, cite *the real you* and *the real him* after a sample of lines has been read.

## Screens: THE NEW ME (100 lines) and THE REAL YOU (198 lines)

Brett pasted one display page of each (THE REAL YOU nos. 38 and 58 absent). Classification is Claude's.

**THE NEW ME: 99 of 100 are the use.** The exception is 21, themarysue.com, "a Story Mode in the new ME game" (*Mass Effect*). Song lyrics (19, 20, 22, 76) and replacement uses (6, *Bull*, "that's Will. He's the new me"; 31, *Goon*, "you're the new me") are the use.

**THE REAL YOU: 193 of 198 are the use.** The exceptions: 1, Fox_Sunday, "the real you are seeing here" (*you* is the subject of a relative clause); 71, ibnarabisociety.org, "if you see the Real you do not abstain"; 75, snagajob.com (garbled); 125, *Dave Chappelle's Block Party*, lyric "When you bring in the real You don't get rotation"; 73, a truncated blog title. Titles that contain the phrase (*In Search of the Real You*, *Will The Real You Please Stand Up*, *The Real You Diet*) count as the use.

## Third person in the secondary use

In both lists, the pronoun in its secondary use takes third-person singular agreement and third-person anaphors, as a common noun would:

- THE REAL YOU 131, *Bazaar* (MAG, 2004): "Does the real you smell like a rose garden?"
- THE REAL YOU 122, *Good Housekeeping* (MAG, 2005): "then the real you is gon na come out"
- THE REAL YOU 129, *Virginia Quarterly Review* (FIC, 2004): "And someday the real you will wish he knew where to call"
- THE REAL YOU 45, *White Collar* (TV, 2012): "I hope the real you lives up to who you are on paper"
- THE REAL YOU 4, *The Actor* (MOV, 2019): "The real you the real you is ugly and scared"
- THE NEW ME 74, *American Beauty* screenplay (FIC, 1999): "And the new me whacks off when he feels horny"
- THE NEW ME 45, *The Inevitable Undoing of Jay Brooks* (MOV, 2007): "The new me is going to get to it as soon as he can"
- THE NEW ME 36, *Rescue Me* (TV, 2009): "I took the old me, and I crumpled her up"
- THE NEW ME 54, *San Francisco Chronicle* (NEWS, 2005): "The old me rarely left the office. The new me rarely went in."

Reading: *you* in its primary use takes plural verb forms (*you are*, *you do*) and is second person. In the secondary use, *the real you* takes *does*, *is*, *lives*, and *he*. The NP is third person singular, headed by a count noun. That fits *CGEL*'s statement that *he* and *she* in *Is it a he or a she?* "are common nouns, not pronouns" (p. 429, n. 46), and it fits §6.1's schema. The description the pronoun brings is its referent (the speaker, the addressee); its grammatical person doesn't come along.
