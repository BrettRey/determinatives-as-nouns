# Compound determinatives with an adjective: *a new everything*, *instant everything*, *a certain someone else* (COCA, 18 October 2026)
<!-- SUMMARY: four COCA searches by Brett (queries from his search history); a + adjective + everything as a count use (10 of 25 lines), adjective + bare everything as a non-count use (16 of 195), and a + adjective + someone/somebody/something + else (17 of 23); the set-denoting use keeps else, which CGEL's conversion to common noun wouldn't; else doesn't separate primary from secondary use · status: record for the taxonomic rewrite; labels Claude's, one pass · updated: 2026-10-18 -->

**Source.** COCA, three searches run by hand by Brett Reynolds on 18 October 2026, while the structural paper's *Poor everybody!* question was being discussed. Query strings, from Brett's search history (18 October 2026; truncated there, completed from the lines): `a [j*] every* .` (25 lines; the full stop after the compound), `[i*] [j*] every*` (195 lines, a display), `a [j*] someone|somebody|something else` (23 lines). Raw lines in the fusion repo's private `reviews/corpus-case-adj-20261018/raw/` (I1–I3). Labels by Claude (Opus 5.5), one pass.

**Results.**

- **Count use, *a* + adjective + *everything*:** 10 of 25 lines: *It's a different everything* (Chicago, 2009), *I'm a terrible everything* (*Heartbreakers*, 2001), *a new everything* (Rolling Stone, 2000; Denver, 1998), *a complimentary everything*, *a better everything* (twice), *a stationary everything*, *a missed everything*, *ANNOYED with a capital everything*. The rest: *everyman/everywoman/everygirl* (6), *everywhere* (3), vocatives (*Have a great, everybody*; 3), other (3).
- **Non-count use, adjective + bare *everything*:** 16 of 195 lines: *in an age of instant everything* (Quarterly Review of Distance Education, 2014; New York Times, 1991, *instant everything*), *wafts of fried everything* (Houston, 2012), *loaded with extra everything*, *We put in new everything* (Houston, 2002), *for free everything*, *mobile everything*, *online everything*, *artificial everything*, *instantaneous everything*, *realistic everything*, *dirty everything*, *undetermined everything*, *original everything*, *cold everything else*. The rest are *every day*, *everyday*, verbs tagged as adjectives (*convincing everyone*), and vocatives. No line has an adjective before bare *everybody* or *everyone*.
- **With *else*:** 17 of 23 lines have an article, an adjective, the compound, and *else*: *a certain someone else* (*Community*, 2010; *Frasier*, 1998, twice), *a certain somebody else* (*Antioch Review*, 2010), *a little something else* (5 lines), *a new someone else* (PBS, 1990), *a new something else* (*The Wire*, 2003), *a specific someone else*, *a possible someone else*, *A dead somebody else*, *a Happy something else*, and *Either I would be a better me, or I would be a better somebody else* (Atlanta, 2007), where the pronoun's secondary use stands beside the compound's. The other 6 are relative clauses (*a toy someone else has*), a verb, and garbled lines.

**Bearing (taxonomic paper).** In the combined manuscript's terms (§ "Secondary set-denoting uses"), the first and third sets are the compounds' secondary set-denoting use, and the second looks like a non-count version of it, which the manuscript's rule (article, pre-head adjective, plural) doesn't yet state. The *else* lines bear on two points. First, CGEL's conversion (p. 423 n. 43) makes these common nouns, and common nouns don't take *else* (\**a person else*); the secondary-use analysis keeps the lexeme and its own dependents, so *a certain someone else* favours no change of category. Second, they half-answer the manuscript's untested footnote: the set-denoting use keeps a post-head dependent of the compound's own. Whether it keeps the post-head restrictor (*a certain someone special*) is still untested; `a little something [j*]` would test it.

**Correction (structural paper discussion).** Claude suggested on 18 October that *else* separates the primary use from the secondary one. These lines show it doesn't: both keep it. Only meaning (whether quantificational force survives) separates them. *Poor everyone else* (NOW) keeps universal force, so it's the primary use, with the exclamative adjective personal pronouns take (*Lucky you!*; CGEL p. 430).

## The post-head restrictor (later the same day)

**Source.** COCA, a fourth search by Brett, `a [j*] somone|somebody|something [j*]` (50 lines; raw I4). The history shows *someone* misspelled as *somone*, so this search didn't look for *someone* at all. It tests the combined manuscript's footnote: "Whether the set-denoting use still admits the post-head restrictor (*a certain someone special*) is a judgment I haven't tested."

**Result.** 46 of 50 lines have an article, a pre-head adjective, the compound, and a following adjective, all with *something*. 43 are *a little something* + adjective (*extra* in most; *special*, *sweet*, *different*, *new*, *small*, *nice happening*), CGEL's own conversion example (p. 423 n. 43). The other three: *There's a certain something special about Peace Corps volunteers* (cafnrnews.com, 2012), *a special something unique to African sensitivity and logic* (*Latin American Research Review*, 1996), *a pre-emptive something fabulous* (CBS, 2007). Excluded: *a hooded somebody --*, *a floating something --*, *a important somebody round town* (no following adjective), and *asking a patient something important* (two NPs).

**Bearing.** The footnote's question is answered yes for *something*: the set-denoting use keeps the compound's own post-head restrictor, as it keeps *else*. Common nouns don't take a bare single adjective after the head, so this is a second dependent of the compound's own that survives, again against CGEL's conversion and for a secondary use with no change of category. The evidence beyond the lexicalized *a little something* is three lines. None has *somebody*, and *someone* wasn't searched (the typo), so the result says nothing yet about the personal compounds.

**Searches in the history not yet reported here:** `a [j*] no one|nobody|nothing …`, `a [j*] anyone|anybody|anything …`, `[v*] [j*] every*`, and `a [j*] everybody .` (all COCA, 18 October 2026). Their lines weren't pasted.
