# *partly* and *largely* with material nouns, with and without a modifier function (18 October 2026)
<!-- SUMMARY: test of whether degree words like partly go with the modifier function of material nouns (Matthews 2014: 28–29); Brett's judgments plus one web attestation (This lava is partly magma) show partly also where the noun has no modifier function · status: record · updated: 2026-10-18 -->

**Question.** Matthews (2014: 28–29) notes that material nouns used as modifiers take degree words such as *partly* and *largely* (*a partly cotton shirt*, *It has lots of largely glass walls*), and that "There are no phrases, in comparison, such as a partly cotton or these largely glasses". If *partly* occurred only with the modifier function, it would be a property of form that varies with that function. The structural paper's criterion would then license Mod–Head fusion for *nylon* in *I prefer cotton shirts to nylon* (CGEL p. 417).

**Judgments (Brett Reynolds, 18 October 2026, NEEDS-FROM-BRETT M4 and M5 in the fusion repo).**

| Example | Function of the material noun | Judgment |
|---|---|---|
| *I prefer pure cotton shirts to partly nylon* | fused use (CGEL: modifier-head) | good |
| *I prefer cotton to partly nylon* | head of an NP naming the material (CGEL p. 421) | good |
| *This shirt is partly nylon* | head of an NP functioning as predicative complement | good |
| *The frame is largely steel* | head of an NP functioning as predicative complement | good |

**Attestation.** "This lava is partly magma." Supplied by Brett from <https://www.conserve-energy-future.com/geothermalenergy.php> (no date shown).

**Reading.** *Partly* occurs where the noun has no modifier function, so it isn't tied to that function. It also modifies predicates that aren't about composition (*It's partly a question of money*). The structural paper's §2 footnote reports the test, and its claim stands that nothing in *I prefer cotton shirts to nylon* varies with the modifier function. Matthews's own explanation, that the modifier use is an adjective by conversion (p. 29), is reported there, not adopted.
