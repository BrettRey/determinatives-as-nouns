## Reading

**Contexts 2–4 put *me* in the secondary use and inflect it for plural.** *Mes* takes a cardinal (*three mes*, *ten mes*, *one me*), a demonstrative-like determiner (*them two mes*), and in context 2 an article with a pre-head adjective (*the good me*, *the same me*). That is the whole bundle of §6.1's schema: plural inflection, an article, a pre-head adjective. What *mes* denotes fits the schema's wording, "the set of individuals fitting its description": individuals each of whom is the speaker (two versions after being cut in half; a body three times the speaker's size; ten selves rattling around in one).

Consequence: the §6.1 footnote's "plural inflection isn't attested for this input" was false for *me*, and was revised the same day. *Hes and shes* still returns nothing, so the gap is specific to the *he*/*she* plurals in that spelling, not the pronoun input. The schema's gloss "the individuals of the pronoun's gender" also fits *he* and *she* but not *me*, whose description is person, not gender.

**Context 1 is not a pronoun.** *Yous* is capitalized and sits in a list of characters who share voice actors, so it reads as the plural of a character name, the use of *the Smiths*. (From memory, not from the source: *Ever17* has a character nicknamed *You*, and two characters carry that name. This is unverified.)
