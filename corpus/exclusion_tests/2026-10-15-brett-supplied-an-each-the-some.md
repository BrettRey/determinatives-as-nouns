## Reading

The excluded side of the schema can't be tested by string search in general (Brett: *a most* returns *a most dire situation*), and these two strings confirm it: nearly all hits are other constructions. But *the some* turned up something positive. *Some*, and in line 62 *all*, take *the* and denote a set of people when contrasted with *the many* or with each other, in edited prose in one case (*Foreign Affairs*). That is the pattern of *the few* and *the many*, not the article-plus-description secondary use of §6.1. It falsifies the sharper property GLM-5.3 proposed (only a cardinality qualifies, so *some* and *all* are excluded), at least in contrast frames.

## Follow-up: `the some who` (1 line)

Brett ran `the some who` to test whether set-denoting *the some* needs a contrast frame. One hit in COCA, *ABC Nightline*, 2 May 1990, "Child Labor Abuse in the United States" (SPOK). Relevant sentences only, from the expanded context:

- WILLIAM BROOKS: "There are some who say that's five percent."
- KOPPEL: "Well, the some who say it is the General Accounting Office-"

No contrast with *many* or *all*: Koppel takes up Brooks's indefinite *some who say* and re-mentions it with *the*, as one re-mentions *a man who says* as *the man who says*. So "only in a contrast frame" fails. What licenses *the* in all three clear lines is that the set is already identified, by its complement (*the all*, *the many*) or by prior mention. The single hit across the whole corpus fits that: bare *some* normally introduces an unidentified set, which clashes with *the*.

## Follow-ups: `the some of us who` (COCA) and `the some who` (NOW)

COCA `the some of us who`: one hit, line 137 above (CBS_SunMorn, SPOK, 2010), now with more context: "worth sacrificing because our country needs some of us to sacrifice, the some of us who can . And we, Mister Stein, we are not suffering." Clear: the speaker re-mentions indefinite *some of us* with *the* and a restrictive relative. Line 137 moves from ambiguous to clear, so COCA has four clear lines (62, 176, *Nightline*, 137). The pattern is the + determinative + *of* + personal pronoun, the same as *CGEL*'s *the both of us* (p. 377).

NOW `the some who`: 10 tokens. Brett reports that NOW won't display the lines for this string because every word in it is so frequent, so they're unscreened. Frequency by the next word: ARE 4, WONT 1, RUSH 1, RUN 1, MIGHT 1, LIKE 1, GIVE 1.

*CGEL* sources checked against the PDF (printed pages): fused heads are licensed when "the expression in fused-head position can occur in the dependent position in NPs where it is not fused with the head. Thus *some* occurs with just the determiner function in *some sausages*" (p. 410); determinatives that are "internal modifier as well as determiner" fuse as modifier-head (p. 415; *the many who did*, p. 416); *the both of us* "is exceptional in that *both* cannot follow *the* (i.e. cannot occur in internal modifier function) in NPs with a noun head" (p. 377); *some* "approximately" (*some thirty applications*) is "a different word, belonging to the adverb category", and *some* has a "special fused-head use" meaning "some people" (both p. 385).

## The deciding comparison: iWeb `the some people` against `the some who`

Brett's iWeb frequency display: THE SOME PEOPLE 53, THE SOME WHO 10. He pasted 50 *the some people* lines (nos. 18, 27, 28, 39, 41, 49 absent) and the *the some who* lines again (9, renumbered 1–9; the same lines as above).

Classification of the 50 *the some people* lines (Claude's reading):

- ***the* for *that*, 15**: 2, 3, 10, 20, 23, 25, 26, 35, 38, 40, 42, 44, 46, 52, 54, e.g. "the fact the some people don't care" (35), "My suspicion is the some people pay a lot more" (26), "the evil the some people are filled with" (3), "keep in mind the some people have a fetish for braces" (46).
- **typo for *same*, 2**: 19 ("the some people are making both the bass AND the strings"), 30 ("ran by the some people that run Mutts").
- **titles or garbled text, 9**: 14, 15, 24, 32, 33, 34 (*Some People are Gay. Get Over It!* campaign), 36, 45, 48.
- ***the* before what reads as plain indefinite *some people*, 24**: 1, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 11, 12, 13, 16, 17, 21, 22, 29, 31, 37, 43, 47, 50, 51, 53, 55, 56, e.g. "Contrary to the some people's belief" (12), "according to the some people in the business" (53). The closest to a contrast: 8, wgt.com, "the some people can't hope to play while others seem unaffected"; 5, answers.com, "the some people will get lower prices. … some will end up paying higher".

None of the 50 is in a frame like *one of the some who*, *out of the some who*, or *the some will become the many*. In the *the some who* lines, *the* does definite work: a contrast with *the all*, *the many*, or *90% of you*; a partitive, *one of*/*out of*; a restrictive relative. In the *the some people* lines, it doesn't: it stands where *that* or nothing would.

Reading: the form a fusion analysis needs as its licence (*the* + modifier *some* + noun) shows up only as noise, while the fused-looking form (*the some who*) carries the definite use. That fits *CGEL*'s own description of dependent *some* as having "just the determiner function" (p. 410), and leaves *the some who* unlicensed under fusion.
