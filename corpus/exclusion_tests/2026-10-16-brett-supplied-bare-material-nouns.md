**What's being tested.** *CGEL* rejects ordinary Head for *nylon* in *I prefer cotton shirts to nylon* on interpretation alone ("understood as having modifier function", p. 421). It restricts fused modifier-heads to listed types (composition *nylon*, p. 417; \**I prefer the sociology*, p. 415), and says the NPs "are either bare or have a definite determiner", with "significant loss of acceptability when the determiner is indefinite" (p. 417; *?a nylon*). The fusion paper's alternative: the noun functions as ordinary Head, and its determiners follow its own countability.

**Bearing.**
- **Bare non-count nouns for one item are routine.** The pattern turns up across materials and sites, and in each case the noun stands for a single count item while staying bare. That's the determiner pattern of the non-count noun itself.
- **Fusion has nothing to say here.** *CGEL*'s fusion puts no understood noun in the syntax, and its stated restriction ("bare or ... a definite determiner") permits bare forms anyway. So these lines don't separate the two analyses on bareness alone.
- **Countability does the work.** With count nouns, bare forms are out (\**I'll take dog*, Brett's judgment) and *a* is fine (*a bathroom*, *a felony*, *a Poisson*). *CGEL*'s restriction gets both of those wrong. The noun's own countability as Head gets both right.
