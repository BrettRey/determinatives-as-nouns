# *Scant* and *ever*: COCA, 19 October 2026

**Source.** COCA collocates search, *scant* in the word box and *ever* in the collocate box (the site's default window), run by hand by Brett Reynolds on 19 October 2026. Five KWIC lines; the raw lines are kept privately in the fusion repo (`reviews/corpus-scant-ever-20261019/raw.txt`, gitignored).

**Why.** CGEL says "while a determiner can mark a clause as negative, an internal modifier never can" (p. 394). It lists *scant attention* among attributive quantifying adjectives (p. 556), and leaves *scant* off its seven approximate negators (p. 815). Brett's constructed *Scant attention has ever been paid to this problem* and *Scant attention was paid to this problem, was it?* would make *scant* an internal modifier that marks a clause negative.

**Result.**

| Line | Where *ever* is |
|---|---|
| *with scant hope that the damage will ever be repaired* (*Atlanta*, 2005) | in a complement clause of *hope*, inside the NP |
| *Biggers had scant hope of ever finding him* (fiction, 2002) | in the complement of *hope*, inside the NP |
| *there is scant hope that patrols could ever find support* (*CS Monitor*, 1991) | in a complement clause of *hope*, inside the NP |
| *leave behind such a scant record that it was ever here* (*Natural History*, 2007) | in a complement clause of *record*, inside the NP, after *such a* |
| *a scant 1.7% … close to its lowest level ever* (*Money*, 1999) | licensed by the superlative; irrelevant |

**Bearing.** *Scant* licenses *ever* within its own NP, as a negative quantity word should, and it still does after *such a*. But no line has *ever* in the main clause, so the corpus doesn't show *scant* making a clause negative. That rests on Brett's judgments of the tag and the main-clause NPI. Licensing inside the NP isn't CGEL's test: it is clausal (p. 394, with tags).

**Result.**

| inside the NP *scant* modifies | 5 | *scant hope of ever providing* (l. 8), *scant evidence that corn ever grew there* (latimes.com, l. 16) |
| irrelevant (superlative *ever*, a poem, *if ever*) | 5 | ll. 2, 4, 6, 7, 13 |

**Bearing.** Brett's constructed *Scant attention has ever been paid to this problem* is attested almost word for word, twice, once on a museum's site. So an NP whose only quantity word is the adjective *scant* licenses *ever* in its clause, as an NP with determiner *few* does. Modifier *few* after *the* doesn't (*The few people who came had ever been there*, constructed; judgment pending). So this property follows the word and the absence of a definite determiner, not determiner against modifier function.

Caveat: licensing *ever* shows a non-affirmative context, not necessarily a negative clause. *Only* licenses it too, and CGEL's diagnostic for negative clauses is the reversed-polarity tag (Ch. 9, p. 787), which Brett isn't confident about with *scant*. So CGEL's claim that "an internal modifier never can" mark a clause negative (p. 394) isn't refuted by these lines. What they show is that the closely related *ever* test doesn't track the function.

## iWeb, *scant* with *never* (19 October 2026)

Six lines (raw in `reviews/corpus-scant-ever-20261019/raw-iweb-never.txt`), none with attributive *scant* and *never* in the same clause:
- 3 have predicative *scant*, with *never* in another clause (*info is really scant and we may never know about him*, invisionzone.com);
- 1 coordinates the adjectives (*a scant, never updated LinkedIn profile*);
- 1 is a title (*a scant two weeks after Never Say Never*);
- 1 has the verb *scant* (a poem).

Nothing here bears on whether *scant* makes its clause negative.

**Query, as Brett described it.** All three searches were collocate searches, *scant* in one box and *ever* or *never* in the other, so the iWeb pair is like for like. iWeb gives 16 lines for *ever*: 10 with *ever* licensed in the clause or NP of *scant* (5 in the clause, 5 inside the NP). It gives 6 for *never*, none with attributive *scant* and *never* in one clause. Whether that asymmetry is evidence turns on two things not yet checked: the overall iWeb frequencies of *ever* and *never*, and the same pair of searches for a positive control adjective.

**Base frequencies (iWeb, Brett, 19 October 2026).** *never* 6,529,668 and *ever* 4,129,990, so *never* is about 1.6 times as frequent. Under the null that a collocate of *scant* is *never* or *ever* in proportion to their overall frequency, about 13.5 of the 22 lines would be *never*; 6 are (binomial P ≈ 0.001). If all 10 relevant lines, with the collocate in *scant*'s clause or NP, were drawn by the same proportions, the chance that none is *never* is about 0.0001. The positive control (*ample* with *ever* and *never*) is still to run.

## NOW, the same two collocate searches (19 October 2026)

**Source.** NOW, *scant* in the word box with *ever*, and then *never*, in the collocate box; run by Brett. The paste has 42 lines for *ever* (rows 8, 22–24, 26 absent) and 12 for *never* (row 9 absent). Labels by Claude, one pass. Lines that repeat iWeb lines or each other are counted once.

**ever (42 lines).**
- *Ever* in the clause of bare *scant*, with no other licensor: 5 new lines.
  - *scant regard was ever given to the people who lived there* (Northern Daily Leader, AU, 2023, l. 14)
  - *scant attention has ever been paid to the unprecedented grouping …* (thejournal.ie, 2018, l. 30)
  - *very scant evidence was ever collected* (Daily Mail, 2013, l. 43)
  - *because scant few are ever called* (Sioux City Journal, 2017, l. 36)
  - *in a fashion that scant few transfers ever have* (footballfancast.com, l. 2)
  - Not counted: l. 44, the iWeb *A scant 306 people* line again.
- *Ever* in the clause, but *only* or a question could license it: 4 (ll. 9, 13, 19, 25).
- *Ever* inside the NP that bare *scant* modifies: 11 new lines (*scant hope they will ever get it back*, *scant risk I'd ever need to sell*, *scant signs they ever will* …). Not counted: l. 21, which repeats l. 17, and ll. 37, 39, 42, and 45, which repeat iWeb.
- After *the*, inside the NP: 1 (*among the scant few people who ever bought a Wii U*, l. 10).
- Irrelevant: 14 (*scant ever since*, 10; *as scant as ever*, 2; *ever revolving*; *Ever wondered*).

**never (12 lines).**
- *Never* in the clause or NP of bare *scant*: 1, marginal: *however scant mention of him never joining Umno Baru* (Malay Mail, l. 12). *Never* sits inside a gerund in the complement, and the prose is garbled.
- *Never* in the clause of an NP where *scant* follows a definite (genitive) determiner: 1, *Ghana's scant resources "can never be safe …"* (Myjoyonline, 2017, l. 10).
- Different clause, a list, or a title: 10.

**Bearing, iWeb and NOW together.** *Ever* falls in the clause or NP of bare *scant* in 26 distinct lines: 10 in the clause and 16 inside the NP. *Never* does in 1 marginal line. Yet *never* is the commoner word (iWeb: 6.5 against 4.1 million). After a definite determiner the pattern lifts: *Ghana's scant resources can never be safe*. Modifier *few* behaves the same way after *the* (CGEL p. 394).

So the adjective *scant*, an internal modifier for CGEL (p. 556), attracts *ever* and repels *never* in its clause, as determiner *few* does. And it stops after a definite determiner, as *few* does. That puts the polarity contrast with the word and the definite determiner, not with determiner against modifier function. It's distributional evidence against CGEL's "an internal modifier never can" (p. 394) that doesn't rely on the tag. A positive control (*ample*) hasn't been run.
