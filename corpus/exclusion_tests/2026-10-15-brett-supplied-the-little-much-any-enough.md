## `the little that` (96 lines): the control

Every line has independent *little* after *the* with a quantity reading ("the small amount that"), heading a nominal with a relative clause. Line 30 is a garbled transcript. The construction is CGEL's own *the little money that remains* without the noun:

- "The sugar in the bowl is nearly gone, the little that remains marked by the girl's finger." (3, FIC 2018)
- "I know very little but the little that I do know could help you." (47, BLOG 2012)
- "you're saying this is consistent with the little bit, the little that's known about what the plane did" (7, SPOK 2016), a self-repair from *the little bit* to *the little*.

## `the any` (94 lines): negative control

## `the enough` (35 lines): negative control

- **Names and titles (19):** the Enough Project (3–5, 7–13, 18, 20, 21, 32, 33); *Enough is Enough* (1, 24); *Enough Said* (25); *the Enough already! sentiment* (6).
- **Typos and non-native text (13):** 2, 14, 16, 17, 19, 22, 23, 26, 27, 30, 31, 34, 35 (*the enough knowledge*, *the enough cash*).
- **Nominal *enough* with a relative clause (3, two writers):**
  - "to seeing the enough that is already here, all around me" (28, BLOG 2012)
  - "More food, more time, more stuff. I will look for the enough that only gets buried beneath all of that more." (29, same post)
  - "it is the enough that defines me. I am adequate." (36, FIC 2000, after "I'm smart enough")

The three are conversions with an abstract reading, "sufficiency" or "adequacy": 29 converts *more* in the same breath (*all of that more*), and 36 picks up degree *enough* from *smart enough*. Line 28 alone could be read as a quantity ("the sufficient amount already here"), but it comes from the same post as 29. None quantifies over an identified set.

## Bearing

1. ***Much* can't test anything.** CGEL: "the plain form *much* is largely restricted to non-affirmative contexts" (§7.11, p. 393), eight lines above the "Except for *much*" sentence the design note built test 3 on. *The much that remains* is affirmative, so its absence follows from the polarity restriction under either analysis. The one hit (line 46) has an affirmative *much* licensed by the parallel with *the little that is said*. The design note's row "Ordinary Head expects: yes (*the much that remains*)" was written without that sentence; it was a design error, now corrected in the note.
2. ***Any* behaves as a negative control.** None of the 94 lines has the independent determinative.
3. ***Enough* gives no independent use either.** Fusion has no licence after *the* anyway: "The sufficiency determinatives are not permitted in pre-head modifier position" (CGEL p. 397). The three hits are conversions and don't count as fused or ordinary-Head uses of the determinative.
4. **What conversion looks like.** The label uses of *any* (3, 5, 14) and the abstract nominal *enough* (28, 29, 36) join *somes* (`2026-10-15-brett-supplied-somes.md`): converted determinatives name a category or a property and don't quantify. *The some who say it* keeps its quantificational reading ("those of the people who say it"). This is further evidence against the conversion repair for *the some who* (structural paper).
5. ***Both*.** 1437 hits for *the both of* in COCA is far from marginal (CGEL p. 377: "used by some speakers in informal style"), but 99% of them have a personal pronoun after *of*. A frequent construction fixed to one frame is what a listed exception is for, so *the both of us* costs fusion little and doesn't help the argument that exceptions become the rule.
6. ***All*.** One fictional line, *the all of me that's broken*, in the same frame as *the some of us who*. Like *some*, it would need a fused modifier-head without, as far as CGEL says, an internal-modifier use. One line from one writer can't carry an argument.
7. **Test 2.** The five coordination strings returned nothing in COCA, so the corpus gives no evidence either way. The constituency test now depends on judgments.

## Where test 3 stands

The inventory doesn't yield the argument the design note hoped for. *Much* is confounded, *both* is a fixed frame, *all* has one line, and the controls give nothing. That leaves *some* as the one productive dissociation (`2026-10-15-brett-supplied-an-each-the-some.md`), which is where the structural paper's argument already stood. What the inventory does show is that the *some* case isn't an artefact of the search: the controls stay empty, and the conversions found (*somes*, label *any*, nominal *enough*) don't look like *the some who*.
