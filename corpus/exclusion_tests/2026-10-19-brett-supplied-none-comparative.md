**Why it matters.** CGEL defines restrictors as AdjPs or nominals "without any post-head dependents of their own" (p. 455). It derives their position from fusion: because determiner and head are fused, a compound like *nothing* can't take pre-head dependents, so their counterparts follow the head (*No sensible ideas* / *Nothing sensible*, p. 423). Of the fused-head construction it says there are "no structural constraints on postpositive AdjPs" (p. 552). Payne et al. count the account "a particular strength" of fusion (p. 583). On that reasoning, every fused determiner-head should take a restrictor, simple determinatives standing alone included.

AdjPs with a *than*-phrase have a dependent of their own, so they follow common nouns too (*a leader younger than you*, CGEL p. 552). They don't bear on the restrictor. The bare comparatives have the restrictor's shape (CGEL p. 455).

**Bearing.** Simple determinatives standing alone are fused determiner-heads for CGEL, as the compounds are. Yet a single adjective follows them only if it's comparative. So fusion isn't sufficient for the restrictor, and CGEL's derivation predicts more than occurs. That adds to the point already in §3 of the structural paper, that fusion isn't necessary for it either, since the restrictor survives conversion to a noun (*a little something extra*). On both analyses the restrictor has to be stated for the words that take it. The comparatives may postpose because their *than*-phrase is understood. In existentials (*There were none braver*), the AdjP may lie outside the NP.

**Much, little, and all (Brett, 19 October).** *There's little new in the report*, *Not much interesting happened*, and *All interesting was sold* are all good. So the words that take a plain restrictor are the compounds plus *all*, *much*, and *little*. CGEL groups these same words with the compounds for *else* (p. 423). *Few*, *some*, and *none* take a single adjective only if it's comparative. The split is lexical, and fusion doesn't predict it: CGEL's analysis fuses all of these words alike.

**Applied, 19 October.** §3 of the structural paper now says fusion isn't sufficient for the restrictor's position, and Table 1's restrictor row reflects it.

**Added 19 October 2026.** GPT Pro (fourth review) pointed out *Few present objected* and *None present raised an objection*. Brett judges *None present objected* good. *Present* follows nouns as well (*the people present*), so the restriction is that a single adjective follows *few*, *some*, or *none* only if it's comparative or one that follows nouns too. §3 now uses *I've heard worse but none weirder* (l. 51, an object NP) in place of the existential *There's none lovelier*, where the AdjP may lie outside the NP.
