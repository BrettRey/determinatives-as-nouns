# *A* + adjective + *once* / *twice* (COCA, 18 October 2026)
<!-- SUMMARY: one COCA hand search; 97 lines; no line has an adjective before once or twice heading a nominal after a; adjective + once occurs only in the noun once-over (17 lines) and in once-in-a-lifetime (3); so the a lucky few pattern doesn't extend to once · status: done · updated: 2026-10-18 -->

**Question.** §4 of the structural paper (*Fusion of functions revisited*) finds that *few* takes an adjective after *a* (*a lucky few*), which costs both analyses a statement. Payne, Huddleston & Pullum (2007: 589) exclude \**last once* but allow *this last once*. Does *once* or *twice* take an adjective after *a*, as *few* does?

## Search: COCA

Brett Reynolds pasted 97 concordance lines (numbered 1–97). Query `a [j*] once|twice` (inferred from the lines and confirmed by Brett, 18 October). The display holds up to 100 lines, so these may be all the hits, but the total wasn't pasted. Years 1990–2019, all genres.

Classification by Claude (Opus 5.5), reading every line:

| Reading | Lines |
|---|---|
| *Once* in the noun *once-over*, after an adjective (*a quick once-over*, *a good once over*) | 17: *quick* 9, *good* 3, *slow* 2, *serious* 1, *sizzling* 1, *cool* 1 |
| *Once* in the compound modifier *once-in-a-lifetime* (*a rare once in a lifetime kind of patient*) | 3: *near*, *rare*, *true* |
| The word after *a* heads the NP *a* begins, and *once* or *twice* is outside it (adjunct, *once more*, *once again*, *once and for all*, the preposition *once*) | 77 |
| An adjective before *once* or *twice* heading a nominal after *a* | 0 |

Examples of the 77: *I saw a psychic once* (*Lost Souls*, 2000); *We had a physical once a year* (*Houston*, newspaper, 1992); *The country has elected a communist twice* (redstate.com, 2012); *a Russian once ranked No. 1* (*New York Times*, 2015); *Freddy was a stranger once more* (*Analog*, 2016); *arriving at a major once again* (*New York Times*, 2003). *Once-over* lines include *give his car a good once over* (*Michael Clayton*, 2007) and *gave us a good once over* (*American Indian Quarterly*, 2004). Line 18, *A wise once told me* (*Men in Black 3*, 2012, a film transcript), has *wise* after *a* and is about *wise*, not *once*.

## Reading

No line has *once* or *twice* heading a nominal after *a* with an adjective, so the *a lucky few* pattern doesn't extend to them in this sample. The search doesn't test *a* + *once* with no adjective, and nothing here shows that *once* takes no *a* at all.
