# Affective adjective + bare compound determinative (*Poor everybody!*): COCA, 18 October 2026
<!-- SUMMARY: 28 COCA lines for poor|lucky|silly + everybody|everyone|somebody|someone; 2 are the exclamative use with no determiner (Poor everybody., New Yorker 2005; Lucky you. - Lucky everyone., Everwood 2004), 19 are lucky + clause, 4 predicative, 1 with a determiner, 2 other · status: record; labels Claude's · updated: 2026-10-18 -->

**Source.** COCA, query `poor|lucky|silly everybody|everyone|somebody|someone`, run by hand by Brett Reynolds on 18 October 2026 at Claude's request (fusion repo NEEDS-FROM-BRETT M3). Brett pasted 28 KWIC lines. The raw lines and labels are kept privately in the fusion repo (`reviews/corpus-poor-everybody-20261018/`, gitignored). Classification by Claude (Opus 5.5).

**Why it was run.** CGEL analyses the compounds as fused determiner-heads and says "Because determiner and head are fused it is not possible for these forms to take internal pre-head dependents" (p. 423). Personal pronouns take a few affective adjectives in exclamations (*Lucky you!*, *poor old me*; CGEL p. 430). The search asks whether the compounds do too.

**Result.**

| Label | Lines | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Adjective directly before a bare compound, exclamation | 2 | *Poor Val. Poor everybody.* (*New Yorker*, 2005, fiction, l. 19); *Lucky you. - Lucky everyone.* (*Everwood*, 2004, TV, l. 22) |
| *lucky* + finite clause | 19 | *You're lucky someone called it in* (*New England Review*, 2016, l. 3) |
| predicative | 4 | *How poor everyone had been after the war* (*New England Review*, 2006, l. 18) |
| determiner before the adjective | 1 | *I made him the lucky someone* (*Dorian Blues*, 2004, l. 21), a noun by conversion like CGEL's *a nobody* (p. 423 n. 43) |
| list, depictive | 2 | *Rich, poor everyone gets a tax break* (l. 12); *If you live poor everyone gets sick* (l. 11) |

**Bearing.** Both uses are exclamations with a compound whose base is personal (*·body*, *·one*), and l. 22 follows *Lucky you* directly. Simple determinatives don't take the construction (Brett, 18 October: \**Lucky few!* bad). So the compounds pattern with the personal pronouns here, against CGEL's "not possible". Under ordinary Head, *everybody* and *everyone* join CGEL's lexical exception beside the personal pronouns. Under fusion, the adjective precedes a fused determiner-head, which adjacency excludes, so fusion needs another analysis of the compound in this use or an exception to adjacency. Two attestations are few, but one is in edited fiction. No *somebody* or *someone* line has the use.

## NOW and iWeb (18 October 2026, later)

**Source.** The same query (confirmed from Brett's search history) in NOW (frequency 294; 188 KWIC lines displayed) and iWeb (frequency 220; 188 lines), run by hand by Brett. Raw lines and labels in the fusion repo's private `reviews/corpus-case-adj-20261018/` (sets C1–C4). Labels by Claude (Opus 5.5), one pass.

**Why.** After the *a lucky few* reanalysis, *Poor everybody!* is the one exception left to fusion's derivation of the adjective gap, and GPT Pro suggested both COCA cases are echoes of a preceding name or pronoun exclamation.

**Result.** Most lines are *a lucky someone* (with a determiner) or *lucky* + clause (*We're lucky someone saw the fire*). The exclamation:

| without such a series | 2: *Lauren observed: 'Poor everyone,'* (University of Chicago Chronicle, 2021, l. 73); the song title *Poor Everybody Else* (Carrie Underwood, 2022; ll. 52, 53, 65, 71), an exclamation in the same frame | 0 |

A same-day re-run of the iWeb frequency list gave 208, not 220, the whole difference in *lucky someone* (138, was 150); NOW's re-run was identical. The exclamation lines are unaffected. One NOW line has the adjective and compound as an NP outside an exclamation, in non-native prose (*they were Soviet type old fashion with poor everyone*, ndtv.com, 2023, l. 47). No *lucky* or *silly* exclamation turned up in either corpus.

**Bearing.** GPT Pro's reading is mostly right. Across the three corpora the exclamation closes a series after a name or noun in seven of nine cases (COCA 2, NOW 2, iWeb 3, not counting the repeated line). But it also occurs on its own twice, so it's a real use, if a marginal one, and nearly always with *poor*. iWeb l. 70 shows the series frame admitting a bare singular count noun too (*Poor girl, poor husband*), so the frame isn't specific to the compounds.
