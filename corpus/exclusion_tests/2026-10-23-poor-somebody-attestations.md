# *Poor somebody!* and *poor somebody else*: two attestations outside the corpora (23 October 2026)
<!-- SUMMARY: the exclamative adjective with somebody, which COCA, NOW and iWeb didn't show: 'Poor somebody!' (Anstey 1884, ch. XIII, p. 146) and "like poor somebody else" (Binchy, Irish Times 1996); found by GPT Pro, checked by Claude in the source texts · status: record · updated: 2026-10-23 -->

**Why.** The 18 October record (`2026-10-18-brett-supplied-adj-compound-exclamatives.md`) found the exclamation *Poor everybody!* and *Lucky everyone!* in COCA, NOW and iWeb, but no line with *somebody* or *someone* in the use. In the lines displayed there, *poor somebody* and *poor someone* occur only as degree uses (*how poor somebody is*). Brett judged *Poor somebody!* "no worse than *poor everybody*" (21 October). GPT Pro, pasted by Brett on 23 October, supplied the two texts below. Claude checked both on 23 October.

**1. The exclamation.** F. Anstey, *The Giant's Robe*, third edition (London: Smith, Elder, 1884), chapter XIII, p. 146. Checked in the Project Gutenberg plain text (eBook 27507, line 5926) and against the page markers of its HTML edition (Pg 146).

> 'Mr. Ashburn and I were just discussing somebody's character,' said Miss Featherstone, by way of ending an awkward pause.
>
> 'Poor somebody!' drawled Caffyn, …
>
> 'You need not pity her,' said Gilda, indignantly; 'we were _defending_ her.'

The novel is in the public domain. *Somebody* refers to the woman under discussion, as the reply shows, and it echoes *somebody's* in the previous turn. That's the same echoing context as the corpus cases: each comes just after an exclamation with a name or pronoun, or after the same word.

**2. The adjective inside a clause.** Maeve Binchy, "Go easy on the feelgood factor", *The Irish Times*, 20 January 1996, <https://www.irishtimes.com/news/go-easy-on-the-feelgood-factor-1.23831>, checked on the page:

> "At least he didn't have a long illness like poor somebody else."

This one isn't an exclamation: *poor somebody else* is the complement of *like*. CGEL p. 430 notes that the pronoun construction "can be integrated into clause structure" (*by poor old me*).

**Bearing.** These are attestations, not corpus counts. They support coding the exclamative-adjective feature as present for *somebody*, as for *everybody* and *everyone*. Nothing here bears on *someone*.
