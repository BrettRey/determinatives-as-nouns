# Restrictive adjective + independent *few*: what precedes it (COCA, 16 Oct 2026)
<!-- SUMMARY: 368 hand-gathered COCA lines for lucky/chosen/fortunate/remaining/priveledged few; of 321 independent uses, all but three have the, a demonstrative, a genitive, we/us, some, or peripheral a before the adjective; the bare type (*Lucky few escaped) is absent outside a headline, a fragment and one web line · status: done · updated: 2026-10-16 -->

**Source.** COCA, gathered by hand by Brett Reynolds on 16 Oct 2026, to test the adjacency point raised in the fusion repo's `reviews/muse-wonder-second-mother-20261016/` (Muse, Fable 5.1, GPT-Pro). Frequency list: LUCKY FEW 262, CHOSEN FEW 170, FORTUNATE FEW 109, REMAINING FEW 64, PRIVELEDGED FEW 2 (total 607). The query used the misspelling *priveledged*, so correctly spelled *privileged few* wasn't searched. The KWIC paste has 368 lines, numbered to 373, with ll. 54, 63, 64, 118 and 289 absent; it isn't the full 607. Labels are in `2026-10-16-adj-few.labels.txt`. Classification by Claude (Opus 5.5).

**Coding.**
- *Use*: I = *few* heads the NP (no following noun); D = *few* + noun (*the remaining few months*), set aside.
- *Prec*: what stands before the adjective: THE, A (peripheral *a*), DEM, GEN, PRON (*we*/*us*), SOME, OTHER, BARE, FRAG (fragment), HEADLINE, NAME.

**Independent uses, 321.**

| Before the adjective | Lines |

| peripheral *a* | 90 |
| demonstrative (*these*, *those*, once *that*) | 25 |
| genitive (*its*, *my*, *his*, *Hollywood's*, *St. John's*) | 6 |

The three with nothing before the adjective are:

- a headline: "Asylum for Lucky Few" (*New York Times* 1991, l. 367)
- a fragment: "some in Hawaii... lucky few..." (blog 2012, l. 172)
- one web line: "other less fortunate few do not accept the change well" (goodreads 2012, l. 103), probably a run-on missing a comma, with *other* as the only element before *less fortunate*

*Remaining* occurs independently only after *the* (8 lines: *The remaining few were the hangers-on*, *Christian Century* 1998, l. 309).

**Peripheral *a* takes plural agreement.** Examples:
- "a lucky few were sent far and wide" (*Washington Post* 2015, l. 38)
- "only a lucky few are able" (*The Atlantic* 2016, l. 26)
- "A lucky few were invited there" (*Town & Country* 2006, l. 236)
- "a fortunate few pay $100" (AP 2004, l. 262)

No singular agreement turned up.

**Bearing.**
1. **The bare type is absent.** \**Lucky few escaped* has no instance in edited prose outside a headline. That's the prediction of fusion's adjacency (bare *few* in Det–Head) and of ordinary Head with a condition on external determination. The fusion paper states that condition for compound determinatives only (§6), and §9.2 lets independent *few* take adjectives without it. The concession has to widen to simple determinatives.
2. **Peripheral *a* is 90 of 321 (28%).** An adjective precedes independent *few* with only *a* before it, and agreement is plural. Fusion has to give *a lucky few* a structure in which the adjective doesn't precede a Det–Head *few*, with *a* inside a complex determinative or something similar. Ordinary Head's condition has to count peripheral *a* as licensing the adjective. Neither account gets this for free.
3. ***Some* + ADJ + *few*** ("Some lucky few have a trusted adult", web 2012, l. 89; "the deep and lasting peace that comes to some fortunate few with age", fiction 2007, l. 226): *some* fills Det before an adjectivally modified independent *few*.

## Second query: *some* after a demonstrative or genitive

Brett ran `these/those/my some who` (his description: "only a single hit"). The hit, from a 2011 web comment on pronunciation: "I guess I'm amongst those some who consider it worthy of the obelus". It echoes the dictionary's "considered by some to be questionable" quoted just above, so *some* is metalinguistic here. So no clean instance of independent *some* after a demonstrative or genitive turns up, and the query can't show whether *the some who* is specific to *the*. It is recorded here because it was run alongside the *few* query.

## Third and fourth queries: what the *a* + adjective slot combines with (frequency lists only)

**`another` + quantity word** (COCA frequency list pasted by Brett; exact string not pasted). Totals by form, summing over tag variants:

| Form | Count | Note |
|---|---|---|
| *another few* | 850 | |
| *another little* | 655 | mostly adjective *little* (*another little girl*); tags JJ, JJ_DA1, RR and others |
| *another much* | 43 | tagged RR in most variants, so probably *another much larger* etc. |
| *another some* | 13 | |
| *another many* | 12 | |

*Another* takes *few* freely and *many* hardly at all. That matches *CGEL*'s "[A further few/∗many volunteers] were needed" and its statement that with *another*, *a further*, *a good* "A plural head is permitted only if it is quantified by a numeral or by few" (p. 353, [69]–[70]). Lines weren't inspected, so the *many*/*some* residue is unclassified.

**`a lucky/fortunate/chosen [mc*]`** (frequency list; total 15):

| Form | Count |
|---|---|
| *a lucky seven* | 5 |
| *a lucky one* | 2 |
| *a chosen one* | 2 |
| *a lucky 55*, *200*, *9*, *13*, *11* | 1 each |
| *a fortunate two* | 1 |

*Lucky seven* is probably the idiom, and *one* the pronoun, so at most six or so are *a* + adjective + numeral in the sense of *a lucky few*. Against 90 *a* + ADJ + *few* lines in the KWIC sample alone, the slot belongs to *few*: *a* + adjective is productive with *few* and marginal with cardinals.

**Bearing.**
- The adjective after peripheral *a* is phrasal (*A very lucky few*, CNN 2005, l. 249; *a very fortunate few*, CNN 2005, l. 247; *a, dare I say, lucky few*, NPR 2003, l. 264) and productive with *few*.
- *CGEL* already has adjectives inside *a few* (*a good few*, *a fair few*), but as a short list. A fusion account has to open that slot to phrasal adjectives.
- Ordinary Head has to let peripheral *a*, itself licensed with *few* (and *little*, *many* in *a great many*), license the adjective.
- Either way the statement is specific to *few*.
