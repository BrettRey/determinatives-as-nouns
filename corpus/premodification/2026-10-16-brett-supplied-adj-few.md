# Restrictive adjective + independent *few*: what precedes it (COCA, 16 Oct 2026)

**Coding.**
- *Use*: I = *few* heads the NP (no following noun); D = *few* + noun (*the remaining few months*), set aside.
- *Prec*: what stands before the adjective: THE, A (peripheral *a*), DEM, GEN, PRON (*we*/*us*), SOME, OTHER, BARE, FRAG (fragment), HEADLINE, NAME.

The three with nothing before the adjective are:

- a headline: "Asylum for Lucky Few" (*New York Times* 1991, l. 367)
- a fragment: "some in Hawaii... lucky few..." (blog 2012, l. 172)
- one web line: "other less fortunate few do not accept the change well" (goodreads 2012, l. 103), probably a run-on missing a comma, with *other* as the only element before *less fortunate*

*Remaining* occurs independently only after *the* (8 lines: *The remaining few were the hangers-on*, *Christian Century* 1998, l. 309).

**Peripheral *a* takes plural agreement.** Examples:
- "a lucky few were sent far and wide" (*Washington Post* 2015, l. 38)
- "only a lucky few are able" (*The Atlantic* 2016, l. 26)
- "A lucky few were invited there" (*Town & Country* 2006, l. 236)
- "a fortunate few pay $100" (AP 2004, l. 262)

No singular agreement turned up.

**Bearing.**
1. **The bare type is absent.** \**Lucky few escaped* has no instance in edited prose outside a headline. That's the prediction of fusion's adjacency (bare *few* in Det–Head) and of ordinary Head with a condition on external determination. The fusion paper states that condition for compound determinatives only (§6), and §9.2 lets independent *few* take adjectives without it. The concession has to widen to simple determinatives.
2. **Peripheral *a* is 90 of 321 (28%).** An adjective precedes independent *few* with only *a* before it, and agreement is plural. Fusion has to give *a lucky few* a structure in which the adjective doesn't precede a Det–Head *few*, with *a* inside a complex determinative or something similar. Ordinary Head's condition has to count peripheral *a* as licensing the adjective. Neither account gets this for free.
3. ***Some* + ADJ + *few*** ("Some lucky few have a trusted adult", web 2012, l. 89; "the deep and lasting peace that comes to some fortunate few with age", fiction 2007, l. 226): *some* fills Det before an adjectivally modified independent *few*.

## Second query: *some* after a demonstrative or genitive

Brett ran `these/those/my some who` (his description: "only a single hit"). The hit, from a 2011 web comment on pronunciation: "I guess I'm amongst those some who consider it worthy of the obelus". It echoes the dictionary's "considered by some to be questionable" quoted just above, so *some* is metalinguistic here. That leaves no clean instance of *some* after a demonstrative or genitive in the independent quantity use, consistent with *the some who* not being an article-specific frozen phrase being hard to show either way. Recorded here rather than with the *the some* screens because it was run in this session.
