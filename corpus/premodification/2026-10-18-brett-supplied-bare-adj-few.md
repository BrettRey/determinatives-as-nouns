## *Only select few*

COCA none (Brett). iWeb 21 lines: 13 head the nominal (*only select few have been allowed to share*), 5 precede a noun, 2 follow *one of*, 1 has *a*. NOW 26 lines: 22 bare, 2 with a determiner (*a only select few areas*, *the only select few*), and 2 with the verb *select*. Of the 22 bare lines, 17 are from Indian sources (country codes in NOW), one each from Pakistan, South Africa, Australia, Canada, and the US.

## *A* + adjective + numeral + verb (COCA)

4 lines: *Of these a good hundred appear in more than one novel* (tvtropes.org, 2012, plural agreement with the phrase on its own); *A mere dozen came to its defense: they were hardly listened to* (Theological Studies, 2010, plural resumptive); *a perfect dozen seared into my memory* (participle, no agreement); *a good hundred paces off* (noun following, mistagged).

## Bearing

- The gap holds for edited prose in COCA: the only bare cases are two headlines and two blog posts.
- In web English, and especially in Indian English, *select few* occurs without a determiner, before a noun as often as on its own (*with select few lenses*, *only select few have …*). It looks like a unit in the making, parallel to *a few*, and the other adjectives follow it only occasionally. On the structural paper's DP analysis this is *select* inside the DP without *a*, so "only after *a*" holds for edited standard English, not for these varieties. Both analyses need that qualification, since on either the adjective is inside the DP.
- The numeral lines fit the DP analysis of *a lucky few*: *a good hundred* on its own takes a plural verb, as *a lucky few* does, and CGEL has *a good three hefty steaks* (p. 353).
