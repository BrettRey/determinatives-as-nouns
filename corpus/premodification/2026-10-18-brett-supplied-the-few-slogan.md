**Question.** CGEL says that determinatives such as *few* and *many*, after a determiner, are "hardly admissible unless accompanied by a following modifier" (Huddleston & Pullum 2002: 416), with anaphoric examples (*these few here*, *the many who did*). Does *the few* occur with no following modifier?

## Source

COCA, NEWS: *Denver Post*, 25 January 1999, p. D-01, "The cast of characters for Super Bowl XXXIII has arrived, including Elway, Anderson, Davis, Reeves Shanahan and ... the media", by Terry Frei, Denver Post Sports Writer. Brett Reynolds pasted the expanded context on 18 October 2026. The passage relied on:

> The few. The proud. The Marines. # They're not here. So instead, you get The multitudinous. The shameless. The media.

## Reading

The writer quotes the Marine Corps recruiting slogan, in which *the few* stands alone with the reading 'few people', beside *the proud*, an adjective of CGEL's *rich* type (p. 417). The parody fills the same slots with adjectives of that type (*the multitudinous*, *the shameless*). So the slot takes *the few* and *rich*-type adjectives alike, and in that reading *the few* needs no following modifier. CGEL's condition holds for the anaphoric reading it illustrates. Spock's *the needs of the many outweigh the needs of the few* (quoted in Littmann 2016: 128) is the same reading; it is cited from that source, not from a corpus.
