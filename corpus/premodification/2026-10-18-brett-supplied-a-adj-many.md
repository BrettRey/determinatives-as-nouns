**Source.** COCA, query `a [j*] many`, frequency list pasted by Brett Reynolds on 18 October 2026 (corpus and query confirmed by him): 37 types, 3,237 tokens. Lines for ten candidate types were pasted the same day (below).

**Why it matters.** CGEL treats *a great many* as a complex determinative: "Great in a great many can be replaced by good, but one or other of these adjectives is required" (Huddleston & Pullum 2002: 394). If other adjectives fill the slot, *a* + adjective + *many* is a phrase with an open slot, like *a lucky few* and *a select few* (`2026-10-16-brett-supplied-adj-few.md`, `2026-10-18-brett-supplied-a-select-few.md`). That fits the structural paper's analysis of *a* in *a few* as a modifier of the whole NP.

**Reading so far.** The two adjectives CGEL names account for 3,192 of the 3,237 tokens (99%). The 45 other tokens are spread over 35 types of one to six tokens each. About a dozen are plausible instances of the construction with a size or degree adjective (*vast*, *tremendous*, *countless*, *considerable*, *huge*, *large*, *multitudinous*, *disproportionate*, *growing*, *greater*, *whole*). None of them can be counted until its line is read, since the string can also cross a clause boundary or end in a compound with *many-*. If even a few survive, CGEL's "one or other of these adjectives is required" is too strong, and the slot is open but rare, like the slot in *a lucky few*.

**Result.** All ten are *a* + adjective + *many*, in the same range of uses as *a great many*: before a noun, partitive, and with no noun. Each type has one token in COCA, against 3,192 for *great* and *good* together. So the slot is open but rarely filled by other adjectives, and CGEL's "one or other of these adjectives is required" (p. 394) is too strong. The adjectives denote size or degree (*vast*, *huge*, *large*, *tremendous*, *considerable*, *countless*, *greater*, *whole*, *growing*, *disproportionate*). *A growing many* beside *a shrinking few* shows the two constructions in parallel.

## The same adjective with *few* (COCA, pasted by Brett, 18 October 2026)

Expanded context from COCA, MAG, *Jezebel*, 2016 ("A Hierarchy of Love and Loss and Prince"): "it took a few seconds, only a few seconds, a whole few seconds, to sink in". This is *a* + *whole* + *few* before a noun, the counterpart of *a whole many more people* above. Here *whole* is inside the phrase headed by *few*, as *very* is in *a very few mistakes* (CGEL p. 392), not an adjective modifying the nominal headed by *seconds*. It shows the slot after *a* taking an adjective in the use before a noun, beside *a lucky few* with no noun (`2026-10-16-brett-supplied-adj-few.md`).

**Bearing.** Like *a lucky few*, this tells against a closed complex determinative and for *a* + adjective phrase + quantity word. The structural paper's §4 footnote on the expansions of *a few* cites it.
