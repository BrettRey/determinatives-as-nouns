# Genitive pronoun + *poor* / *rich* / *elderly* heading an NP (COCA, 18 October 2026)
<!-- SUMMARY: two COCA hand searches; genitive determiners with the rich type are routine (their poor, its poor, our elderly), against CGEL p. 417 "The NPs are determined by the definite article"; 500-line display: 19 fused + 9 Lazarus quotations; lines ending at the adjective: 41 fused of 57 (poor 22, elderly 17, rich 2) + 7 Lazarus; demonstratives not tested · status: done · updated: 2026-10-18 -->

**Question.** CGEL says of fused modifier-heads with special interpretations (*the rich*, *the very poor*): "The NPs are determined by the definite article – we couldn't even substitute a demonstrative: ∗these very poor" (Huddleston & Pullum 2002: 417). Do genitive determiners occur? Brett Reynolds judges *the world's poor* and *our elderly* fine (18 October 2026).

## Reading

Genitive determiners are routine with *poor* and *elderly* in this construction, in edited news and academic prose as well as speech and the web, and occur with *rich*. CGEL's "determined by the definite article" is too strong: these NPs take *the* or a genitive. CGEL's further claim, that a demonstrative is excluded (\**these very poor*), isn't tested by these searches. The full KWIC displays were pasted in the session and aren't stored; the lines relied on are listed above.
