**Why it matters.** The sentence-final period excludes *isn't much of a town*, so every line is bare predicative *much* under negation. The subjects are largely entity-denoting rather than amount-denoting: *The Bahamas* (1), *the pay* (7), *It* (many), *Earth* (15), *The Wycoff house* (13), *her resume* (145), *Soup bones* (114), *the summaries* (152), *this house* (179), *The restaurant* (178), *The town* (154), *He* (52). Solt (2015) stars the negated forms with non-amount subjects in her (76) (*\*Vegetation that survived the drought ... wasn't much*, *\*John's patience ... isn't much*), and she says there that she includes the negated version deliberately, "to show that its infelicity is not due entirely to its NPI-like character". Her stated condition is semantic type: predicative *much*/*little* want a subject that denotes an amount (her (77), *The amount of water in the bucket was little/wasn't much*, *Ten gallons is very little/not much*).

So these lines bear on her condition as stated. The caveat is the reading: most of them are evaluative, "not much of a town / of a resume", where the corpus lines' subjects are concrete entities, whereas her starred subjects are abstract or mass (*evidence*, *the benefit*, *John's patience*) and would need a quantity reading. What the set shows is that the type restriction needs that distinction, not that polarity is doing the work.

**Because the query requires a negator, this set says nothing about affirmative predicative *much*.**
