## Reading

Each line was read for polarity and subject. Classification is Claude's, from the KWIC window; Brett hasn't checked it.

- **Negative or non-affirmative: 66.** Negated *be* or modal (*won't be much*, *hasn't been much*), negation higher in the clause (*I don't think it was much*, *it doesn't seem to be much*, *it didn't have to be much*), *never*, and other non-affirmative licensers (*I doubted it was much*, *It's unlikely to be much*, *Not that there is much*). Includes the four *Romeo and Juliet* lines (5, 41, 50, 66).
- **Affirmative, present-day, genuine: 3.** All three follow a negated or minimized alternative:
  - 3, *Girls* (TV, 2016): "It didn't feel like very much while it was happening. But it is much."
  - 68, *Scandinavian Studies* (ACAD, 1997): "Organization is not everything, but it is much." Subject *it* = *organization*, which denotes no amount.
  - 4, *Denver Post* (NEWS, 2015): "Elway thanked everyone for all that was accomplished. And it was much." Subject = *all that was accomplished*, closer to an amount.
- **Affirmative, colloquial "that's much": 3.** 10 (*How I Met Your Mother*, a character speaking stilted English: "I have much gold. Huh, that is much."), 46 (*I Hope They Serve Beer in Hell*: "That 's much."), 51 (*Bratz*: "Yeah, that 's much."). Reply idiom; subject a demonstrative.
- **Affirmative, not present-day or not native: 4.** 19 (Early Modern English, docsouth: "is thought to be much"), 22 (non-native web: "examples of changes in science are much"), 26 (machine-spun spam), 18 (*There 's much*, existential, unclear context).
- **Artefacts: 4.** Sentence-splitting before a comparative: 20 (*much ... shallower*), 29, 30, 35 (*much . much cheaper/closer/worse*).
- **Unclear: 1.** 72 (*It is much ...*, truncated fiction).

What it shows: affirmative bare predicative *much* is rare against the negated cases (3 genuine present-day affirmatives, plus 3 colloquial replies, against 66), and the genuine ones occur in a contrast with a negated or minimized alternative ("not everything, but it is much"). One of them, line 68, has a subject that denotes no amount, in edited academic prose: the frame Solt (2015: (76)) stars. So the distribution looks sensitive to polarity and contrast, which Solt explicitly sets aside, more than to the subject's semantic type.
