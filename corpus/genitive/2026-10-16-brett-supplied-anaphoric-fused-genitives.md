**Labels.** G = genitive on the fused head (head or independent genitive). C = contraction of *is*/*has* (*the other 's a moron*). E = slip for plural *others'* (*the other 's will think*). N = not the construction: the noun *Other* (*the Other 's humanity*) or a name (*Little Sisters of the Poor 's*, *the Rich 's* department store and ship).

**Bearing.**

1. *CGEL* treats *the former*, *the latter* (p. 416, [23ii]) and *the rich*, *the very poor* (p. 417) as fused heads, and says fused heads "either cannot occur in the genitive at all or do so only very rarely" (p. 420). That holds for none of the anaphoric forms. Its case-matching reason (the genitive belongs to the head, not the dependent) applies equally to a fused modifier-head, so it wrongly excludes *the latter's* along with the compounds' *everyone's* (p. 479).
2. On every silent-noun account (Lobeck, Kester, Llombart-Huesca, Günther), *the latter* and *the other* have a silent head, and they take the genitive freely. So "the head genitive needs an overt head" is false, and a silent noun doesn't explain *\*some's*. The fusion paper's §10 concession to silent nouns rests on that explanation.
3. *The poor's* is attested in edited prose (5 ACAD/MAG lines here), against Pullum's (1975: 175–176) use of the possessive as a test for "nouniness" and the paper's *\*the poor's houses*. It's rare next to *the latter's*; *the rich's* appears only on the web.
4. What's left of the gap is the simple determinatives (*\*all's*, *\*some's*, *\*many's*, *\*few's*; *CGEL* p. 420). That hasn't been checked against a corpus. If it holds while *the poor's* and *the latter's* occur, the gap tracks the head's category, which is what ordinary Head says that head is. The proposed next queries, parallel in form to *the poor 's*, are `the few 's`, `the many 's`, `each 's`, `either 's`, `neither 's`.
