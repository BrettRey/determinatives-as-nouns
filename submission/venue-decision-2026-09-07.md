They describe a broad English linguistics journal. The reports provide evidence about this manuscript's reception, not a basis for calling ELL a corpus journal.

Glossa's [current guidelines](https://www.glossa-journal.org/site/author-guidelines/) set an initial ceiling of 13,000 words including references, rising to 15,000 after revision, and an abstract maximum of 250 words. Its [submission policy](https://www.glossa-journal.org/submissions/) allows preprints and requires an anonymous PDF, third-person self-citations, and an account of previous reviews. The [OLH AI policy](https://www.openlibhums.org/site/ai-policy/), linked by Glossa and last revised December 2025, permits assistance with editing but prohibits wholesale generation of the main argument or methodology and generation of most of an article's text. A plan for Codex to produce a complete replacement manuscript creates a policy-fit question even though the thesis is Brett's. Glossa would require a compatible author-led writing process or editorial clarification. JL and ELL instead explicitly require declaration and description of generated text and analytical uses, with responsibility remaining with the author.

## The argument the rebuild must establish

**Reader contract:** this paper will address how evidence warrants primary lexical categories and their subclasses, using English determinatives to test whether a separate primary category or a place within Noun gives the better account, and whether determinatives should be coordinate with pronouns within Noun.

This retains two questions. Evidence that determinatives are nominal does not by itself distinguish the proposal from Hudson's nesting of determiners within pronoun. Likewise, two well-separated clusters in the 2021 matrix do not by themselves exclude nesting: a superclass can contain distinct subclasses. The rebuild must state what is inherited under the nested analysis, what coordination changes, and which observations or economy of description favour one hierarchy. This is the first analytical task, before drafting a new introduction.

The three existing diagnostics—productivity, structural saturation and internal modification—remain candidates for the nounhood/headhood argument. Their relevance to the coordinate-subclass claim must be assessed separately. If the hierarchies make the same empirical predictions, the paper must identify the comparison as architectural economy and specify how that economy is assessed.

## Rebuild sequence after this review

1. **Specify the rival analyses and their consequences.** Correct the Hudson account; read the held sources directly and pursue the missing Sommerstein, Lyons and Hudson passages identified in the handoff. Keep classification, phrase headedness and grammatical function distinct. Make a comparison table with evidence that could favour or count against each proposal.
2. **Audit and extend the evidence.** Reproduce the 2021 matrix analysis, document feature selection, and assess sensitivity to choices that could manufacture the desired clusters. Plan counts with stated denominators for independent uses across lexemes and for internal modification. Code discourse context for the saturation cases. Generic uses alone do not eliminate every silent-noun analysis. Inspect available CGELBank annotation before committing to it as the main empirical source; recorded labels cannot serve as independent proof of their own category scheme.
3. **Write a new exposition around worked constructions.** Begin with the two category questions, the proposed answer and its consequences. Define each diagnostic, walk through an example, and state its inferential limits. Include separate category/function labels and explain *almost every experienced teacher* using the existing example from the review record.
4. **Reduce secondary material.** Keep synchronic English as the scope. Remove prototype terminology and unsupported numerical precision; keep HPC optional. Propose moving the Lean architectural sketch to a companion, and omit the *we linguists* aside unless it becomes necessary to the argument. Preserve the submitted packages and a snapshot of the current working draft.
5. **Test the completed argument on readers.** Before submission, use an outsider and a corpus-methods perspective as well as a specialist critic. Select models and redundancy with Brett before dispatching the review board. Run the registered manuscript and citation checks on the rebuilt text, then produce the venue package after the target is confirmed.

For terminology, take *noun*, *pronoun*, *head*, *modifier* and *lexical category* as familiar to the target reader. Explain *determinative*, category versus function, *Nom*, fusion, saturation, subtype inheritance and the two uses of *DP* at first use. Introduce *subclass* through the actual hierarchy. Run the terminology checker after the new text exists.

## Risks and later submission checks

The largest **desk risk** is an insufficient contribution beyond the existing nominal analyses. The largest **reviewer risk** is using evidence of nounhood to claim evidence against nesting. The largest **empirical risk** is adding counts that describe the examples without distinguishing analyses. Correcting the literature, stating rival consequences, and collecting relevant evidence address these risks; none is recorded as resolved or accepted yet.

For JL, source formats, the length allowance, abstract guidance, reference style, figures and supplements have been checked at consideration level. Word and LaTeX are accepted. Before packaging, recheck the submission queue, anonymity instructions, any participant-research requirements arising from the actual design, and the source bundle.
